A Shadow, a Beatles Song, and an Apple Walk into a Hard Problem: What Matt Segall, Anil Seth, Bernardo Kastrup, and a Neuroscientist Inadvertently Agree On

In short: Three apparently opposed views of consciousness, Whiteheadian panpsychism, idealism and epiphenomenalism, turn out to agree on more than they appear to. David Chalmers's own later work points toward a falsifiable, peer-reviewed, constraint-based alternative that dissolves the Hard Problem he named.

Related evidence and competing explanations: David Chalmers’s “Hard Problem of Consciousness” Has a Hard Problem of the Gaps: How His Own Methodology Dissolves Both · When Matthew Segall's “No Scientific Evidence For Consciousness” Becomes an Unintentional Shield.

Revised July 2026

Three conversations. One with Whiteheadian panpsychist philosopher Matt Segall on YouTube, defending the view that experience is woven into the fabric of reality at the most fundamental level. One with an idealist philosopher on the Essentia Foundation’s YouTube channel, arguing that consciousness is the ontological ground from which physical structure emerges. One debate with an epiphenomenalist neuroscientist in the Facebook comments of my Consciousness Naturalized: A Falsifiable Substrate-Agnostic Consciousness Theory paper, insisting that experience is real but causally inert, a shadow riding alongside mechanism without touching it. They looked like three distinct positions covering the full spectrum: consciousness as cosmic substrate, consciousness as metaphysical foundation, consciousness as redundant byproduct. One inflated experience into a universal primitive. One inflated it into a cosmic ground. The third deflated it entirely.

They were running the same pattern.

Why Panpsychism, Idealism, and Epiphenomenalism All Rely on the Same Explanatory Gap and the Physicalist Methodology They Reject

All three relied heavily on evidence generated by physical-scientific methodology: the research program committed to explaining phenomena, including mental phenomena, in terms of physical structure, dynamics, organization, and causal relations without invoking nonphysical substances (Ladyman and Ross, 2007). All three then declared that this methodology could not reach consciousness. In these exchanges, the metaphysical commitments themselves generated few or no differential empirical predictions, experiments, clinical tools, or discoveries beyond those already produced by the naturalistic research programs they interpreted. Their empirical role remained predominantly post hoc. And all three were, in the precise sense this essay will demonstrate, administering Descartes’s disease homeopathically: locating an explanatory gap between mechanism and experience, then filling it with a primitive rather than first testing whether the gap was generated by the framing.

This essay traces the pattern through the conversations, connects it to a parallel instance in the philosophy of biology, identifies the shared error, and argues that a substantial component of the Hard Problem of consciousness, David Chalmers’s (1995) term for the question of why physical processes are accompanied by subjective experience at all, is produced by grammatical reification, intuitive dualism, and compressed self-modeling rather than by a demonstrated discontinuity in reality. Much of the scaffold for a candidate solution already exists across partially independent, peer-reviewed literatures scattered through fields engineered never to speak to one another. Several empirical components are independently supported. The cross-disciplinary synthesis and phenomenal identity remain to be tested directly. The synthesis requires assembly, formal discrimination from rival theories, and prospective testing.

Where All Roads Lead: Perspectival Organizational Closure as a Testable Identity Hypothesis

The conclusion, stated upfront so you can evaluate everything that follows against it: consciousness is the perspectival organization that thermodynamic organizational closure keeps from dissolving.

More precisely:

Phenomenal consciousness is hypothesized to be a recursively integrated, temporally extended perspectival control regime physically realized through thermodynamic organizational closure.

“Coupling position” denotes the system-relative organization of accessible distinctions and control relations produced by a system’s actively maintained boundary, history, capacities, and dependencies. It is not a tiny spectator hiding inside the mechanism. It is the asymmetry in what can be sensed, retained, valued, predicted, and acted upon from that system’s particular location in the causal network.

“Thermodynamic organizational closure” is the full term; “organizational closure” will serve as shorthand. It describes a regime in which constraints depend upon and contribute to maintaining one another, while matter and energy continue to flow through the system. The system’s identity lies not in retaining the same material parts but in regenerating the organization that makes those parts function as parts of that system.

Organizational closure alone is not proposed as sufficient for consciousness. Cells, tissues, immune systems, and simple autonomous systems can maintain boundaries, regulate viability, and adapt without thereby being established as phenomenally conscious. The proposed discriminator is recursive perspectival availability: distinctions concerning the system’s own state, uncertainty, memory, possible actions, values, and expected consequences must become jointly integrated across time and available for flexible, counterfactual control of what happens next.

In compact form:

C = O ∧ P ∧ R ∧ T ∧ A

where:

O = endogenous organizational closure
P = system-relative self/world/viability distinctions
R = recursive availability of those distinctions
T = temporally extended integration
A = flexible, counterfactual control of action and inference

The Hard Problem is therefore decomposed rather than simply declared solved.

The meta-problem asks why humans judge and report that consciousness presents an unbridgeable explanatory gap.

The boundary problem asks which organized system constitutes the relevant subject.

The phenomenon problem asks whether the recursively integrated causal perspective identified here is identical with phenomenal perspective.

Organizational closure directly addresses the boundary problem. Cognitive science, linguistic analysis, cultural comparison, and self-modeling research increasingly constrain the meta-problem. The remaining identity claim is the scientific wager.

Not additional to physics. The physics, organized into a temporally extended coupling position. The grammar of “experience arising from mechanism” can smuggle in a production gap between two things before either thing has been independently established. Remove that production grammar and one major source of the apparent gap closes. What remains is not nothing. What remains is the empirical question of which physical organizations constitute phenomenal presence.

The zombie argument then changes status. If phenomenal consciousness is constitutively identical to the proposed perspectival organization, a physically identical zombie is incoherent because it holds the constitutive organization fixed while stipulating its absence. But that conclusion follows from the identity hypothesis. It is not independent proof of it. The theory must earn the identity by explaining conscious state, content, and subject boundaries better than rival accounts and by surviving tests that could show the organization present without consciousness or consciousness present without the organization.

The strongest version of the thermodynamic argument is equally precise. Landauer’s principle does not say that every distinction continuously consumes kBT ln 2 merely by existing. A physical distinction can persist passively in stable or metastable structure. The cost enters when a finite, noisy system must actively preserve, update, correct, overwrite, or reset the distinction. When maintenance maps multiple possible error states onto a smaller set of corrected states, discards obsolete estimates, or resets finite machinery for reuse, the operation is logically irreversible and exports entropy somewhere in the implementation. Landauer supplies an ideal lower bound under specific conditions. Nonequilibrium information thermodynamics supplies the broader accounting.

A living self/non-self boundary is not a passive bit resting undisturbed behind an energy barrier. It is continually challenged by molecular turnover, diffusion, damage, infection, sensor noise, metabolic fluctuation, and environmental change. Maintaining it requires recurrent sensing, comparison, correction, rewriting, and reset. The resulting causal perspective is therefore not thermodynamically free.

This does not by itself refute epiphenomenalism. An epiphenomenalist can assign all thermodynamic work to the physical organization and claim that phenomenality merely accompanies it. The stronger challenge is the self-report dilemma: if experience never affects judgment, memory, attention, or report, then reports about experience are not caused by the experience they purport to describe. A common physical cause may produce both experience and report, but the theory must then explain why the semantic content of the report should reliably track a causally isolated phenomenal state.

Landauer and nonequilibrium thermodynamics establish that the physical organization supporting perspective is not free. The self-report argument challenges the claim that phenomenality is detached from that organization. Together they support an identity theory more strongly than either argument can alone.

Clinically, Owen et al.’s (2006) detection of covert command-following, Casali et al.’s (2013) Perturbational Complexity Index, and the misdiagnosis rates reported by Andrews et al. (1996) and Schnakers et al. (2009) show that behavioral unresponsiveness cannot safely be equated with an absence of conscious processing. They do not prove organizational closure, nor do they show that epiphenomenalism caused the misdiagnoses. They establish something more immediate and less theatrical: consciousness must be measured with methods capable of detecting organized processing when ordinary behavior fails.

Terrence Deacon’s teleodynamics (2012) shows how absence and constraint can perform causal and explanatory work without becoming extra substances. This opens the central possibility developed here: consciousness need not be an ingredient added to mechanism. It may be the system-relative topology of distinctions maintained and recursively integrated by the mechanism.

The Convergence in Brief: Twelve Fields, One Constraint Topology

Before the case studies, the evidential foundation. Twelve fields, working across different scales, methods, centuries, and intellectual traditions, do not independently prove that consciousness is organizational closure. They converge on something more basic and more defensible: a common constraint topology.

Across thermodynamics, organizational biology, autopoiesis, active inference, relational quantum mechanics, ontic structural realism, process philosophy, enactivism, cybernetics, category theory, biosemiotics, and relational epistemologies, stable identity is repeatedly described not as an intrinsic substance but as a pattern maintained through relations. Boundaries are enacted rather than simply given. Perspective is constituted by asymmetric coupling rather than added to an otherwise perspective-free mechanism. Persistence depends on recursively regenerated organization under finite energetic conditions.

The convergence is therefore eliminative and architectural rather than twelvefold experimental confirmation. These fields repeatedly weaken the same explanatory options: free-floating substances, observer-independent identity, costless information, passive boundaries, and perspectives detached from the processes that constitute them. They also supply complementary components of a candidate naturalization of consciousness.

The empirical fields constrain what physically realized perspective must require. The formal fields describe how recursive organization and control can be represented. The philosophical and relational traditions expose assumptions generated by substance ontology and views from nowhere. Their evidential weights differ, and several are historically or conceptually related rather than fully independent.

Its force lies not in twelve votes for one answer, but in twelve routes repeatedly closing the same explanatory exits.

I. The Pattern

The surface contrast between panpsychism, idealism, and epiphenomenalism is dramatic enough that entire philosophy departments organize around it. Many panpsychists say experience is a fundamental feature of reality, present in some form at the most basic scales and organized into richer subjects, a tradition running from Whitehead’s process philosophy through contemporary defenders such as Goff. Idealists say consciousness is the ontological ground from which physical structure emerges or within which it appears, a family of views ranging from Berkeley to contemporary analytic idealism. Epiphenomenalists say consciousness is real but causally inert, that physical mechanisms do all the work and experience accompanies them without contributing, a position associated with Huxley and given a rigorous modern formulation by Jackson.

These categories contain substantial internal differences. Analytic idealism is not Advaita. Whiteheadian panexperientialism is not constitutive micropsychism. Neutral monism is not dual-aspect monism. Madhyamaka is not an early draft of structural realism waiting for Western notation. The comparison concerns a recurring explanatory operation, not the claim that every position is secretly the same doctrine.

Examine what each position does in the exchanges considered here, and the structural similarity becomes visible.

Each accepts most or all of the explanatory work performed by physical-scientific methodology and then locates the decisive phenomenal fact where that methodology cannot directly adjudicate it. The panpsychist says the tools describe organization but miss the experiential primitive grounding it. The idealist says the tools describe appearances within consciousness but cannot reach consciousness as the fundamental ground. The epiphenomenalist says the tools describe every causal mechanism while experience remains a real but causally idle accompaniment. Same structural gap. Different metaphysical placement.

The generative asymmetry is the diagnostic. The proper question is not whether a metaphysical view is compatible with existing findings. Nearly any sufficiently flexible ontology can achieve compatibility after the findings arrive. The stronger question is:

What additional observation, intervention, excluded outcome, or explanatory compression follows from adding the metaphysical hypothesis to the empirical base model?

Let B represent the empirically grounded model and H the metaphysical addition. Then:

ΔG(H) = G(B + H) − G(B)

A metaphysical framework may perform conceptual, ethical, existential, or interpretive work even when its incremental empirical yield is zero. Falsifiability is not the sole criterion of meaning. Mathematics, phenomenology, logic, and metaphysics possess different error-correction procedures. But a framework without empirical loss conditions does not by itself discriminate among observable possibilities, and it should not be presented as having empirically outperformed a model whose findings it merely redescribes.

In the exchanges examined here, the panpsychist cites IIT, active inference, Bell experiments, and neural-complexity measures. The idealist cites predictive processing, psychedelic neuroimaging, active inference, and quantum foundations. The epiphenomenalist cites neural integration, behavior, cognitive control, and clinical diagnosis. These are outputs of naturalistic research programs. Removing the metaphysical overlay leaves most of the evidence and interventions intact. The metaphysical commitment therefore owes an account of what further work it contributes.

This is the precise sense in which the dependency can become parasitic: not because philosophy is useless, but because an empirically frictionless overlay can consume the authority of a research program while adding no differential risk of its own.

The negation isomorphism makes the structure clearer. Panpsychism, idealism, eliminative materialism, and epiphenomenalism often continue arguing within a substance framework: mental stuff, experiential stuff, physical stuff, or a phenomenal property excluded from causal commerce. The organizational closure account rejects that starting point. It treats entities as relatively stable organizations of constraints and relations across scales.

Greedy reductionism fails for the same reason metaphysical inflation fails. The higher-level organization is real, causally explanatory, and physically implemented. It is not “nothing but” its lowest-level parts because the relevant explanatory object is the organization those parts instantiate. But it is not an additional substance floating above those parts either.

The Hard Problem is the debate’s hinge. The panpsychist uses the gap to motivate an experiential primitive. The idealist uses it to motivate consciousness as ground. The epiphenomenalist treats it as a permanent separation between physical explanation and phenomenal fact.

The revised organizational account does not simply deny the phenomenon. It questions whether “experience arising from mechanism” is the right grammatical and causal model. The phrase presupposes two independently identified relata and then demands a bridge between them. The alternative is a constitutive identity:

Phenomenal perspective is hypothesized to be the recursively integrated coupling position of an organizationally closed system.

That hypothesis may fail. But it does not begin by conceding the gap it is meant to investigate.

One final diagnostic before the case studies. Ask each framework what would change its mind. Some metaphysical positions can provide internal coherence conditions, but many cannot specify observations that would count against their central ontology. That does not make them meaningless. It makes them empirically underdetermined.

Bateson gives the relevant scientific criterion: a difference that makes a difference. A metaphysical addition compatible with every possible neural, behavioral, evolutionary, and thermodynamic outcome may remain an interpretation of the evidence. It is not yet an empirical explanation of why one outcome occurs rather than another.

Compatibility is free. Production is expensive.

II. The Problem-Identifier’s Dilemma: What Chalmers’s Own Later Work Does to the Hard Problem

Before the case studies, the problem-identifier deserves direct engagement because the most powerful pressure on the Hard Problem’s irreducibility emerged from Chalmers’s own later research program.

After the exchange with the epiphenomenalist neuroscientist, he sent a follow-up containing what I consider a sophisticated defense of Chalmers: the philosopher’s primary contribution was identifying a problem. The proposed metaphysical solution was secondary. Philosophy identifies where explanation may fail; science and engineering attempt to solve what can be solved.

This is a generous and partially correct reading of Chalmers. It also changes what must be defended.

If Chalmers’s naturalistic dualism and psychophysical laws are treated as secondary, the central diagnostic remains: even after explaining all cognitive and behavioral functions, why is their performance accompanied by experience?

Fine. Evaluate the diagnostic directly.

In 2018, Chalmers introduced the meta-problem of consciousness: the problem of explaining why humans judge, report, and believe that consciousness presents a special explanatory problem. By his own taxonomy, the meta-problem is functionally tractable. It concerns judgments, reports, dispositions, and cognitive mechanisms. Chalmers suggested that solving it might illuminate the original problem.

That does not concede that the Hard Problem is unreal. It does mean the force of the Hard Problem can no longer rest solely on the felt obviousness of the explanatory gap. If the intuition of a gap is produced by discoverable cognitive mechanisms, those mechanisms become part of the evidence rather than irrelevant noise surrounding an otherwise transparent metaphysical perception.

The organizational closure account contributes to this program. “Experiencing” is a process term that grammar nominalizes into “experience.” A compressed self-model represents selected consequences of distributed processing while omitting most of the machinery constructing the representation. The resulting state appears simple, intrinsic, directly apprehended, and detached from mechanism because the system has no practical reason to represent every causal detail of its own construction.

The meta-problem therefore has a plausible mechanistic pathway:

distributed processing → compressed self-model → omitted construction → intrinsic-seeming awareness → nominalized phenomenal object → apparent explanatory remainder

Berent’s work on intuitive dualism and essentialism supports the claim that ordinary judgments about mind and body are shaped by cognitive biases. It does not settle ontology. It weakens the inference from intuition to ontology.

The second pressure comes from Reality+. Chalmers argues that appropriately organized virtual systems and inhabitants can be genuine and conscious. This does not formally contradict his zombie argument. Chalmers can hold that organization gives rise to consciousness in the actual world because contingent psychophysical laws connect them, while a zombie world instantiates the same organization under different psychophysical laws.

The stronger criticism is not contradiction. It is incremental explanatory yield.

If the same relevant organization supports consciousness across biological and virtual substrates, organization appears to do the empirically productive work of identifying which systems are conscious, how their contents differ, how their reports arise, and how perturbations alter their states. The added psychophysical laws must then show what they predict, exclude, or explain beyond the organization itself.

They may explain the bare fact that phenomenality accompanies organization. But that claim cannot be established merely by repeating that organization leaves the fact unexplained. That would place the conclusion inside the definition of the problem.

Chalmers’s later work therefore reframes the Hard Problem without formally refuting his earlier position. It opens three tractable lines of inquiry:

  1. Why do systems like us generate Hard Problem intuitions?
  2. Which organizational invariants support consciousness across substrates?
  3. What incremental work, if any, remains for additional psychophysical laws once those invariants are specified?

A conceptual problem can reveal an ambiguity or incompatibility in our framework. A scientific problem must eventually generate discriminating observations.

The historical analogy with vitalism remains useful if kept within bounds. Before modern biology, an apparent gap between chemistry and life motivated appeals to a special vital force. Wöhler’s synthesis did not single-handedly refute every form of vitalism, and the decline of vitalism involved far more than one experiment. But the history illustrates a pattern: an apparent substance gap can become an organizational research problem once mechanisms formerly treated as categorically separate are integrated into one causal account.

The proposal here is analogous. Replace:

“What experiential ingredient must be added to mechanism?”

with:

“What organization constitutes a causal perspective, when does that perspective become recursively available to itself, and does that organization exhaust phenomenal presence?”

The first question presupposes an extra ingredient. The second generates measurements, interventions, and loss conditions.

Chalmers’s diagnostic contribution remains intact. His later work supplies tools that make the diagnostic less metaphysically self-sealing and more scientifically productive. The Hard Problem is not defeated by a contradiction in Chalmers. It is decomposed by taking his later questions seriously.

The Bounded-Zombie Problem

Grant Chalmers the distinction between actual-world organizational invariance and metaphysically possible zombies. A further problem remains.

A physically and functionally identical zombie must share our finite memory, limited processing capacity, partial observability, and inability to calculate every future state of a complex environment. It must therefore perform the same action-relative compression we do. It must partition the world into distinctions that matter for prediction and control, discard distinctions that do not, preserve a temporally extended self-model, represent uncertainty, and revise its abstractions when they fail.

The zombie therefore possesses the same causal interiority: the same system-relative world, the same coupling position, and the same recursively available model of self, world, and possible action. It also generates the same judgments and reports about phenomenal interiority. Chalmers acknowledged this consequence directly in his discussion of Zombie Dave, whose self-ascriptions are produced by the same functional mechanisms despite the stipulated functional mechanisms despite absence of qualia.

This does not make the zombie logically incoherent. It makes the explanatory remainder unusually narrow.

If phenomenal experience contributes to compression, valuation, memory, attention, deliberation, or report, then its removal changes the system’s functional organization and the zombie is no longer an isomorph.

If experience contributes to none of those processes, then organization alone constructs everything operationally meant by a bounded interior. The phenomenal facts may remain metaphysically additional, but they add no differential causal or empirical explanation.

Chalmers can preserve both claims through contingent psychophysical laws: the organization does the causal work, while the laws guarantee that phenomenality accompanies it in our world. The remaining question is therefore not whether this position is coherent. It is what explanatory work the additional laws perform once the organization has already generated the entire navigable perspective and every judgment made from within it.

III. The Philosopher: Hans and Seth on the Essentia Foundation

The Essentia Foundation runs an interview series featuring consciousness researchers in conversation with Bernardo Kastrup’s analytic idealism program. One episode paired Anil Seth, one of the most empirically productive consciousness researchers working today, with an idealist philosopher named Hans whose commitments structured much of the exchange.

The episode is worth studying because it demonstrates the explanatory-dependency pattern in real time.

What Seth Actually Argues

Seth’s research program is built around the idea of perception as controlled hallucination: conscious perception is constrained prediction rather than passive registration of sensory input. The brain does not simply receive the world. It continuously predicts the causes of sensory signals and updates those predictions when they fail.

Seth’s “beast machine” framework grounds this process in biological self-regulation. Brains predict the external world and the body’s internal condition because living systems must maintain themselves within viable bounds. Interoception, including signals associated with heartbeat, respiration, hunger, temperature, and internal chemistry, contributes to the experience of being an embodied self.

Translated into organizational language, the brain maintains probabilistic relations among body, world, memory, uncertainty, and possible action. Those relations are continuously regenerated against perturbation. The boundary between self-model and world-model is not a static line but a maintained control relation.

Seth’s biological naturalism and organizational closure overlap substantially on embodied regulation. They are not identical theories. Seth emphasizes the possibility that consciousness depends on specific properties of living biological computation. The present framework proposes a substrate-neutral organizational criterion and therefore accepts more risk: a nonbiological system could qualify if it developed equivalent self-maintaining, recursively perspectival organization.

This creates a real future disagreement rather than an apparent consensus:

Biological naturalism: familiar living processes may be necessary for consciousness.

Perspectival organizational closure: familiar biology is one realization of the relevant organization, not necessarily the only one.

The disagreement can eventually be tested in synthetic and artificial systems rather than settled by metaphysical preference.

PCI is clinically relevant here. Casali and colleagues developed the Perturbational Complexity Index to quantify the complexity of cortical responses to perturbation. Casarotto and colleagues later reported 100 percent sensitivity and specificity between conscious and unconscious conditions in a benchmark population and 94.7 percent sensitivity for minimally conscious patients. The authors also cautioned that the selected threshold should not be interpreted as an absolute metaphysical boundary between consciousness and unconsciousness.

PCI measures perturbation-evoked complexity. It does not directly measure organizational closure, autopoiesis, thermodynamic distance from equilibrium, or the self/non-self boundary. The hypothesis advanced here is that PCI may indirectly track part of the integrity of a broader perspectival regime. That interpretation must add predictive value beyond PCI before it deserves to be treated as more than translation into my preferred vocabulary.

Seth’s work is internally coherent, externally coupled to evidence, and exposed to failure. His predictions can be challenged. The relevant methodological virtue is not that his framework has already solved consciousness. It is that the framework produces tractable comparisons rather than relying on compatibility after the fact.

What Hans Is Actually Doing

Hans runs a four-step pattern across the conversation.

Step one: establish agreement on the limits of naive materialism. This is uncontroversial. Seth agrees that the cartoon version of physicalism, where consciousness is “just atoms bouncing around,” is inadequate. Organizational closure agrees as well. Rejecting greedy reductionism does not imply idealism.

Step two: move from “naive materialism is insufficient” to “naturalistic explanation is insufficient.” This is the critical slide. The failure of a seventeenth-century substance model does not establish the failure of relational, process-based, multiscale physicalism. The missing option is that neither cartoon materialism nor idealism is correct, because both inherit a Cartesian picture of inert matter and then disagree about what must be added to it.

Step three: import idealist conclusions through compatibility. Hans repeatedly notes that idealism is compatible with the data Seth cites. Seth gives the right reply: compatibility is easy once the thing with which one is compatible already exists.

Last-Thursdayism, or Seth’s preferred Next Tuesdayism, is compatible with every present observation. Compatibility alone therefore cannot establish explanatory superiority.

Apply the same demand symmetrically. Idealism says consciousness is fundamental and individual experience is a bounded appearance or dissociation within a larger consciousness. But what specifies the boundary? Why does one perspective possess these memories, sensitivities, and action possibilities rather than another? What mechanism determines the qualitative differentiation between bat, octopus, infant, dreaming human, and artificial system?

Analytic idealism can offer conceptual answers. It may describe dissociation, representation, or appearance within mind-at-large. The empirical question is whether this addition predicts anything beyond the organizational structures already required to explain those boundaries and differences.

The combination and subject-individuation problems remain serious for panpsychism and related views. Kadić describes the subject-combination problem as a possibly insurmountable challenge for standard forms before proposing a monadic alternative. This does not refute every panpsychism. It demonstrates that “experience is fundamental” does not by itself explain why there are bounded subjects.

Step four: appeal to quantum mechanics as metaphysical license. Bell-inequality violations, the measurement problem, and observer-relative interpretations are sometimes invoked as though they establish consciousness as ontologically fundamental.

They do not.

Bell experiments constrain local hidden-variable theories. Relational quantum mechanics treats physical facts as interaction-relative. QBism treats quantum states as agent-relative expectations. Decoherence explains the emergence of effectively classical behavior from quantum dynamics. These positions differ substantially, and none entails analytic idealism merely by rejecting a classical view from nowhere.

Quantum theory may eventually constrain theories of consciousness. It currently supplies no automatic bridge from contextual measurement to consciousness as the ground of reality.

The COGITATE adversarial collaboration adds an accountability contrast but not a verdict. It challenged important predictions associated with IIT and GNWT. The later controversy over IIT’s scientific status, along with responses defending or qualifying the theory, shows the field actively disputing what counts as a testable consciousness theory. The lesson is methodological: proponents should state predictions and interpretations before seeing results.

It would be inaccurate to treat COGITATE as confirming organizational closure. It confirms the value of adversarial testing and leaves the field theoretically underdetermined.

The Empirical Dependency

Hans’s position requires naive material substance to reject, though structural and process-oriented physicalisms already reject it. It requires an observer/observed wall for quantum mechanics to demolish, though several quantum interpretations never posit that wall in the required form. It requires computational functionalism to represent naturalism, though Seth’s biological naturalism explicitly does not. It requires the Hard Problem to remain one indivisible question, though Chalmers’s meta-problem and contemporary cognitive science permit decomposition.

Remove any single dependency and the argument weakens. Remove them all and the idealist position can still survive as a metaphysical interpretation, but its incremental empirical contribution becomes harder to identify.

There is one dependency deeper than the others. Idealism requires the “inside” of experience to remain unexplained by naturalistic organization. The organizational framework does not claim that any boundary automatically generates phenomenality. It claims that a causal perspective is physically constituted by asymmetric coupling and that phenomenal perspective may be identical with a specific recursively integrated form of that organization.

The identity is not smuggled in as proof. It is exposed as the bridge hypothesis.

The Collapse Without Its Foil

Remove cartoon physicalism from the conversation. Grant that a sophisticated naturalistic framework can treat higher-level relational organization as real, causally efficacious, and irreducible to a mere list of lower-level parts. Grant that system-relative perspective can be physically constituted through actively maintained distinctions. Grant that the resulting theory is not ordinary input-output functionalism because internal organization matters.

What does idealism add?

It may relabel the relations as mental rather than physical. It may offer an overarching interpretation of why there is anything rather than nothing or why phenomenality exists at all. Those may be genuine philosophical functions. But if the labels change no observation, intervention, or exclusion, they do not yet improve the empirical theory.

The standard panpsychist criticism of naive physicalism remains important: experience cannot be derived from a substrate defined in advance as intrinsically devoid of every property relevant to experience. The organizational response is not to add experience to the smallest units. It is to reject the inert-substance model and ask which dynamically maintained relations constitute perspective at higher scales.

Cartesian dualism may be the disease. Panpsychism can still administer a diluted form of the same disease if it preserves the original gap and adds experience as a compensating primitive. The dose differs. The pathology is the reified gap.

Clinical evidence does not directly refute filter or conditioning theories. PCI does not show that consciousness declines according to a uniquely production-like threshold while filter accounts require a smooth gradient. Both families can accommodate nonlinear transitions. The stronger observation is that conscious state covaries with specific patterns of organized neural dynamics, and any conditioning theory must explain why those patterns track state and content with such precision.

Ask the generative-asymmetry question directly:

What additional observation, intervention, or excluded outcome follows from idealism rather than from the naturalistic organization it interprets?

The answer may eventually be substantial. In the exchanges examined here, it remained unclear.

IV. The Neuroscientist and the Epiphenomenalist Trap

The second case study comes from an extended exchange in the comments of my paper Consciousness Naturalized: A Falsifiable Substrate-Agnostic Consciousness Theory. Ivo, a neuroscientist posting under a pseudonym whom I have debated over several years, raised objections more serious than most. The conversation eventually moved both of us toward a more precise problem than either began with.

Ivo’s initial position was that experience is “real but redundant.” The brain is “simply a cascade of if-then mechanisms.” He does not need to assume experience to model neural dynamics. Whatever consciousness is, it performs no additional causal work.

This is the epiphenomenalist mirror of idealism. Idealism places consciousness beyond physical explanation as ontological ground. Epiphenomenalism places it beyond physical explanation as causally inert residue.

But Ivo’s “cascade of if-then mechanisms” silently requires more organization than the phrase admits. A nervous system is not merely a sequence of externally specified rules. It is embedded in a living system that repairs, regulates, predicts, and modifies the conditions of its own operation. The relevant contrast is not mechanism versus mystery. It is passive dynamics versus self-maintaining organization.

Naming that organization does not yet prove consciousness. It identifies the causal system whose relation to consciousness must be tested.

The exchange ran across multiple rounds. Its value lies in how the analogies progressively clarified the framework.

The Shadow That Turned Out to Be Causal

Ivo offered the shadow as his model case for something real, natural, and causally irrelevant. Your shadow exists because of you, reflects changes in your body, yet is not an independent material object.

A shadow is a spatial distribution of reduced photon flux produced by occlusion. There is no additional shadow substance. The structured absence is physically describable and can alter biological behavior through ordinary causal chains. Plants redirect growth in response to light and shade. Visual systems use shadows for depth, motion, and object interpretation. Predators and prey respond to changing illumination.

The instructive point is not that every shadow is an agent or every absence independently pushes matter around. It is that constraint and structured absence can be physically consequential without becoming extra substances.

Ivo had nominalized “shadowing,” the process of differential light occlusion, into “a shadow,” looked for the substance of the noun, and found none independent of the process. That is exactly the kind of grammatical move that can manufacture an apparent ontological remainder.

The analogy therefore supports a limited lesson:

Failure to locate a separate substance corresponding to a process noun does not show that the process is nonphysical or causally inert.

It does not prove that consciousness is organizational closure. It shows why the demand to find “experience” as an additional component may already contain a category error.

The Song That Demonstrated Substrate-Agnostic Organization

Ivo’s second analogy was more sophisticated. The Beatles’ “She Loves You” can be instantiated through vinyl, live performance, magnetic storage, digital encoding, notation, memory, and neural activity. The song is not identical with any one of these substrates.

Correct.

That is compatible with a substrate-agnostic organizational account.

The song is an invariant relational structure preserved across materially different realizations. It does not float above physics. Each realization requires a medium capable of preserving and transforming the relevant constraints.

Substrate agnosticism is therefore not substrate indifference.

A digital file, vinyl groove, performance, and memory have different energetic costs, failure modes, temporal properties, and transformational possibilities. The same higher-level pattern can be realized differently, but it cannot be realized by nothing.

The analogy goes deeper. “The song” can itself become a misleading frozen noun. In practice, songs persist through performances, recordings, memories, reinterpretations, covers, social transmission, and cultural selection. The song changes listeners. Listeners change the song. The identity is a historically extended pattern of constrained transformation.

That is bidirectional coupling.

Songlines provide a more culturally and ecologically embedded example, but they must be described with care. In many Aboriginal traditions, song, Country, route, law, memory, season, and practice are relationally constituted rather than separable archives pointing at one another. This does not mean songlines are literally identical to Markov blankets or organizational closure. It means they offer a developed relational ontology in which knowledge is enacted through place, performance, and responsibility rather than treated as a substance stored in isolation.

Nunn and Reid argue that some Aboriginal oral traditions plausibly preserve memories of coastal inundation extending more than seven thousand years. Reser and colleagues found that an Aboriginal-inspired memorization technique increased the odds of perfect recall relative to the method of loci in a controlled educational setting, although mean recall improvements were similar. These findings support the efficacy and durability of relational memory practices. They do not demonstrate that one civilization conducted a sixty-five-thousand-year experiment against the Hard Problem.

Their relevance is generative: they motivate comparative tests of how cultural and linguistic models of self, substance, and relation shape consciousness intuitions.

The Apple That Corrected the Slogan

Ivo’s third analogy supplied the missing apple.

Money may be paid to grow, transport, purchase, or eat an apple. The apple is not money. By analogy, a conscious system may pay thermodynamic costs without consciousness being identical to the cost.

The criticism is correct against a literal reading of “consciousness is what organizational closure costs.”

But economic payment and thermodynamic implementation are different relations.

Money is transactionally associated with the apple. A world can contain apples without money.

Energy flow is constitutive of a living apple’s development, metabolism, repair, and continued organization. A world cannot contain living apples performing those processes at zero physical cost.

Even here, the noun “apple” spans different organizations. A living fruit on a tree, a harvested fruit, a decaying material structure, a commodity, and a concept can all be called an apple. Active biological organization can cease while enough material pattern remains for ordinary classification.

The corrected conclusion is therefore precise:

The energetic cost is not the phenomenal organization. It is part of the physical process that keeps the organization available.

Consciousness is not the heat organizational closure dissipates. It is the perspectival organization that dissipative maintenance keeps from dissolving.

The Thermodynamic Cost of Keeping a Perspective Physically Available

Landauer’s principle concerns logically irreversible transformations, especially operations such as erasure that map multiple possible logical states onto fewer outputs. Under standard ideal conditions, erasing one bit imposes a lower bound of kBT ln 2 on heat dissipated to the environment.

The principle does not say that a distinction consumes this amount continuously merely by existing. Memories can persist passively in stable or metastable physical states. Nor is kBT ln 2 a universal tariff covering every biological distinction regardless of architecture, speed, accuracy, or nonequilibrium preparation.

The stronger claim is this:

A finite, noisy physical system that actively preserves reliable distinctions through updating, refreshing, error correction, overwriting, or reset must consume physical resources and produce entropy somewhere whenever those maintenance operations are irreversible.

Biological sensing and regulation strengthen the point beyond elementary bit erasure. Lan et al. demonstrated energy-speed-accuracy trade-offs in sensory adaptation. Govern and ten Wolde showed that energy dissipation is essential for biochemical sensing systems to escape equilibrium accuracy limits. Sartori and Pigolotti connected biochemical error correction with chemical driving and dissipated work. Chiribella and colleagues derived nonequilibrium costs for accurate information processing.

The cost attaches to physical information management, not to the semantic importance of the distinction.

A self/non-self boundary remains usable only because correlations are regenerated against noise and turnover. Obsolete states are overwritten. Errors are corrected. Components are reset. Signals are renewed. The coupling position is enacted rather than merely described.

This establishes:

A physically maintained causal perspective is not thermodynamically free.

It does not establish:

Every thermodynamically maintained perspective is phenomenally conscious.

The second proposition requires the additional RPOC criteria and the differential tests stated later.

The Self-Report Dilemma

Ivo conceded that the self-report argument was particularly strong and difficult to evade. He later rejected strict causal epiphenomenalism, although he did not thereby endorse organizational identity theory.

The argument is simple.

If phenomenal experience is causally inert, then the report “I am conscious” is not caused by consciousness. It is caused entirely by physical mechanisms that would, by hypothesis, produce the same report in the absence of consciousness.

The epiphenomenalist can invoke a common cause:

M → C

M → R

where M is a physical state, C is consciousness, and R is the report.

This preserves correlation without phenomenal causation. But it creates an epistemic problem: why should the content of R reliably describe C when C never influences judgment, memory, attention, or report?

The problem is not merely that the theory feels strange. The theory threatens the evidential route by which its own central claim is known.

On the RPOC identity hypothesis, report and phenomenal presence arise from the same recursively available perspectival organization. Reports are not infallible. They are causally connected to the organization they attempt to describe.

This does not prove the identity. It gives strict epiphenomenalism a burden that ordinary common-cause correlation does not automatically discharge.

The Clinical Evidence, Correctly Used

Owen and colleagues detected instruction-dependent imagery responses in a behaviorally unresponsive patient. Andrews and colleagues reported that 17 of 40 patients referred as vegetative had been misdiagnosed. Schnakers and colleagues found that 18 of 44 patients diagnosed with vegetative state were classified as minimally conscious using standardized assessment.

These findings show that behavioral assessment can miss preserved awareness or cognition. The causes include motor impairment, fluctuating arousal, visual limitations, insensitive bedside methods, and failure to detect subtle purposeful behavior.

They do not show that clinicians adopted epiphenomenalism or treated patients as mechanisms without interiority. The philosophy did not produce the percentages.

The defensible conclusion remains morally severe:

Behavioral silence is not reliable evidence of an empty interior, and inadequate measurement can alter treatment and end-of-life decisions.

PCI likewise measures the complexity of perturbation-evoked cortical dynamics. It does not directly measure phenomenality, thermodynamic organizational closure, or the causal efficacy of qualia. The RPOC framework must show that its own measures add predictive information beyond PCI rather than taking credit for PCI after the fact.

The congenital-insensitivity-to-pain argument should not be used as a clean subtraction of phenomenal pain from otherwise intact nociceptive circuitry. Known congenital painlessness conditions alter nociceptors or nociceptive development. They demonstrate the protective importance of nociceptive-pain systems, not the independent causal contribution of phenomenality while mechanism is held constant.

The Circularity Charge

Ivo compared the identity claim to defining free will as cognitive control and then discovering cognitive control. The objection is serious if RPOC is defined through whatever properties happen to accompany consciousness.

The response cannot be merely that the framework has many definitions and equations. A circular framework can own excellent stationery.

The relevant variables must be operationalized independently of consciousness reports:

  • endogenous recovery after perturbation;
  • mutual maintenance among constraints;
  • integration of self-state, world-state, uncertainty, memory, action, and value;
  • temporal depth;
  • counterfactual control;
  • scale-specific autonomy;
  • incremental prediction beyond existing consciousness measures.

The identity is falsified or weakened if those properties are present without reliable consciousness indicators, absent despite reliable consciousness, or predictively redundant once rival variables are controlled.

That is the distinction between defining consciousness into the framework and exposing an identity hypothesis to the world.

V. The Same Pattern, Three Times

Hans inflates consciousness into an ontological ground. Ivo initially deflates it into a causally redundant byproduct. Segall distributes experience through reality as a primitive feature of process. These positions are not equivalent, but each uses a gap between relational-process description and phenomenal presence to motivate something that the naturalistic description allegedly cannot contain.

The shared logical risk resembles an argument from explanatory remainder:

Current explanation is incomplete, therefore a special primitive is required.

Incomplete explanation does not establish the nature of what completes it. The failure of one model constrains the possibility landscape. It does not automatically license idealism, panpsychism, epiphenomenalism, Platonism, or any other preferred resident of the gap.

The organizational alternative seeks to force revision rather than fill the space with a protected primitive. It asks which systems maintain their own constitutive constraints, how those systems partition causal possibility, when those partitions become recursively available, and what observations would show that this organization is not consciousness.

That is not a proof by elimination. It is a research program.

The Dennett Question

Ivo asked whether the framework conflates identity theory with Dennett’s functionalism.

It should not.

Dennett’s work is useful for diagnosing the inflation of private phenomenal properties and for challenging the intuitive force of zombies. But the present theory makes a positive identity claim Dennett would not necessarily endorse.

It is not classical functionalism. Functionalism identifies mental states by causal or computational role and can permit different internal realizations so long as the relevant functional relations remain. RPOC makes the internal causal organization constitutive. Two systems can produce similar outputs while differing in consciousness if one instantiates endogenous perspectival closure and the other is externally driven or merely replays the same signals.

It is not classical brain-state identity theory. Place and Smart identified mental states with neural states or processes in a particular biological substrate. RPOC identifies consciousness with an organizational regime that may, in principle, be realized in different physical substrates.

It is not ordinary computationalism. Abstract algorithmic equivalence is insufficient because the implementation must instantiate the relevant counterfactual, thermodynamic, and self-maintaining organization.

It is not eliminativism. The theory does not say phenomenal consciousness is nothing. It proposes what sort of physically realized process phenomenal consciousness may be.

Dennett helps dissolve a confused framing. Organizational closure supplies a theory of physical identity. Enactivism supplies structural coupling and sense-making. Active inference supplies models of prediction, uncertainty, and action. The synthesis borrows tools without pretending their originating theories were identical.

Reality Does Not Owe Us Explanations

Ivo argued that something need not make sense to us in order to exist.

Correct.

Reality is not required to satisfy human intuitions, conceptual schemes, or preferred levels of description.

But this principle cannot distinguish one hypothesis from another. It protects every unexplained assertion equally. The scientific question is not whether reality must be comprehensible. It is whether competing models make different observations more or less expected.

The Hard Problem itself is an explanatory demand. It says that even after the physical and functional facts are explained, a further explanation remains due. One cannot use “reality owes no explanation” to protect the demand for an additional explanation while exempting the proposed remainder from every criterion of explanatory work.

The proper stopping rule is not perfect intelligibility. It is comparative constraint:

Which account compresses more evidence, survives more interventions, excludes more outcomes, and accumulates fewer protected auxiliaries?

Phenomenology, Nondualism, and the Scope of the Theory

Phenomenology is prior as lived access. It helps specify the explanandum and reveals structures that third-person models can flatten or ignore. That epistemic priority does not establish ontological priority.

We encounter water before molecular theory. We experience pain before nociceptive science. First-person access identifies what must not disappear from the explanation. It does not determine in advance which physical organization constitutes it.

Nondual and neutral-monist frameworks also deserve more precision than the label “idealism” permits. A nondual metaphysics may reject subject and object as ultimately separate. Neutral monism may treat both mental and physical descriptions as aspects of a more basic structure. Dual-aspect views may preserve one underlying reality with irreducible modes of presentation.

These interpretations may be compatible with organizational closure. They are not entailed by it, and it is not entailed by them.

The scientific question is which organization constitutes perspective and how it changes under intervention. The metaphysical question is how that relational structure should ultimately be interpreted. Confusing the questions produces either imperial science or empirically decorative metaphysics, two institutions already well supplied with volunteers.

The theory also has a scope limit. It does not derive temporal order from quantum gravity, solve the Wheeler-DeWitt equation, or explain why there is a universe capable of forming perspectives. It asks a conditional question:

Given temporally ordered, nonequilibrium physical dynamics, which organizational regime constitutes a conscious perspective?

Whether time is fundamental, relational, or emergent remains orthogonal unless competing cosmologies imply different conditions for RPOC.

Near-death experiences likewise require neither dismissal nor metaphysical promotion. Severe physiological crisis is not identical to a demonstrated absence of all consciousness-supporting organization. Destabilization may transiently alter inhibition, excitation, memory, temporal integration, and self-modeling before closure collapses or while it recovers. Rich retrospective reports do not by themselves establish nonlocal consciousness.

Evidence of veridical, temporally localized conscious processing during a demonstrated absence of every relevant neural and organizational process would strongly challenge the framework. Residual, reorganizing, or recovering activity would not.

VI. The Dormant Solution: Twelve Fields, One Constraint Topology

The Hard Problem’s grammar can reify a process into a substance. “Something it is like” is already relational: something it is like for a system. The question presupposes a perspective while asking where perspective comes from.

What process did the grammar reify?

Twelve fields constrain the answer from different directions. They do not carry equal evidential weight. They are not completely independent. Several share historical lineages and formal vocabulary. Some provide experiments. Some provide mathematical tools. Some provide philosophical interpretation. Some provide culturally situated alternatives to substance ontology.

What converges is a constraint topology.

Thermodynamics

Landauer established a relation between logical irreversibility and physical dissipation under specified conditions. Bérut and colleagues experimentally verified the bound in a classical one-bit memory. Aimet and colleagues extended experimental investigation into a quantum many-body regime. Shimizu and colleagues’ 2026 DRAM experiment showed that realistic architecture and nonequilibrium preparation can impose limits beyond the idealized Landauer scenario.

The relevant conclusion is not that every distinction continuously burns kBT ln 2. It is that actively maintaining reliable distinctions through irreversible update, correction, overwrite, or reset cannot escape physical accounting.

Prigogine’s dissipative structures show that far-from-equilibrium organization persists through continuing exchange and dissipation. Active biological organization is therefore neither static substance nor thermodynamically free pattern.

Thermodynamics constrains the implementation of perspective. It does not independently identify phenomenality.

Organizational Closure

Montévil and Mossio define biological organization through mutually dependent constraints that contribute to one another’s maintenance. Moreno and Mossio develop this into a broader theory of biological autonomy. Kauffman and RAF theory explore autocatalytic organization and collectively self-sustaining reaction networks.

This literature supplies a principled answer to the boundary problem. A system is not merely whatever an observer circles in a photograph. Its organization is revealed by dependencies, compensation, regeneration, and collapse under intervention.

Closure produces a causal inside/outside asymmetry. It does not alone prove phenomenal interiority.

Autopoiesis and Adaptivity

Maturana and Varela describe living systems as networks that produce the components realizing their own organization and boundary. Di Paolo adds adaptivity: the system regulates its relation to conditions threatening its continued viability.

This contributes endogenous normativity. Some states are better or worse for the system because they preserve or threaten the organization whose continued existence defines the norm.

The perspective is not imposed entirely from outside. It is enacted through the system’s own viability conditions.

Active Inference

Friston’s free-energy principle supplies a formal language for systems that maintain a restricted repertoire of states through perception and action. Markov blankets can model conditional-independence relations between internal, external, sensory, and active states.

A Markov blanket is not identical to an organismal boundary, organizational closure, an Umwelt, or consciousness. Many systems can be described using blanket formalisms without being autonomous or conscious.

Active inference contributes models of prediction, uncertainty, action, and self-evidencing. Organizational closure adds endogenous regeneration of constitutive constraints. RPOC adds recursive integration of system-relative variables across time.

Whyte and colleagues’ minimal active-inference theory of consciousness identifies a small set of testable commitments shared across active-inference models. This places RPOC within an emerging research family rather than outside the literature.

Relational Quantum Mechanics

Rovelli’s relational quantum mechanics treats physical variables as relative to interactions between systems rather than as possessing complete observer-independent values in every context.

This supports a general rejection of a God’s-eye inventory of intrinsic properties. It does not establish that interacting systems are conscious or that quantum relationality is phenomenality.

Its contribution is conceptual discipline: perspective need not be an embarrassing late addition to a universe otherwise describable from nowhere.

Ontic Structural Realism

Ladyman, Ross, and French argue that structure and relations may be ontologically prior to objects conceived as bearers of intrinsic properties. On this view, objects are relatively stable nodes or patterns within structure.

This makes “what substance is consciousness made of?” less obviously mandatory. An identity can be relational and organizational without being unreal.

Structural realism does not supply a consciousness mechanism. It removes one metaphysical obstacle to taking organizational identity seriously.

Process Philosophy

Whitehead and Rescher reject substance ontology in favor of events, processes, and becoming.

Many Whiteheadian insights can be reconstructed naturalistically: persistence as repeated reconstitution, novelty as constrained transition, identity as historical organization, and entities as relational achievements rather than self-sufficient substances.

Rejecting panexperientialism abandons a substantive Whiteheadian commitment. It would be inaccurate to claim that every Whiteheadian commitment survives translation. The sharper claim is that process-relational insights can survive while the experiential primitive remains under separate evidential scrutiny.

The primitive may perform metaphysical work. Its differential empirical yield beyond process organization remains unclear.

Enactivism

Varela, Thompson, Rosch, and Di Paolo treat cognition as the enactment of a meaningful world through organism-environment coupling rather than the passive representation of a pregiven world.

Sense-making arises when perturbations matter relative to the organization’s own viability and capacities.

This closely overlaps with the coupling-position account. It does not automatically identify all sense-making with phenomenal consciousness.

Cybernetics

Ashby and Bateson provide the language of regulation, feedback, requisite variety, and differences that make a difference.

Bateson’s phrase is not identical to Landauer’s principle. One is semiotic and cybernetic; the other concerns physical information processing and irreversibility. Their relation is complementary: for a difference to guide a finite physical controller, some physical distinction must be detected, retained, transformed, or acted upon.

Cybernetics contributes the control logic through which distinctions become consequential.

Category Theory and Relational Biology

Lawvere’s work on self-reference and cartesian closed categories, Rosen’s closure to efficient causation, and Louie’s relational biology provide formal resources for describing systems whose components and entailment relations close upon one another.

Closure to efficient causation and organizational closure are related but not interchangeable. Category theory is a formal language, not experimental evidence for consciousness.

Its contribution is the possibility of substrate-neutral formalization without reducing the system to a list of material parts.

Biosemiotics

Peirce, von Uexküll, Hoffmeyer, and Deacon describe organisms as inhabiting worlds of signs and relevance structured by their sensory and action capacities.

An Umwelt and a Markov blanket offer partially overlapping descriptions of system-relative accessibility. They are not identical. An Umwelt includes meaning, affordance, and species-specific relevance; a Markov blanket is a statistical structure.

Biosemiotics contributes a vocabulary for qualitative differentiation: different organizations do not merely receive different data. They inhabit differently structured fields of significance.

Indigenous Relational Epistemologies and Madhyamaka

Many Indigenous epistemologies emphasize relation, place, responsibility, performance, and continuity rather than treating knowledge as an object separable from land and practice. Madhyamaka rejects intrinsic, self-sufficient existence and analyzes things as dependently originated.

Dependent origination is not organizational closure. The traditions differ in purpose, scope, formalism, and evidential method.

They share a structural refusal of intrinsic identity. That convergence can inform philosophical analysis without colonizing the traditions into being ancient endorsements of RPOC.

Their strongest scientific contribution is a prediction: Hard Problem intuitions should vary across cultures and languages with different models of self, substance, and relation.

What the Convergence Establishes

The twelve fields do not prove a phenomenal identity.

They establish a narrowing possibility landscape:

  • stable identities can be relational rather than substantial;
  • biological boundaries are actively maintained;
  • reliable information processing is physically implemented;
  • perspective can be system-relative without being arbitrary;
  • meaning and relevance depend on organization and coupling;
  • processes can be real without becoming extra substances;
  • no view from nowhere is required to make science objective.

A viable consciousness theory must explain how a maintained causal boundary becomes a system-relative world, how that world remains integrated across time, how its qualitative topology is formed, and why perturbing its organization changes conscious state and content.

RPOC is proposed as the structure satisfying those converging constraints.

The convergence is consilient in a qualified Whewellian sense: heterogeneous lines of inquiry constrain the same family of explanations even though they do not carry equal evidential weight or independently prove the identity claim.

The convergence is not designed. It is discovered.

It is convergence at the level of architecture, not twelve independent confirmations of phenomenality.

VII. Closure Is Not Yet Consciousness: The Perspectival Transition

The strongest objection to the closure formulation is simple:

Does organizational closure explain experience, or does it merely explain the conditions under which a bounded causal interior exists?

A cell has a biochemical interior.

A company has privileged internal information.

A closed curve has a geometric interior.

None of those facts alone establishes felt presence.

The theory must therefore distinguish levels of organization.

L1: Dissipative persistence
A pattern persists through energy flow but does not regenerate a rich organization of constraints.

L2: Organizational closure
Mutually dependent constraints regenerate the system’s boundary and viability conditions.

L3: Viability-sensitive cognition
The system detects relevant differences, learns, anticipates, and selects actions relative to its continued organization.

L4: Integrated perspectival control
Variables concerning self, world, uncertainty, memory, value, and action become jointly available to guide policy.

L5: Recursive phenomenal consciousness
The perspectival control regime becomes recursively and temporally available within its own ongoing regulation.

L6: Metacognitive and narrative self-consciousness
The system models itself as a persisting subject, reflects on its states, and can construct explicit reports or narratives.

RPOC places phenomenal consciousness no lower than L5. This is a hypothesis, not a stipulative definition.

Recurrence is insufficient. Spinal, cerebellar, homeostatic, and local sensory loops are recurrent.

Self-reference is insufficient. Regulatory systems track their own variables without necessarily producing phenomenality.

Integration is insufficient. Unconscious processes may integrate information, and the literature on subliminal integration remains methodologically contested.

Global broadcast is insufficient if the broadcast is externally scaffolded or lacks endogenous perspectival organization.

Higher-order representation is insufficient if it merely labels a state without participating in self-maintaining control.

Metabolism is insufficient. A liver is metabolically active.

Energy expenditure is insufficient. A furnace dissipates energy with impressive dedication and little reason to suspect existential anguish.

The proposed transition occurs when system-relative variables form one counterfactually effective, self-restoring control organization across time.

The qualitative character of experience then tracks constraint topology rather than scalar cost.

Two systems can dissipate similar energy while differing radically in:

  • sensory partitions;
  • bodily organization;
  • temporal horizons;
  • memory;
  • affective and viability weighting;
  • affordances;
  • action possibilities;
  • uncertainty structure;
  • developmental history.

Therefore:

similar energetic cost does not imply similar phenomenality.

If the identity hypothesis is correct:

different perspectival topology implies different qualitative character.

This explains why trauma, learning, development, culture, and bodily change can reorganize experience without requiring a new substance. Energetic expenditure constrains whether the organization can persist. History determines much of the topology of what can appear, matter, threaten, invite, and guide action within it.

The same principle addresses the nested-scale problem.

Cells, tissues, organs, organisms, and collectives may each instantiate partial closure. Which scale is the subject?

RPOC predicts that subject boundaries follow the scale at which perspectival control becomes independently self-restoring. A subsystem is a separate subject candidate when it can maintain its own memory continuity, uncertainty regulation, action selection, and self/world boundary under perturbation. A higher-scale subject candidate exists when lower systems cannot preserve those functions independently and participate in one integrated control regime.

This is the organizational version of the combination and exclusion problem. It is more experimentally tractable than combining microexperiences because the relevant dependencies can be perturbed.

The theory can still fail.

If consciousness reliably occurs without RPOC, the criterion is too strong.

If full RPOC reliably occurs without consciousness, the criterion is too weak.

If subject unity tracks another measure after closure topology is controlled, the scale criterion fails.

VIII. Differential Predictions of Recursively Perspectival Organizational Closure

A consciousness theory is not distinguished by how many findings it can accommodate after they occur. It is distinguished by where its expectations diverge from those of rival accounts.

The current framework combines established physical constraints, retrodictions consistent with existing evidence, and prospective differential predictions.

Tier One: Background Constraints

These are inherited from thermodynamics and organizational biology. They should not be counted as direct confirmations of the consciousness identity.

  • Active physical self-maintenance requires nonequilibrium resources.
  • Irreversible correction, overwriting, and reset have thermodynamic consequences.
  • Biological boundaries are regenerated rather than merely observed.
  • Passive persistence differs from organizational closure.

Tier Two: Retrodictions Shared with Rivals

These show compatibility and explanatory organization but do not uniquely support RPOC.

  • Anesthesia and severe brain injury alter integration, recurrence, complexity, and conscious report.
  • Conscious states generally support richer perturbational responses than many unconscious states.
  • Behavioral unresponsiveness can coexist with covert cognition or awareness.
  • Conscious state transitions often involve large-scale thalamocortical and subcortical organization.

GNWT, IIT, recurrent-processing theories, active-inference theories, biological naturalism, and several hybrid accounts predict overlapping portions of this evidence.

Tier Three: Prospective Differential Predictions

Prediction One: Cross-Substrate Double Dissociation

Construct two artificial systems with comparable behavior, memory, global broadcasting, uncertainty reports, and self-modeling.

System A is externally maintained. Its energy, memory integrity, error correction, goal continuation, repair, and reactivation are controlled by outside operators.

System B regulates resources, diagnoses and repairs damage, preserves identity conditions, integrates self-state with possible action, and restores its organization after perturbation.

RPOC predicts that B is a substantially stronger consciousness candidate than A despite similar output competence.

This differs from unconstrained computational functionalism and some higher-order or workspace interpretations, which may treat the relevant computation or access as sufficient. It also differs from strict biological naturalism by predicting that a genuinely self-maintaining nonbiological system could qualify.

The prediction is weakened by convergent evidence of consciousness in systems with recursive access but no endogenous maintenance, or by the systematic absence of consciousness indicators in genuinely self-maintaining nonbiological systems.

Prediction Two: Closure Without Recursive Perspective Is Insufficient

Cells, tissues, immune systems, and simple autonomous robots may possess organizational closure, learning, and adaptive regulation without phenomenal consciousness.

RPOC predicts:

O = 1 and R = 0 implies C = 0

where R denotes recursive integration of self/world/action variables across time.

Robust consciousness evidence in systems lacking any such recursively available model would falsify the requirement.

Prediction Three: Closed-Loop Organization Versus Yoked Replay

Compare two conditions with closely matched activity, broadcast, recurrence, integration, outputs, and energy consumption.

In the closed-loop condition, internal states genuinely influence subsequent sensing, prediction, correction, and action.

In the yoked condition, recorded or externally generated signals reproduce similar activity and outputs without participating counterfactually in the system’s own maintenance.

RPOC predicts greater consciousness in the closed-loop condition.

If replay preserves the same conscious state after endogenous counterfactual control is removed, organizational closure is not constitutive in the proposed sense.

IIT may make a similar prediction because it emphasizes intrinsic causal power. The decisive comparison would ask whether consciousness follows IIT’s maximal causal complex or the RPOC network of self-maintaining perspectival variables when the proposed boundaries diverge.

Prediction Four: Selective Perspectival Disconnection

Preserve sensory recurrence, stimulus discrimination, broad network activity, and some forms of global availability while disrupting the integration of content with bodily state, uncertainty, temporal memory, valuation, and action possibilities.

RPOC predicts altered or fragmented presence, ownership, salience, unity, or flexible conscious access despite preserved local content processing.

This differs from local recurrent-processing theories if adequate sensory recurrence remains sufficient on those accounts. It pressures workspace accounts if broadcast remains intact, and IIT if global causal integration remains high.

If perspectival variables can be selectively disrupted while phenomenal presence and unity remain fully intact, those variables are not necessary in the proposed form.

Prediction Five: Incremental Prediction Beyond Existing Metrics

During anesthesia, sleep, coma, recovery, and stimulation, measure:

  • PCI or related perturbational complexity;
  • global connectivity and broadcast;
  • local recurrence;
  • gross metabolic activity;
  • causal integration;
  • recursive self/world/action coupling;
  • recovery autonomy after perturbation.

RPOC predicts that a preregistered measure of recursively perspectival closure will improve out-of-sample prediction of conscious state after the established variables are controlled.

In compact form:

P(C | RPOC, PCI, G, R, E) > P(C | PCI, G, R, E)

If RPOC measures add no reliable predictive or explanatory value, the theory has probably renamed the conjunction of existing mechanisms rather than discovered a new constitutive organization.

Prediction Six: Subject Boundaries Follow Closure Topology

The number and boundaries of conscious subjects should follow independently self-restoring perspectival-control loops, not anatomy, communication, maximal integration, or global broadcasting alone.

Two coupled subsystems count as separate subject candidates when each can preserve its own memory continuity, uncertainty regulation, action selection, and self/world boundary under perturbation.

They count as one higher-scale subject candidate when neither can sustain those functions independently and both depend on one integrated control regime.

Callosotomy, modular artificial agents, coupled organoids, distributed robots, and human-machine systems provide possible test domains.

If subject unity and fragmentation consistently follow another metric after RPOC topology is controlled, the scale-selection claim fails.

Prediction Seven: Closure-Specific Hysteresis

Because organizational closure is self-maintaining and historically conditioned, loss and recovery of consciousness should be path-dependent.

Systems with similar instantaneous drug concentration, connectivity, energy use, and activity may differ because one retains a recoverable perspectival attractor and the other does not.

Conscious state should therefore depend on recent trajectory, not current variables alone:

C(t) = f(X(t), H(0:t))

This predicts hysteresis between induction and recovery and sensitivity to the order in which control relations fail or return.

Active-inference, metastability, and resonant-closure theories may make related predictions. If instantaneous broadcast, integration, or complexity fully predicts state and history adds nothing, the temporal-closure component is unnecessary.

The Prediction Fingerprint

No one prediction differentiates RPOC from every competitor.

Closed-loop causality overlaps with IIT.

Recursive self-modeling overlaps with higher-order, attention-schema, and active-inference accounts.

Global integration overlaps with GNWT.

Recurrence overlaps with recurrent-processing theory.

Embodied self-maintenance overlaps with biological naturalism.

Dynamic closure overlaps with resonant-closure and regenerative-signaling proposals.

The distinctive claim is the joint necessity of:

endogenous self-maintenance + recursive system-relative modeling + temporal integration + counterfactual control + scale-specific closure + substrate neutrality

That is a prediction fingerprint, not a solitary knockout experiment.

The predictions should be preregistered against explicit rival interpretations. Proponents of the rival theories should approve or correct how their views are represented before data collection. Otherwise every theory discovers after the result that it had predicted the outcome all along, a behavior normally considered suspicious outside consciousness science and organized religion.

IX. What the 2025–2026 Literature Actually Shows

The empirical and theoretical landscape has shifted substantially in the last two years, but not toward one completed answer.

The COGITATE adversarial collaboration, published in Nature in 2025, tested preregistered predictions associated with IIT and GNWT across fMRI, MEG, and intracranial recordings. Important predictions of both theories were challenged. The result did not confirm organizational closure. It demonstrated that prominent theories can survive decades of broad compatibility while encountering difficulty under jointly specified differential tests.

The methodological lesson is direct: RPOC should enter adversarial comparison before its auxiliary assumptions become elastic enough to explain every result.

The IIT scientific-status controversy produced an open letter arguing that central claims were insufficiently testable, followed by substantive responses disputing that assessment. The episode did not settle IIT’s status. It made falsifiability, construct validity, and advance specification of predictions unavoidable topics for the field.

Seth’s biological naturalism argues that consciousness may depend on concrete life processes rather than abstract computation alone. Milinkovic and Aru’s 2026 biological computationalism develops this line by emphasizing scale-inseparable, substrate-dependent, multiscale and continuous-valued features of biological computation. Their proposal challenges substrate-neutral theories, including RPOC, to specify what physical organization could reproduce those properties outside biology.

Whyte and colleagues’ peer-reviewed minimal theory of consciousness implicit in active inference identifies shared commitments across active-inference models and frames them as a testable theory. Its emphasis on world modeling, inferential selection, and recursively shared belief is closely adjacent to RPOC. The overlap strengthens the research direction but reduces any claim that the ingredients are unique.

Arneth’s 2026 resonant-closure hypothesis explicitly proposes consciousness as a dynamically self-stabilized informational regime involving recursive self-modeling, active inference, phase coherence, and dynamically maintained entropic boundary conditions. The resemblance is substantial. It is also a theoretical proposal, not experimental confirmation of either framework.

Cariani and Baker’s 2026 organizational-closure account argues that neural signals must be actively regenerated through recurrent and reentrant circuits to persist and contribute to awareness. This directly supports the importance of regenerative organization while preserving the necessary qualification in their title: “a possible basis for conscious awareness.”

Kirkeby-Hinrup and Stoll’s 2026 naturalization proposal begins from a methodological demand close to the one advanced here: every accepted property of consciousness must be made continuous with properties admitted by the natural sciences. Their probabilistic-computational identity differs from RPOC. Their work demonstrates that naturalization is becoming an explicit rather than apologetic research goal.

Toker and colleagues’ 2026 adversarial artificial-intelligence framework analyzed more than 680,000 electrophysiological samples and validated models across 565 patients, healthy volunteers, and animals. It generated testable mechanistic predictions involving basal-ganglia pathways and cortical inhibitory coupling, then supported those predictions using diffusion MRI, RNA sequencing, animal models, and electrophysiology. This implicates distributed thalamocortical-basal-ganglia organization rather than a single local consciousness center.

Again, this does not confirm RPOC. It demonstrates that mechanistically interpretable models can now generate causal predictions and intervention targets in disorders-of-consciousness research.

The 2026 DRAM experiment by Shimizu and colleagues provides an important thermodynamic correction. Real physical architecture did not achieve the ideal Landauer limit even under effectively long erasure protocols because the initial state could not be prepared in thermal equilibrium. Accuracy, error tolerance, speed, and physical preparation all matter. This supports implementation-dependent thermodynamic costs, not a universal ongoing fee attached to every distinction.

Taken together, the literature supports the following statement:

A discernible 2025–2026 research trend favors dynamic, recurrent, multiscale, self-stabilizing, and physically embodied accounts of conscious processing. Organizational and entropic closure are now explicit members of that research family. The field remains theoretically plural, and no experiment has established that these regimes are identical to phenomenality rather than necessary for it.

That is as strong as the current evidence allows.

The trajectory is not “every active program is converging on my answer.” The more interesting result is that several programs are independently converging on overlapping problems:

  • how information becomes globally and recursively available;
  • how biological and artificial implementations differ;
  • how conscious state depends on multiscale dynamics;
  • how causal organization rather than surface behavior determines subject attribution;
  • how boundaries, uncertainty, and self-models are maintained;
  • how theories can be forced into preregistered disagreement.

RPOC belongs inside this convergence. It has not inherited ownership of it.

X. The Generative Asymmetry

I want to close with the question neither Hans, Ivo, nor Segall answered in an empirically discriminating form, and that I think remains the most important question.

What additional work does your framework do?

Not what is it compatible with. Not what can it accommodate after the fact. What observation becomes more likely? What outcome is excluded? What intervention follows? What contradiction is resolved? What new measurement becomes possible?

The naturalistic consciousness research program produced covert-awareness paradigms, standardized behavioral assessment, PCI, anesthesia models, perturbational methods, cross-species comparisons, and increasingly mechanistic intervention targets.

No metaphysical position deserves ownership of those discoveries merely because its advocates can reinterpret them. The same correction applies to RPOC. The framework did not produce Owen’s experiment or PCI. It may explain why those measurements matter and generate stronger future predictions, but it cannot retroactively claim every useful result generated by the wider scientific tradition.

The proper comparison is incremental:

What does RPOC predict beyond the empirical base model?

The differential-prediction section answers that question provisionally. Closed-loop versus yoked replay, incremental prediction beyond PCI and broadcast, subject boundaries following recovery topology, and the cross-substrate double dissociation are the core wagers.

Panpsychism and idealism may provide coherent metaphysical interpretations. Epiphenomenalism may preserve a distinction between causal organization and phenomenal accompaniment. Nondualism may reject the ultimate separation of subject and object. These positions can perform conceptual or existential work.

Their empirical work begins when they specify what observation would be different if they were true rather than false.

That standard applies symmetrically to this theory.

If RPOC adds no prediction beyond recurrence, integration, broadcast, metacognition, and biological regulation, then it is a useful synthesis but not a distinct consciousness theory.

If recursive perspectival closure is found in systems reliably lacking consciousness, the identity fails.

If robust consciousness occurs without it, the identity fails.

If subject boundaries follow another metric, the scale claim fails.

If externally maintained systems with no endogenous closure exhibit the same convergent consciousness indicators as self-maintaining systems, the necessity claim fails.

That is what it means for the framework to do empirical work rather than merely narrate the evidence.

Compatibility is free. Production is expensive.

The expense is still the point, but not because thermodynamic cost is itself consciousness. The physical organization of perspective must be maintained against noise, error, turnover, and perturbation. It must regenerate the distinctions through which a world remains available to the system. It must preserve some history, discard other history, integrate uncertainty, and keep possible action coupled to value and consequence.

Consciousness, on this account, is the recursively integrated perspectival organization that this work keeps from dissolving.

Conclusion: The Hard Problem as a Scientific Wager

Nothing nonphysical has yet been shown to be missing.

What the evidence increasingly reveals is that perspective is not a passive view attached to mechanism. It is a dynamically maintained organization of distinctions, predictions, memories, values, and possible actions, continually regenerated within a finite physical system. That organization is not thermodynamically free. It persists because errors are corrected, obsolete states are overwritten, signals are regenerated, boundaries are repaired, and nonequilibrium resources are consumed.

The proposal advanced here is that consciousness is identical not to this expenditure itself but to the recursively integrated perspectival organization the expenditure sustains. Thermodynamic organizational closure keeps a world available to a system from its own coupling position. The topology and history of that closure determine what can appear, matter, persist, and guide what happens next.

Multiple fields converge on the architecture of this proposal, though not yet on its phenomenal identity. They repeatedly replace intrinsic substance with maintained relation, passive boundary with enacted boundary, static representation with recurrent control, and observer-independent description with system-relative access.

That is not twelve independent proofs.

It is a rapidly narrowing possibility landscape.

The Hard Problem should therefore be decomposed.

Its meta-problem component is open to cognitive, linguistic, cultural, and self-modeling explanation.

Its boundary component becomes tractable through organizational closure and intervention-sensitive system identification.

Its remaining phenomenal component becomes a scientific wager:

Is recursively integrated perspectival organizational closure identical with phenomenal presence?

The wager can fail.

Systems may instantiate the proposed organization without consciousness.

Consciousness may occur without the proposed organization.

Rival theories may predict state, content, or subject boundaries more accurately.

The framework may turn out to be an elegant synthesis of necessary mechanisms rather than a sufficient identity.

That is not a weakness to be hidden. It is the opening through which reality enters the theory.

The grammar suggested that mechanism and experience were separate things requiring a bridge. The science increasingly suggests that what matters is not an extra ingredient but an organization continuously kept from dissolving.

Whether that organization exhausts phenomenality is now a scientific question rather than a metaphysical exemption.

The Hard Problem is not explained away.

It is made answerable to the world.

Appendix: Revision Clarifications

This revision preserves the article’s central argument while sharpening its empirical commitments.

  • “Consciousness is what organizational closure costs” is retained as an intuition but clarified: consciousness is proposed as the perspectival organization sustained by costly closure, not the heat or energy expenditure itself.
  • Organizational closure is treated as the physical scaffold of perspective rather than a sufficient condition for consciousness by itself.
  • The Hard Problem is decomposed into meta-problem, boundary, and phenomenal-identity components.
  • Chalmers’s later work is presented as reframing and increasing pressure on the Hard Problem rather than formally contradicting his earlier position.
  • Landauer’s principle is applied specifically to irreversible updating, correction, overwriting, and reset rather than to the mere persistence of every distinction.
  • Clinical findings are used to establish the limits of behavioral diagnosis and the importance of measuring consciousness, not to assign those misdiagnoses to a philosophical doctrine.
  • PCI is described as perturbation-evoked complexity, with organizational closure presented as a testable interpretation rather than a synonym.
  • The twelve-field convergence is recast as convergence on a constraint topology, with empirical, formal, philosophical, and cultural contributions kept distinct.
  • Indigenous relational epistemologies and Madhyamaka are treated as structurally relevant but nonidentical traditions that generate comparative hypotheses rather than as direct confirmations.
  • The framework’s new differential predictions replace broad background constraints and shared retrodictions as its primary tests.
  • Recent peer-reviewed 2025–2026 work is incorporated as an emerging research family rather than claimed as confirmation of the identity hypothesis.
