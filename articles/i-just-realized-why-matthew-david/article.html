Where I think the debate now stands

My framework has reciprocal obligations. D must preserve enough stable content across the relevant rivals to support the inference being made, and questionmark ?X cannot become permanent insulation against evidence. On August 30, in the correction that followed his first misattribution, Matt described my position as “explicit agnosticism regarding the primitive intrinsic character of causality.”[13]

Nathan’s request for an erratum is fair. I haven’t read the entire reply he has shared, since I just don’t have the time right now. That said, there remains a substantive disagreement that Nathan himself recognizes when he asks whether the decomposition between experiential and non-experiential understandings of causal relation is finally coherent. I do not think it is finally coherent to sever affectivity from causal relation, even hypothetically. My objection is not that Nathan has chosen the nonexperiential variant, but that the factorization already treats causal uptake, integration, transition, response, etc, as a common core that can be rendered intelligible independently of feeling.

My argument that “experience is not a predicate” was trying to get at this issue. I accept Nathan’s point that his argument does not depend on Whitehead’s technical definition of that term. But my proposal is not that an antecedently intelligible causal relation possesses an additional experiential feature, but that concretely considered, causal relation just is felt inheritance of the past into the present.

Schelling made a convergent point against Newtonian physicists in his 1797 text Ideas for a Philosophy of Nature: “You can in no way make intelligible what a force might be independent of you. For force as such makes itself known only to your feeling.” He goes on to say that physics nevertheless makes objective use of the concept of feeling in its explanations of causal relations. This does not by itself prove nature is experiential, but does make the case that the supposedly objective concept of causal force derives its concrete intelligibility from embodied feeling.

Now, I agree with Nathan that this identification of bodily feeling and causal relation a such bears a comparative burden with alternative metaphysical accounts of causation. But my identity claim is not offered as the detection of an additional feature. Rather, it competes abductively with rival accounts of what causality concretely is. Here is where Nathan’s epistemic asymmetry between physicalism and panexperientialism breaks down. A “physical” description gives us abstract structural dynamical relations, whereas embodied experience provides the one case in which causal efficacy is disclosed directly from within. Again, this doesn’t necessarily prove the universal extent of experiential relations, but it does provide an evidential basis for asking whether the supposedly experientially neutral causal core is itself an abstraction from something more concrete.

So while the comparative burden is one I take seriously, there is no scheme-neutral standpoint that might vindicate one scheme over another (as Nathan himself also seems to admit). The relative verification of a scheme is sought in its general success in elucidating experience across the various domains of human life (including but not limited to scientific investigation), and not in its peculiar certainty or initial clarity of first principles. Rival schemes are experimental matrices of categories whose warrant is determined through comparison of their coherence, adequacy, applicability, and avoidance of misplaced concreteness. We cannot start our comparison of schemes by assuming an experience-neutral common ground of causality in relation to which the rivals might be judged, since whether such a concept of causality is legitimate or already too abstract is precisely what is in dispute.

So I grant that Nathan was not positively asserting feelingless causality. My question is whether causality can even be rendered hypothetically in a concretely intelligible way after feeling has been analytically bracketed out. I take Whitehead’s panexperiential proposal to be a fallible but evidentially motivated speculative generalization, not an experiential predicate arbitrarily added to an otherwise complete causal description.

The disagreement has changed levels

Matt is primarily asking an ontological question:

What is concrete causal actuality really like?

My work is now operating at two additional levels.

At the methodological level, I ask:

Which conclusions actually depend on which metaphysical assumptions?

At the operational level, I can ask:

Which candidate boundary predicts better? Which constitutive relation survives ablation or intervention? Which conclusions remain invariant when X is affirmed, denied, or left unresolved?

Those three levels interact, but they are not interchangeable.

Suppose, for the purpose of analysis, that I grant Matt’s preferred description:

Concrete causal actualization is prehensive feeling.

The next question remains:

Was that claim necessary to derive the result under examination?

If the result follows from D while X and ¬X both remain undecided, then the result cannot by itself discriminate X from rivals that preserve D.

That is the point my worked deductive proofs in my Synthbiosis article made much easier to see.[3]

D ⊬ X
D ⊬ ¬X

Therefore C can be derived without deciding X.

The conclusion is a dependency claim: X was unnecessary to that derivation.

But those worked deductive proofs go further than proof bookkeeping. They supply a concrete case in which the factorization constrains model comparison and intervention. That is more than an out-of-domain illustration. It is a worked stress test with consequences.

This is no longer only a dispute about which metaphysics sounds more coherent

Before that worked demonstration, my factorization could still be treated as one philosophical description competing with another. Matt could ask whether I had merely redescribed experiential actuality in thinner language and then mistaken the abstraction for an independent causal core.

That is the category change.

The universal metaphysical question can remain open: whether every concrete actual occasion everywhere in the universe is constituted through prehensive feeling. They can be investigated.

If adding X changes no derivation, boundary prediction, intervention, or explanatory constraint in the worked case, then the success of that case cannot be credited specifically to X.

What the worked case already discriminates

The worked deductive case in my Synthbiosis article gives D plus questionmark ?X a more specific evidential achievement than simple compatibility.

It supplies direct counterevidence to any claim that X is required for the positive operational content of causal efficacy, inheritance, integration, constitution, boundary, or intervention. The worked derivations proceed while X is unresolved. Candidate boundaries can still be compared. Interventions can still succeed or fail. Constitutive claims can still be ablated. The framework can still lose.[3]

That means at least four stronger necessity claims are no longer available without additional argument:

X is required for D to have positive content.

X is required for D to generate nontrivial derivations.

X is required to discover or compare causal boundaries.

X is required for intervention-sensitive research to proceed.

The worked case shows that substantial process-relational inquiry can do all four while the universal experiential extension remains open.

This is genuine discrimination, but its target should be stated precisely. It discriminates against necessity claims for X and against attempts to credit shared operational successes specifically to X. It does not yet identify a phenomenon that the Whiteheadian X itself forbids.

Matt can still accommodate the results of those proofs by redescribing the successful causal and relational structures as abstractions from an underlying prehensive actuality. But that flexibility is not additional evidential support for X. If X is compatible with the result whatever the result turns out to be, then that result had little capacity to expose X as mistaken and therefore supplies little severe evidence specifically for X.

Deborah Mayo and Aris Spanos formulate the relevant methodological principle in terms of severe or probative testing: a claim earns evidential warrant when it passes a test that was genuinely capable of revealing flaws or discrepancies had they been present.[17] Mayo later summarizes the same idea by saying that a claim is severely tested to the extent that it passes a test that “probably would have found flaws” if they were present.[18]

On that standard, evidence that X was effectively guaranteed to accommodate is weak evidence for X even if X remains perfectly compatible with it.

An independent hypothesis-discrimination perspective reaches the same place. The evidential value of a procedure depends in part on how well it can discriminate between competing hypotheses, not merely on whether one hypothesis can be made consistent with the observed outcome.[19] If X, questionmark ?X, and serious rival completions of D all preserve the same result, that result has little power to choose among them.

A hypothesis does not gain differential evidential credit merely by surviving evidence it was never at serious risk of failing.

Compatibility is cheap when every live outcome can be absorbed. What matters comparatively is whether X risks something: a result, constraint, failure condition, likelihood difference, or explanatory consequence that changes specifically because X is true.

So the worked example changes the burden in a very specific way: X can no longer earn evidential credit merely because the shared process-relational machinery works. It must earn credit for something that changes when X is present.

My deductive proofs in the Synthbiosis article do not answer the universal Whiteheadian question. They change what we can do while that question remains open.

D plus questionmark ?X has begun functioning as an operational research programme at the methodological level: it generates derivations, rival boundary models, interventions, invariance tests, and failure conditions while the disputed metaphysical extension remains unresolved.

The Whiteheadian residual question therefore becomes sharper:

What further explanatory, predictive, intervention-relevant, or cross-domain result specifically requires X once the shared process-relational machinery has already done this much?

The reciprocal burden: D, the scope bridge, and what would make either side revise

Matt’s latest reply also identifies a positive gain more clearly than before. He argues that prehension gives a common account of causal efficacy and the becoming of an occasion of experience: in embodied experience, the world’s influence is constitutive, and prehension generalizes that relation throughout nature.[20]

I agree that this kind of continuity is a genuine theoretical virtue. But it also isolates the bridge that now needs to carry the universal extension.

Embodied experience is not an arbitrary sample of causal actuality. It is a highly organized case involving living organization, integration, memory, responsiveness, valuation, nervous systems in the human case, and whatever further organization is relevant to the phenomenology being discussed. So the comparative question is not whether embodied causal influence can be constitutive. I grant that. It is what warrants treating the character of causal influence disclosed in those cases as representative of concrete actuality universally.

That bridge might be a necessity relation, a structural identity, an explanatory dependency, a demonstration that serious rivals fail, an argument that the experiential dimension cannot arise only under particular organizational conditions, or something else. But the generalization needs some independently assessable support beyond the fact that Whitehead’s hypothesis performs the generalization.

There are two possible readings of the phrase “the same activity” in Matt’s continuity argument.[20] If it means causal inheritance, receptivity to antecedent conditions, integration, transition, constraint, and real causal influence, then much of the continuity belongs to D and can be preserved by serious non-Whiteheadian rivals. If it means the full objective-subjective structure of prehension, including felt inheritance, then the sentence contains X and states the universalization whose warrant is under examination.

This is the distinction I now want to draw between substantive explanatory unification and categoreal unification. Substantive unification earns comparative credit when one account removes independent primitives, constrains phenomena that rivals leave unconstrained, reveals dependencies rivals miss, improves explanatory compression, transfers successfully across domains, or solves a problem competitors solve only with additional machinery. Categoreal unification, by itself, places heterogeneous phenomena under one general description.

I am not claiming that Whitehead achieves only categoreal unification. I am asking how we tell the two apart in this case.

My side carries a matching burden. If D is supposed to preserve content across rival ontologies, I have to show that the common content is stable enough for the target inference rather than merely verbal similarity produced by abstraction. If causal efficacy in Whitehead, causal power in a powers account, and causal dependence in another process ontology do not preserve sufficient common counterfactual, relational, intervention-relevant, or explanatory structure, then that particular D has fractured. My framework should stop factorizing at that level and compare the whole rivals.

Failure of that factorization would count against my method at that application. It would not by itself establish X.

And questionmark ?X has a revision condition too. If X repeatedly outperforms its strongest rivals under criteria that do not assume X, removes explanatory burdens they cannot remove without greater cost, acquires a defensible bridge for its universal scope, predicts where my D-factorizations will fracture, and survives serious comparative attempts to make it lose, then continued suspension could become less warranted than provisional acceptance.

The reciprocal question for Matt is equally concrete: what result, rival success, failed scope bridge, or explanatory cost would lower his confidence in the universal extension even if Whitehead’s scheme remained coherent?

An asymmetry in evidential exposure

There is now an important asymmetry between the methodological programme and the universal metaphysical extension.

My framework already exposes some of its claims to failure. A proposed boundary can predict worse than a rival boundary. An alleged constitutive dependency can disappear under ablation. An intervention can fail to produce the expected difference. A cross-domain rule can break when transferred. Those failures would count against the corresponding methodological or causal claim.[3]

In the material under discussion here, X has not yet been tied to a comparably specific outcome that would count against it. Matt explicitly describes the move as an abductive metaphysical generalization rather than a deduction or unique experimental result.[1]

That flexibility is a vice for differential evidential support. The more readily X can absorb mutually incompatible possible outcomes, the less any one of those outcomes can count as a severe test of X. Mayo’s framework makes the point without requiring a simplistic one-observation-one-refutation picture: evidential warrant depends on whether the inquiry had a serious capacity to uncover error, not merely on whether the claim remained standing afterward.[17][18]

The same asymmetry appears in comparative discrimination. If a result would occur under X, questionmark ?X, and serious rivals alike, observing that result gives us little leverage for choosing among them. A hypothesis earns more differential credit when the evidence discriminates in its favor, not when it merely survives alongside everything else.[19]

So the relevant question is not only whether X can describe a successful result after the fact. It is whether that success would have been less expected, less coherent, less derivable, or more difficult to accommodate under the strongest alternatives.

Whitehead’s own method makes that burden unusually clear

This is where Whitehead himself becomes methodologically important.

In Adventures of Ideas, Whitehead describes speculative philosophy as embodying the method of the “working hypothesis”. He explicitly allows multiple competing working hypotheses and says that a new one “must be criticized from its own point of view.” He also warns against the “dogmatic fallacy”, the treatment of a working hypothesis as clear and irreformable.[4]

Whitehead then describes his own experiential generalization conditionally. If we hold that final actualities are occasions of experience, then, “on that hypothesis,” the direct evidence of experiential connectedness can suggest categories applicable more widely in nature.[4]

That is close to one explicit fallibilist strand of Whitehead’s own methodological language.

Whitehead adopts the hypothesis. I have not yet found enough differential warrant to move from questionmark ?X to X. The methodological overlap is recognizable: formulate a sufficiently general working hypothesis, compare its reach and failures, and keep it open to revision.

My framework makes that comparative instruction more operational. It asks which conclusions survive premise ablation, which boundaries improve prediction or intervention, which rival descriptions preserve the target, and where a disputed metaphysical commitment changes the result. In that sense, the worked proofs in my Synthbiosis article do not replace Whitehead’s speculative method; they add a way of tracking proof dependence and empirical uptake inside the comparison.

This matters because a rival cannot be fairly tested by first requiring it to contain the distinctive commitment of the hypothesis it is competing against.

If experiential togetherness is the very thing under comparison, then:

“Your causal terms are unintelligible unless they already contain experiential togetherness”

cannot by itself settle the comparison. If X is built into the criterion for what counts as intelligible causation without an independent argument establishing that criterion, the comparison would beg the question against rivals that deny X. The rival must be allowed to specify causal efficacy in its own terms, and then those terms can be tested for coherence, adequacy, explanatory reach, cost, derivational consequences, and empirical performance.

Otherwise X has stopped functioning as one working hypothesis among rivals and has become the admission rule for which rivals are allowed to count as intelligible.

The philosophical literature makes the rival space concrete, while the worked deductive example in my Synthbiosis article shows how those rivals can be compared without first settling the universal metaphysics.

Abduction can justify serious pursuit without yet settling acceptance

Rune Nyrup’s peer-reviewed treatment of inference to the best explanation develops an explicitly Peircean account on which explanatory reasoning “first and foremost justifies pursuing hypotheses” rather than automatically licensing their acceptance as true.[5]

That does not diminish Matt’s abductive case. It types its evidential force more carefully. X can earn pursuit, increased credence, explanatory attention, and even superiority to some competitors while the move from questionmark ?X to X remains open.

Real causal production has non-Humean, nonexperiential competitors

Robert Koons’ work on powers ontology is useful because it occupies precisely the logical territory that disappears if the choice is framed as experiential causation versus Humean regularity. Koons develops a realist, non-Humean ontology of causal powers, including active powers that initiate change and passive powers for undergoing it, without defining causal production as phenomenal feeling.[6]

The existence of such theories does not decide the contest. It establishes that there is developed metaphysical territory between bare succession and Whiteheadian prehension.

That means X has to outperform serious powers and process rivals rather than receive causal realism by default.

Monism does not uniquely select panexperientialism

Donovan Wishon’s work on radical empiricism and neutral monism is especially relevant to Matt’s appeal to William James. Wishon characterizes neutral monism as grounding mind and matter in a reality “intrinsically neither mental nor material”, and places James within a historical debate in which different monists disagreed about the character of the common ground.[7]

James therefore strengthens the case for taking relations and experience seriously. He does not uniquely decide the further Whiteheadian claim that every concrete actual relation is prehensive feeling.

This matters because the live comparison is not Whitehead versus bifurcation. There are monist rivals concerning what the common relational ground is like.

First-person causal phenomenology is evidence, but its metaphysical reach remains a further question

Matt’s abductive starting point is phenomenological: the felt inheritance of the past in embodied experience. That evidence belongs in the tribunal.

Contemporary work on the experience of agency also gives us reason to distinguish phenomenological presentation from transparent access to causal ontology. Synofzik, Vosgerau, and Voss review evidence that the experience of agency integrates predictive and postdictive cues rather than functioning as simple infallible access to the causal structure itself.[8]

Their target is agency rather than Whiteheadian causal efficacy in general, so the result should be used narrowly. It supports a methodological caution: first-person causal phenomenology is genuine evidence, while the step from that phenomenology to the universal metaphysical character of causation still requires a bridge.

Experiential continuity carries its own unity question

Matt explicitly distinguishes Whitehead’s process-relational view from constitutive micropsychism, and that distinction should be preserved.[2] Whiteheadian actual occasions are not simply tiny subjects aggregated like particles into a larger subject.

Still, Matt’s own formulation says that the many feelings of the past become one feeling in the present.[1] The general panpsychism literature is relevant at this narrower point because it shows that experiential ubiquity does not by itself erase questions about phenomenal unity and composition.

Sam Coleman formulates the subject-summing problem as a serious obstacle for constitutive panpsychism.[9] Gregory Miller, writing sympathetically from within the panpsychist programme, describes an explanatory gap between micro-experiential and macro-experiential facts and develops phenomenal bonding as a proposed solution.[10] Siddharth subsequently argues that phenomenal bonding remains inadequate as a solution.[11]

These papers do not directly refute Whitehead’s concrescence. They show why the phrase “experience all the way down” cannot by itself be credited with solving every bridge problem. A Whiteheadian account can answer the unity problem differently, but that answer still belongs on the comparative scorecard.

What Matt’s argument now successfully establishes

Matt has now given me a much cleaner reason to take X seriously.

X offers a unified interpretation in which the causality disclosed in embodied experience is generalized rather than treated as a local exception. It promises continuity between experience and nature, avoids one traditional form of mind-world bifurcation, and provides a phenomenologically anchored interpretation of inheritance and receptivity.

Those are genuine theoretical virtues. They can raise the comparative standing of X.

The remaining question is how much discriminatory work those virtues perform, how far they warrant the scope of X, and what they cost relative to the strongest rivals.

The comparison now has at least two scorecards. One is philosophical: coherence, phenomenological adequacy, continuity, primitive cost, explanatory compression, scope, and treatment of rival problems. The other is operational: derivational dependence, prediction, intervention, boundary discrimination, invariance under premise changes, and cross-domain transfer.

Does X remove an independent bridge between causal efficacy and experience that rivals require? Does it reduce primitives? Does it constrain phenomena that rivals leave arbitrary? Does it improve explanatory compression or successful transfer? Does Whitehead’s account of concrescence discharge a unity problem more effectively than rival accounts discharge theirs? What becomes harder to explain if universal prehension is removed and the strongest rival substituted?

And now there is an additional question supplied by the worked proofs:

When X is varied, suspended, or replaced while D is preserved, which concrete results actually change?

Those are the questions that can move me from questionmark ?X toward X, away from X, or toward a more differentiated view in which X matters for some explanatory targets and not others.

D plus questionmark ?X already has differential support against the claim that X is necessary for operational causal content, derivation, boundary comparison, intervention, or cross-domain methodological productivity.

X has not yet supplied, in this exchange, a result that both discriminates it from D plus questionmark ?X and exposes X to meaningful evidential risk.

That second sentence should not be read as equal weighting of the metaphysical rivals. X may still have abductive virtues that increase its plausibility. It means only that the shared empirical and operational successes cannot do the discriminating work by themselves.

Likewise, D is not exempt from pressure. If the supposedly shared carrier repeatedly fractures under faithful translation, or preserving it requires artificial abstractions or hidden bridges, that should count against the factorization. If X repeatedly removes those liabilities while retaining the evidence, I should update toward X.

The live issue is therefore reciprocal rather than one-sided: what does X specifically earn, what does D genuinely preserve, what warrants the universal transport, and what could make either framework revise?

Why the earlier loop happened

I can now state the recurring problem more precisely.

Part of the loop was genuine terminological friction. Some of my shorthand compressed a process-relational view into language that could sound like substances bearing properties or two independently constituted terms being identified. Matt was right to object to that grammar, and I have now replaced it.

But that does not explain the whole loop.

Even after Matt accurately reported my agnosticism, the evaluation often translated that agnosticism through assumptions internal to the Whiteheadian hypothesis. Analytical distinction became ontological separation. Leaving X unresolved became “bracketing experience.” Asking what D can derive without X became imagining causal actuality with experience subtracted from it.[15][16]

The provenance record makes the distinction especially clear: Matt himself had previously described my position in terms of the intrinsic character of causality remaining open, and had described his own positive proposal as causal relation concretely being felt inheritance.[13] The later vocabulary correction therefore narrows the wording problem without eliminating the inferential problem.

That is why lengthy responses could remain philosophically sophisticated while missing the proof-dependence question.

Matt’s September 17 reply improved the situation because he stated the key sentence:

D does not entail X.[1]

Once that is accepted, the dispute no longer needs to keep circling around whether my factorization secretly means two worlds, an extra property, or feelingless mechanism.

We can compare the hypotheses.

The position I now occupy is closer to Whitehead’s method than the debate makes it sound

I have retained a great deal of the process-relational architecture that motivated Whitehead: becoming, relation, causal inheritance, integration, embodiment, anti-bifurcation, the reality of experienced relations, and the importance of refusing to mistake abstractions for concrete actuality.

I have also kept the panexperiential extension live as a serious hypothesis.

Where I stop is the universal promotion from evidenced experiential cases to every concrete actual occasion everywhere in nature.

Whitehead adopted the experiential working hypothesis. Matt adopts it and offers an abductive case for it. I presently assign it questionmark ?X because the comparative tribunal has not yet produced enough differential warrant to close the question.

At the same time, questionmark ?X is no longer merely a philosophical suspension. It is the open variable inside a research programme that can continue deriving results, comparing boundaries, designing interventions, and registering failures while the universal metaphysical question remains unsettled.

That is what serious engagement with process ontology has produced for me.

It is close to where a stricter application of Whitehead’s own fallibilist working-hypothesis method might leave the inquiry: preserve what the process-relational framework earns, keep the experiential generalization live, compare it against serious rivals on their own terms, and promote it only to the degree that the comparative evidence warrants.

The deductive proofs in my Synthbiosis article add one further step to that Whiteheadian posture: track which successful results are actually invariant across the metaphysical options. Those invariants tell us what the shared process-relational machinery has earned independently of the unresolved extension.

The argument I was trying to have

My diagnostic question for future replies is now slightly different.

Does the response evaluate the proposition I actually hold, in terms that preserve its logical structure, before translating it into the categories of the competing hypothesis?

The terminology question is now subordinate to that test. A wording correction succeeds when it preserves the proposition more faithfully; it does not by itself answer the proposition.

Matt’s latest response gets much closer to that standard. It explicitly distinguishes my suspension from a positive nonexperiential alternative, recognizes what my proofs do and do not establish, concedes that shared operational successes cannot automatically be credited to his proposal, and states a positive comparative gain: explanatory continuity between causal efficacy and experiential becoming.[20]

That leaves us with a cleaner set of questions.

Can D preserve enough stable content across the relevant rivals to perform the inferential work I assign to it?

If not, which invariant fractures, and what conclusion fails?

What warrants treating embodied experiential causation as representative of concrete causal actuality universally?

What bridge carries the specifically experiential dimension across that scope?

When physical, biological, and experiential existence are said to involve the “same activity,” which part of that sameness belongs to the shared causal-relational structure and which part specifically requires X?

Does universal prehension provide substantive explanatory unification beyond categoreal unification?

Which strongest rival fails where X succeeds?

What would lower confidence in X, and what would permit the Whiteheadian categories themselves to lose?

And symmetrically, what evidence would make continued questionmark ?X less warranted than accepting X?

Those are the questions I think Matt’s latest reply lets us isolate.

Not back to whether suspension secretly asserts not-X. Not back to whether D is a completed metaphysics, because it is not intended to be one. Not back to whether one imperfect phrase proves that I misunderstand Whitehead’s constitutive grammar.

But forward to the comparative problem:

What does X specifically earn? What does it cost? What warrants its scope? What could make it lose? And when would its gains become strong enough that refusing to update toward it would itself become unreasonable?

That is a comparison I am prepared to let Whitehead win.
