Joel's conclusion: Aristotle, Thompson, and the untested causal bridge

Joel closes with Aristotle's biological observation, D'Arcy Thompson's mathematical transformations, nested powers of psyche, and the importance of direct engagement with nature. This is an attractive history of inquiry. Formal causes, levels of organization, and non-neural cognition are important philosophical and scientific subjects. If he intends the contrast to reach me, it misrepresents me; if not, he should keep the distinction visible for readers who encounter no other named critic.

Is Platonic ingress false? That may be the least interesting question.

What do Joel's tests license? Not the conclusion that Platonic ingress is false; no array of sorting cells was ever going to carry that weight. They license something narrower and still useful: for the outcomes he tested, the added nonphysical input is idle, because the rules he implemented already do the explaining and it earns no support. That is a finding about those outcomes, and it presumes nothing about a final inventory of reality.

Being wrong is ordinary. A hypothesis can be false and nevertheless be responsibly formed, tested, and revised. The worse failure is immunization: a speculative cause acquires the authority of an established result while every route for correcting it has been closed. Under that practice, every surprise counts as confirmation and every failure is explained as something the auditor was unable to see. A false claim can be found out and dropped; an immunized one cannot, and that is why one need not prove P false to reject the credit assignment.

What would the error cost? In a purely private discussion, perhaps very little. In the public presentation of science, the risks include confusing causal demonstrations with ontological conclusions, distorting research priorities, misleading audiences about what an experiment establishes, and importing unsettled premises into decisions about unfamiliar minds, agency, consciousness, or welfare. These are possible pathways, not claims that every belief in a latent space causes harm. The stakes rise when an unearned conclusion is used to guide interventions on systems that cannot easily refuse or correct their investigators. The person or organism bearing a cost may not be the one allowed to decide whether the premises are revisable.

Here is the specific credibility-to-harm pathway. If readers accept “77.6++%” as verification of Levin's additional nonphysical causal ingress, the headline acquires support that Joel's own 0.0%-earned ingress result does not supply. If those readers also accept that the alleged reductionist critic was refuted by a failed stability explanation, they can dismiss my surviving argument without seeing that I had withdrawn that explanation on October 13, or that its failure cannot establish an independent Platonic cause. Those two unearned transfers of credit can travel into citations, journalism, funding decisions, education and work on biological or artificial systems. Researchers with public platforms and technical credentials can amplify the errors, while those affected by downstream decisions may have no opportunity to inspect their source. The scale and likelihood of such harms need evidence; the mechanism warrants attention before an evidential score becomes a metaphysical endorsement.

Levin has publicly stated the categorical claim “physicalism is false” (the linked transcript is automated), and his published Ingressing Minds advances an explicitly causal nonphysicalist research programme. This goes beyond merely identifying gaps in present physical explanations. I likewise challenge exhaustively defined physicalist ontologies that claim more than evidence warrants; that symmetrical caution does not establish Levin's alternative. The decisive questions are which physicalist proposition he has actually defeated and what positive evidence establishes his proposed replacement. My xenobots analysis treats these as separate burdens and grants the biology its full achievement. Biological novelty does not, by itself, settle the ontology of the universe.

Underdetermination is not a consolation prize for P and not a defeat for D. It means that specified available evidence has not separated every remaining rival. For those observed outcomes, the mechanism and mathematics that already account for them retain their evidential standing. P receives no additional credit without a demonstrated contribution. We can therefore be cautious in consequential decisions without pretending to know P is false. This is more accurate than absolute denial and more useful than permanently protected affirmation. It preserves a path to change our minds while refusing to spend other people's welfare on an unearned conclusion.

The test that could change either conclusion

Specify P, its causal bridge to D, and a result that differs materially between D and D+P. Preregister the comparison with strong naturalistic and nonreductive rivals, controls, held-out data, and real loss conditions. If a P-specific relation improves prediction, compression, intervention, or transfer independently of the mathematical structure already present in D, that should change my assessment. If D wins, it should change theirs. Neither side gets a fixed exemption.

None of this subtracts from the work Joel has done. His computations stand, and they deserve accurate citations and faithful descriptions of the people he argues against. If his current score really does leave nonphysical ingress at zero earned points, we are standing on the same evidential ground, which makes it hard to see what the “reductionist blog critics” framing was for. A label can manufacture a disagreement; only a proposition in my surviving argument that turns out to be wrong can win one, and Joel has not yet pointed to one.
