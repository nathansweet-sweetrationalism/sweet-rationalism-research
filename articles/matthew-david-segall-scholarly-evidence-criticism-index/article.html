Sweet Rationalism · Scholarly criticism and evidence index

Process philosopher Matthew David Segall: evidence, scholarly criticism, corrections, and competing explanations. An annotated public research catalogue covering Whiteheadian prehension, panexperientialism, consciousness, divine valuation, process theodicy, and the limits of scientific explanation.

Bottom line. Whiteheadian process philosophy deserves its strongest formulation, including Segall's serious published arguments against rival physicalist ontologies. But the demonstration that organisms use history, relational organization, or value-sensitive control does not, by itself, demonstrate that every causal interaction is felt prehension, that unactualized possibilities require divine valuation, or that physical mechanisms are inadequate. Those further propositions require their own evidence and inferential bridges. The same rule applies to my preferred alternatives. A serious explanation says what it adds, what alternatives can already explain, and what would make it lose support.

This index sorts literature by its actual relationship to Segall. A named reply to Segall is not automatically peer reviewed. A peer-reviewed critique of panpsychism is not automatically a paper about Segall. A neuroscience experiment about consciousness is not a metaphysical refutation. Treating these distinctions as optional would reproduce the very evidential laundering this archive criticizes.

1. Peer-reviewed criticism directly naming Matthew David Segall

Verification status, November 9, 2026: I have not independently verified a peer-reviewed research article that explicitly names a work by Matthew David Segall and publishes a direct rebuttal of that work. This category is therefore intentionally unpopulated. It is not a claim that no such article exists, nor a license to relabel criticism of Whitehead, process theism, or panpsychism as direct criticism of Segall. Verified examples belong here if found.

A direct scholarly response may be public and substantive without being a peer-reviewed journal article. Those responses appear immediately below. The later peer-reviewed literature evaluates claims Segall advances without pretending its authors were responding to him.

2. Named critics and public arguments that engage Segall directly

These authors explicitly engage Segall's published work or arguments. Their disagreements vary: some question theological or political implications, others press claims about Whiteheadian experience, predication, and the move from a conceptual framework to ontology. None of these entries is falsely presented as a peer-reviewed rebuttal.

Review of Matthew D. Segall's work and the future of integral political praxis.

Pressure point: Whether a process-cosmological orientation yields an adequately discriminating practical account of plural perspectives, political mediation, and democratic agency. A cosmology's ethical attractiveness is not evidence that its ontology is necessary.

Response to Segall's criticism of Kastrup's earlier panpsychism essay.

A stated response to Matthew David Segall.

3. Peer-reviewed literature and testable rivals relevant to Segall's claims

These publications do not directly review Segall unless explicitly stated. They are included because they address questions his formulation inherits: the parsimony and necessity of universal experience, Whiteheadian metaphysics, mechanistic causal explanations, measured consciousness, or the moral adequacy of process theodicy. A serious index also includes defenses and competing experience-first explanations.

3A. Whitehead, panpsychism, and the alleged need for universal experience

Peer-reviewed · Process Studies · 1974

John J. Shepherd, “Panpsychism and Parsimony”

Process Studies 4(1), 3–10. Full text; DOI not independently confirmed.

Challenge: Does the philosophical generalization of experiential properties to nature earn explanatory economy, or add a posit without independent need? Relevant to Segall's identity between causal actuality and felt inheritance, but not authored in reply to him.

Scholarly counterargument · Process Studies · 1976

Charles Hartshorne, “Why Psychicalism? Comments on Keeling's and Shepherd's Criticisms”

Process Studies 6(1), 67–72. Full text linked; no verified DOI.

Counterargument: A defense of process psychicalism against historical critics, included because a fair test must preserve the strongest response. Bibliographic note: volume 6 is dated 1976, notwithstanding a conflicting date on one full-text host.

Peer-reviewed · Journal of the APA · 2016

Leemon B. McHenry and George W. Shields, “Analytical Critiques of Whitehead's Metaphysics”

Journal of the American Philosophical Association 2(3), 483–503. DOI: 10.1017/apa.2016.21.

Relevance: Examines historical analytical critiques while also offering a more sympathetic reconstruction of Whitehead. It is neither a Segall rebuttal nor a straightforward rejection of process metaphysics.

Peer-reviewed · Analysis · 2019

Henry Taylor, “Is Panpsychism Simple?”

Analysis 79(2), 265–275. DOI: 10.1093/analys/any049.

Challenge: Whether a claimed theoretical-simplicity advantage can carry a panpsychist conclusion. The immediate target is an argument associated with Philip Goff, not Segall; the burden of comparing ontological costs nonetheless applies.

Peer-reviewed · Acta Analytica · 2024

Jacek Jarocki, “Three Kinds of Arguments for Panpsychism”

Acta Analytica 39; online 2023, volume 2024. DOI: 10.1007/s12136-023-00566-z.

Challenge: Disentangles families of arguments that otherwise slide between continuity, metaphysical inference, and agnosticism. Compatibility with consciousness evidence must not be misreported as a comparative win.

Peer-reviewed · Philosophical Perspectives · 2017

Hedda Hassel Mørch, “The Evolutionary Argument for Phenomenal Powers”

Philosophical Perspectives 31, 293–316. DOI: 10.1111/phpe.12096.

Serious rival: A positive argument for causal powers of experience. It should be confronted on its own premises, not dismissed because it supports an experiential interpretation. It does not alone establish universal Whiteheadian prehension.

3B. Causal explanation and biological organization without an assumed universal phenomenology

Peer-reviewed · Philosophy of Science · 2000

Peter Machamer, Lindley Darden, and Carl F. Craver, “Thinking About Mechanisms”

Philosophy of Science 67(1), 1–25. DOI: 10.1086/392759.

Alternative: A research programme for specifying productive entities, activities, and organization at tested scales. It supports local causal explanation without making a claim that all causal actuality is either experiential or nonexperiential.

Peer-reviewed · Journal of Theoretical Biology · 2015

Maël Montévil and Matteo Mossio, “Biological Organisation as Closure of Constraints”

Journal of Theoretical Biology 372, 179–191. DOI: 10.1016/j.jtbi.2015.02.029.

Alternative: An explicit account of biological organization and reciprocal constraint dependence. It shows explanatory work that organization can perform; it does not settle the intrinsic phenomenal nature of that organization.

Peer-reviewed · Erkenntnis · 2025

Miles K. Donahue, “Methodological Naturalism, Analyzed”

Erkenntnis 90, 1981–2002. DOI: 10.1007/s10670-024-00790-y. Publisher correction.

Relevance: Clarifies varieties of methodological naturalism and the inferential restrictions at issue when evidence, explanatory practice, and ontology interact. The correction concerns presentation details, not an identified reversal of its argument.

3C. Consciousness: operational evidence and adversarial model comparison

Peer-reviewed · Science Translational Medicine · 2013

Adenauer Casali and colleagues, “A Theoretically Based Index of Consciousness Independent of Sensory Processing and Behavior”

DOI: 10.1126/scitranslmed.3006294.

Evidence: Perturbational complexity can operationally discriminate certain conscious and unconscious states. It is not a direct measurement of universal prehension and should not be described as proof against it.

Peer-reviewed · Nature · 2025

Cogitate Consortium, “Adversarial Testing of Global Neuronal Workspace and Integrated Information Theories of Consciousness”

Nature 642, 133–142. DOI: 10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1.

Methodological example: Theory-specific predictions confronted by shared experiments. The mixed findings are a reminder to update rival accounts rather than award metaphysical certainty to a model the experiment never tested.

3D. Process theology, animal suffering, and ethical consequences

Peer-reviewed · Religious Studies · 2006

Gary Chartier, “Non-human Animals and Process Theodicy”

Religious Studies 42(1), 3–26. DOI: 10.1017/S0034412505008097.

Challenge: Nonhuman suffering poses a distinctive test of whether process theodicy provides a satisfactory moral explanation. Directly relevant to the question of what preserving a prey animal's suffering in God could justify.

6. Claim map: where the arguments can win or lose

These are testable questions, not a ledger of predetermined verdicts. Empirical underdetermination should not be dressed up as disproof of intrinsic experience, and conceptual consistency should not be dressed up as evidence that experience is universal.

Claim type · Ontological identity

All causal actuality is felt inheritance

Reported target: Universal experiential interpretation of causal efficacy.

Rivals: Mechanistic, organizational, structural, or phenomenally agnostic causal accounts.

Loss condition: If the claim is offered as empirically privileged, identify cross-scale bridge principles and an observation or intervention that favors it over specified rivals. Failure to distinguish them suspends comparative support; it does not deductively prove nonexperience.

Claim type · Abductive explanation

Divine valuation renders possibilities effective

Reported target: A further contribution of divine valuation after organization, physiological need, and environmental history.

Rivals: Adaptive and developmental constraint selection; non-theistic process accounts; agnosticism.

Loss condition: Specify the causal or modal difference made by divine valuation and independent grounds for ranking it above rivals. If every outcome is accommodated equally, claimed empirical superiority has no warrant.

Claim type · Empirical bridge

Consciousness evidence licenses metaphysics

Reported target: Move from measured correlates, reports, or experimental paradigms to a universal claim.

Rivals: GNW, recurrent processing, higher-order, integrated information, biological and organizational models.

Loss condition: Name the measured variable, biological or computational boundary, temporal scale, discriminating prediction, and control. A marker of a specific state does not by itself identify a universal ontological substrate.

Claim type · Normative/theological

Divine preservation answers suffering

Reported target: God's retention of a creature's experience as a response to apparently gratuitous harm.

Rivals: Finite process theodicy, non-theistic explanations, ethical rejection of the alleged justification.

Loss condition: Separate descriptive claims about cosmic memory from normative claims about justification, repair, or responsibility. The transition needs an ethical premise, not merely the vocabulary of preservation.

My own framework is subject to the same rules. A simpler rival with equal or better prediction, intervention, and transfer should displace unnecessary machinery. The Recursive Corrigibility Framework is a method to expose those conditions, not an exemption from them.

7. Questions, distinctions, and fair objections

Does this index establish that Whiteheadian process philosophy is false?

No. It documents specific challenges, alternative models, and missing or disputed bridges. An ontology can be coherent while its proposed necessity or unique explanatory advantage remains unsupported. An account's failure to discriminate evidence does not logically establish its negation.

Does “no verified direct peer-reviewed rebuttal” mean nobody has criticized Segall?

No. Section 2 documents named public responses. Section 3 documents published academic arguments directly relevant to claims Segall makes. Their publication status and target must remain distinct.

Why include process-philosophy defenders and experience-first philosophers?

A genuinely critical index cannot define criticism as anything hostile to its target. Hartshorne's reply, sympathetic reconstructions of Whitehead, Mørch's positive argument, and Segall's own publications constrain which objections survive.

Is a claim metaphysical rather than scientific automatically pseudoscience?

No. The problem arises when a metaphysical commitment is marketed as uniquely supported by scientific results that equally fit rivals, or when accommodating every outcome is misrepresented as a severe successful test.

What would change the assessment?

A verified direct journal exchange would be added to section 1. A successful discriminating prediction, an independently substantiated inferential bridge, a decisive counterexample, or a demonstrated misreading would require revisions to the relevant entry and dependent articles. Corrections should be dated and preserved, rather than silently rewritten into retroactive certainty.

Why does the archive live on GitHub if this is the original page?

This Sweet Rationalism article is the canonical public edition. GitHub provides auditable source files and revision history; any article-specific Zenodo DOI should refer to a real archived release, not an anticipated one. Archival copies should point back to this page rather than compete with it as separate canonical publications.

Scope and corrections. The entries are annotated for their specific evidential relevance. Inclusion is not endorsement. Historical and methodological disagreements remain open to revision; readers who locate a missing direct peer-reviewed response or a mistaken citation can bring a DOI, bibliographic record, or primary text for verification. This index does not expose private, draft, or unlisted essays.
