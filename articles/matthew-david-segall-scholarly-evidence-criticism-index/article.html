A research index of arguments, corrections, competing explanations, and the evidence required to settle them. Matthew David Segall's engagement with Whitehead, process philosophy, consciousness, and divine valuation raises serious questions. The question is what the empirical findings establish, what the metaphysical interpretation adds, and whether anything observable could force that interpretation to change.

These essays are unapologetically critical. The most consequential dispute is not whether experience, agency, or organization matter. It is whether claims about particular observations warrant a universal metaphysics of experience, prehension, or God. A rival that explains the same observations without those commitments remains a live competitor until a discriminating test succeeds. If the proposed metaphysics is compatible with every possible result, announcing its compatibility with a successful experiment is not a scientific victory.

Claims about consciousness, causal actuality, and ontological necessity

A useful audit distinguishes an observed phenomenon from a model of that phenomenon and from the additional claim that the model reveals what every instance of causation intrinsically is. Explanatory need is a burden of argument, not a birthright conferred by philosophical tradition.

God, value, and the ethical consequences of explanation

Calling a metaphysical addition speculative is not a substitute for specifying its difference-making role. If divine valuation influences relevance, adaptation, or the world, what distinct predictions follow, what mechanisms are proposed, and what empirical or moral considerations could count against that proposal? Claims about cosmic valuation also face questions about predation, suffering, and responsibility. These are substantive objections, not failures of politeness.

The exchange, misattribution, and the public correction record

Arguments must address their opponents’ actual commitments. These essays preserve the public dispute over a two-way conditional, the difference between suspending judgment on intrinsic experience and denying it, and the adequacy of later corrections. A correction repairs an error only to the extent that readers exposed to that error can actually encounter its repair.

How criticism could lose

The central challenge is deliberately falsifiable. Identify a target phenomenon and its measurement boundary; state what the Whiteheadian or theistic interpretation predicts that an equally well-specified mechanistic or non-experiential rival does not; conduct a discriminating observation or intervention with appropriate negative controls; and identify in advance what outcome would weaken the interpretation. If such a bridge and successful test are produced, these criticisms must be revised. If the evidence underdetermines the rivals, nobody gets to pocket the surplus as confirmation.
