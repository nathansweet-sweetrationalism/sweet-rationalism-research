This is the reductio that breaks Matthew David Segall’s objection to my question. If leaving the universal identity open already counts as subtracting experience, then suspension has been redefined as denial. That does not prove Whiteheadian panexperientialism wrong, nor should it have to: demanding deductive disproof of a whole ontology would set the wrong burden, because competing metaphysical accounts are typically compared by relative evidential support, explanatory gain, discriminatory power, and vulnerability to counterevidence, not by proving every rival impossible.

What the reductio does is collapse the move by which my suspension of the universal identity is redescribed as subtraction. If asking whether A = B already commits the questioner to A without B, then uncertainty has been converted into negation and the disputed identity has been protected from genuine comparison.

The remaining question is therefore not “where did experience go?” I never removed it. The question is: what independently warrants the universal identity in the first place?

Before we follow that chronology, the target has to be fixed. My starting point is already surprisingly close to Whitehead. I assign high current confidence to much of the process-relational core where the evidence warrants that confidence: concrete events are dynamically related, inherit conditions from prior events, make real differences to one another, unfold through temporal becoming, and are not well described as isolated pieces of inert substance. Embodied experience is real, causal efficacy matters, and Whitehead captures features that thinner physicalisms miss.

The unresolved step is the word all. I have not found evidence that presently discriminates whether every concrete causal actuality, at every scale and everywhere in the universe, is literally identical with experiential feeling or felt inheritance. Experience is already inseparable from my picture. The question is how far the evidence lets us extend it.

Openness is not equal weighting. Whitehead can be ahead of some rivals; some rivals can be rejected; other whole ontologies can remain serious. D + ?X is therefore not Whitehead with experience surgically removed. D contains the substantial process-relational overlap and every instance of experience already in evidence; X is the further claim that every concrete causal actuality everywhere just is experiential feeling or felt inheritance; ?X leaves only that universal identity unresolved. In every case where Matt and I already have evidence of experience, D + ?X contains as much experience as his Whiteheadian view does. The disagreement begins only where that identity is promoted beyond the evidenced cases to all causal actuality. If the overlap fractures, my method says to stop factorizing and compare whole theories instead.

This produces the simplest reductio in the article. If withholding the universal identity already counts as subtracting experience from the cases where experience is present, then epistemic suspension has been converted into ontological negation. Before water = H₂O was established, asking whether water was H₂O would already have counted as stripping H₂O out of water; asking whether lightning was electrical discharge would have posited a non-electrical lightning-stuff. That is absurd. Uncertainty about A = B does not entail A without B. Matt can still argue that universal X is the best explanation. What he cannot obtain from the mere existence of ?X is the conclusion that I have already denied or removed X. The reductio targets that subtraction argument, not his positive Whiteheadian case.

The same problem recurs in six forms throughout the exchange: suspension becomes negation; requesting evidence becomes a presupposition against the questioner; universal experience is built into the meaning of “experience”; shared evidence is made to select between theories that equally contain it; failure of factorization is treated as selecting Whitehead; and a comparative hypothesis becomes a condition for intelligible comparison itself.

Nor is that question alien to the scholarly tradition. Whitehead himself framed the universal experiential move conditionally in Adventures of Ideas: “if we hold” that final actualities are occasions of experience, then “on that hypothesis” our direct experience can suggest categories for nature more generally. It is far from demonstrated or self-evident.

Jorge Luis Nobo later called this Whitehead’s “basic working hypothesis.” Shepherd and Hartshorne disputed the warrant for psychicalism; Kimball later challenged the causal-efficacy bridge; McHenry and Shields compared Whitehead with contemporary analytic panexperientialism; and Jarocki’s 2024 audit still treats the major panpsychist argument families as live and contested. The record does not settle the answer in my favor. It shows that asking what earns the universal step belongs inside the debate. I strive to remain open to alternative accounts, though I am not convinced they avoid the problems I find important.

What has frustrated me throughout our exchanges is not that you have attacked me personally. Polemical does not necessarily mean ad hominem. I called your approach polemical because its tone is often prosecutorial, and because your questions are posed as if I have simply evaded them. In fact addressing these questions has been central to my published work for well over a decade.

Your present question is a good example. You now grant what you call a process-relational causal core of concrete occurrence, inheritance, receptivity, integration, response, novelty, etc. You then ask what warrants the further claim that concrete causal actuality is universally experiential rather than leaving its intrinsic character open.

But this formulation still assumes precisely the distinction I have been contesting. It imagines that we first possess a complete, empirically neutral account of causal actuality and then asks why “experience” should be added as a further feature. My claim is that this supposedly neutral causal core is already a free floating abstraction.

This has been a core argument in my published work, including in my article “The Varieties of Physicalist Ontology” (2020), my books Physics of the World-Soul (2021) and Crossing the Threshold (2023), through more recent articles including “Physics Within the Bounds of Feeling Alone” (2023), “Whitehead’s Radical Notion of Prehension” (2025), and most recently my Substack post “Against Physicalism’s Misplaced Concreteness” (May 2026), among many other publications. My basic move borrows and extends Whitehead’s interpretation of our felt embodiment and its place in a robust hermeneutics of nature. Yes, physics gives us extraordinarily successful abstract descriptions of causal structure, but it tells us nothing about the nature of causal occurrences themselves; embodied causal efficacy gives us our only concrete disclosure of causation from within.

In Symbolism, Whitehead develops his account in dialogue with Hume and Kant. Hume looks for necessary causal connection in clear sensory presentation and does not find it. Kant largely accepts that starting point and relocates causality into the synthesizing activity of the understanding. Whitehead instead challenges their shared starting point. Clear sense perception, what he calls “presentational immediacy,” is a derivative mode of perception. Beneath it lies perception in the mode of “causal efficacy,” most intimately disclosed through the “withness of the body,” ie, the eyes’ involvement in seeing (which becomes apparent when dust gets under our eyelids), visceral affect, fatigue, pressure changes (causing our ears to pop), and more generally the rush of the immediate past into the constitution of a novel present.

This is a phenomenological account in the broad sense, but the bodily feelings Whitehead uncovers are not primarily “intentional” in the specifically Husserlian sense. They are prehensional. On this account, a subject does not first exist and then apprehend an object. It becomes a subject through its reception and integration of an already actual world. Intentional directedness is a higher achievement built upon this more primitive causal-affective mode of inheritance.

To be clear, my argument is not: “Some causes are accompanied by experience, therefore all causes must be experiential.” My argument is that the one case in which causal efficacy is disclosed concretely rather than merely as an abstractly reconstructed model reveals causation as receptive inheritance, as the past entering into and making a difference to a presently becoming actuality. Whitehead’s “feeling” is the speculative generalization of this minimal prehensional character.

I cannot accept your sense that we share an empirically neutral “shared causal core.” What exactly are receptivity, inheritance, integration, and response once every concretely felt sense of being affected has been bracketed? What is inheritance apart from the way a past becomes ingredient in a present, and what meaning can be given to “a present moment” without an experiential point of view? What is integration if inherited differences make no difference to an actual occasion doing the integrating?

An answer given entirely in terms of mathematical relations, dispositions, structural transitions, or counterfactual dependencies continues to describe abstract patterns of causation. These are handy for modeling. They leave the ontological question dangling. Physical science doesn’t need to answer the ontological question (on its own structural terms, it can’t!); but as a philosopher, it is precisely what I am seeking to address.

I do not regard the panexperiential generalization as symmetrical with the physicalist one. Physicalism moves from the success of abstract mathematical description to the claim that such abstractions exhaust the meaning of concrete actuality. Panexperientialism instead begins from the concrete case in which causal efficacy is disclosed directly in bodily feeling, notes that such feelings could be the only real sources of the meaning of all our concepts of causal influence, and abductively generalizes feeling/prehension throughout nature, being careful to avoid importing the higher features of consciousness, intentionality, cognition, or self-awareness into primitive actuality.

Repairing the public record without ending the philosophical dispute

Information does not unspread itself. An inaccurate characterization sent to thousands of readers is not repaired merely because a later note, comment, or footer contains a qualification. The relevant question is whether substantially the same audience was given a comparably clear correction.

But the required correction has also changed as this exchange has continued.

The original error was explicit: Matt collapsed my conditional agnosticism about the intrinsic character of concrete causal actuality into a commitment to a nonexperiential alternative. He later withdrew that attribution. Since then, however, substantially the same structure has repeatedly returned in new locations. What I supposedly believed became something my question allegedly presupposed, then something my factorization supposedly required, then something my analytical method supposedly implied, and finally something my suspension was said to “slide” back into.

That matters because the reductio now exposes the deeper problem.

Uncertainty about whether A = B does not entail an ontology of A-without-B. My leaving the universal Whiteheadian identity unresolved does not posit a nonexperiential causal remainder, a neutral causal stuff, or a complete causal ontology to which experience might later be attached.

The analogy is exact at the level that matters. Before water was established as H₂O, asking whether water = H₂O did not require positing some independently constituted “water minus H₂O.” Before lightning was identified with electrical discharge, asking whether lightning = electrical discharge did not commit the questioner to some independently complete non-electrical lightning-substance.

Likewise, asking whether every concrete causal actuality just is experiential feeling or felt inheritance does not commit me to a separately constituted nonexperiential causal actuality.

The alternatives under comparison are whole accounts of actuality.

That distinction also fixes a recurring ambiguity in D + ?X. D is not a hidden metaphysical substrate, carrier, or nonexperiential remainder. It is target-relative comparative bookkeeping: the commitments, phenomena, constraints, and explanatory obligations that multiple rival whole theories presently share. Where the overlap becomes unstable or theory-relative, I do not insist that D survives as an independently intelligible thing. I stop factorizing and compare the whole theories directly.

So failure of factorization does not select Whitehead either. It changes the comparison procedure. Abandoning a shared comparison term is not evidence that one of the whole rival ontologies is thereby true.

The public repair therefore needs two layers.

First, correct the attribution. I do not begin with a complete nonexperiential causal ontology and ask why experience should be added to it. D + ?X already includes every evidenced case in which actuality is experiential, together with the substantial process-relational commitments that survive the relevant comparison. The unresolved question concerns the scope of the constitutive identity claim: whether every concrete causal actuality everywhere is correctly characterized as experiential feeling or felt inheritance.

Second, identify the later objection accurately as Matt’s argument about my comparative method, rather than as an ontology already contained in my position. Matt may argue that even permitting the universal Whiteheadian identity to remain unresolved illegitimately abstracts from what Whitehead understands concrete actuality to be. That is a substantive objection. But if he claims that my method itself entails an independently intelligible nonexperiential actuality, the relevant entailment still has to be derived from premises I actually affirm.

Those are different claims.

There are also two separate ledgers here. One concerns whether Matt’s positive Whiteheadian philosophy survives correction of the attribution. It plainly can. The other concerns whether an identifiable person was publicly assigned a position he did not hold and whether substantially the same audience received the correction. Argument survival is not reputational repair. Different ledger. Different debt.

The repair is therefore straightforward: correct the original article prominently and through substantially the same subscriber channel; distinguish my stated position from Matt’s reconstruction of what he believes my method implies; stop describing ?X as subtraction, predicate-addition, or commitment to independently intelligible nonexperiential causality unless that entailment is actually shown; and keep judgments about my prose, provenance, workflow, posture, or cognitive mode separate unless one of those facts changes the truth, validity, or evidential support of a premise.

Moving a withdrawn attribution from “what Nathan believes” to “what Nathan’s question secretly presupposes” does not itself supply the missing derivation.

A correction adequate to the present record could say:

I previously collapsed Nathan’s conditional agnosticism about the character of concrete causal actuality into a commitment to a nonexperiential alternative. He does not hold that position, and I withdraw that attribution.

Nathan does not posit a complete nonexperiential causal substrate to which experience might subsequently be added. His D is a provisional comparison term for commitments, phenomena, constraints, and explanatory obligations shared among rival whole theories, not an independently complete ontology. He explicitly allows that factorization to fail, in which case the comparison proceeds between whole rival ontologies instead.

His ?X therefore does not mean not-X. It suspends judgment only on the universal identity claim that every concrete causal actuality just is experiential feeling or felt inheritance, while retaining every experiential case he regards as evidenced.

Accordingly, ?X does not treat experience as a detachable predicate added to an independently complete nonexperiential remainder, and it does not assert that any nonexperiential actuality exists. Nathan explicitly leaves open whether the distinction can be given any ontological separation at all. If it cannot, his method requires abandoning the factorization and comparing the whole rival accounts directly.

The suspension therefore concerns only the universal scope of the identity claim under dispute. It is not a positive counter-claim that the identity is false, nor a commitment to an alternative nonexperiential ontology.

In subsequent replies, I have continued to argue that Nathan’s way of leaving that universal identity unresolved abstracts from what Whitehead understands concrete actuality to be. That is my objection to his methodology, not a position Nathan himself affirms. Where I have treated that objection as something already implied by his question, factorization, analytical method, or suspension, the relevant implication should be distinguished from Nathan’s stated position unless and until it is actually derived from premises he accepts.

The correction is therefore straightforward: Nathan does not assert a nonexperiential alternative, does not begin with an independently complete causal remainder and add experience to it, does not treat experience as a detachable predicate, and does not take ?X to mean not-X. He leaves unresolved only whether the Whiteheadian identity of concrete causal actuality with experiential feeling or felt inheritance holds universally.

Nothing in that correction prevents Matt from continuing to argue against my actual position. The correction and the philosophical dispute perform different jobs. The correction establishes what I actually claim; only after that is fixed can we meaningfully ask whether the claim is mistaken. A substantive disagreement cannot substitute for correcting a factual misrepresentation of the position being disputed.

Nor am I asking Matt to concede the philosophical question. I am asking that the question remain genuinely open long enough to be compared. Otherwise the disputed Whiteheadian identity does double work: first as the hypothesis whose universal scope is under examination, and then as a rule for declaring any method that leaves that scope unresolved already malformed. At that point the hypothesis is no longer merely competing for support; it is helping determine in advance which competitors are allowed to count as intelligible. That is precisely why the correction matters before the argument continues.

Where the argument should end

Grant Matt as much as the evidence presently supports: embodied causal efficacy, inheritance, novelty, receptivity, organization, real causal influence, the inadequacy of purely external description, and even that Whitehead may provide one of the strongest systematic interpretations presently available.

Granting those things does not require first constructing a nonexperiential world and then adding experience back into it. They are already part of the phenomena and commitments under comparison.

Nor does D + ?X mean “Whitehead minus experience.”

In every case where experiential actuality is evidenced, D + ?X contains it. The disagreement begins only with the further identity claim that every concrete causal actuality, without exception and at every relevant scale, is of that same experiential kind.

That is a scope question about a constitutive identity claim, not a property-addition question.

The remaining disagreement is therefore:

What warrants assigning universal scope to the Whiteheadian identity of concrete actuality with experiential feeling or felt inheritance?

That wording matters. I am not asking:

“Here is causality. Why should we add experience?”

I am asking:

“Among competing whole accounts of concrete actuality, what makes the Whiteheadian account deserve universal scope rather than remaining one serious interpretation among others?”

Those questions have different ontological grammar.

We are primates on one planet with direct first-person evidence of experiential actuality in at least some systems, growing comparative evidence concerning other minds, and extremely limited means of discriminating among rival whole ontologies at scales far removed from those cases.

The epistemic transition is therefore not:

nonexperience → experience.

It is:

evidenced experiential actuality in some cases → claim that the same constitutive identity characterizes every actuality universally.

Between those are questions of construct validity, scale, invariance, rival discrimination, and theory selection.

Matt writes:

“I have no proof of the prehensions of particles.”

Nor am I asking for proof. Abductive metaphysics rarely provides proof.

I am asking what makes the whole Whiteheadian ontology deserve more confidence than the strongest whole alternatives, especially at the point where its constitutive identity is assigned universal scope.

That requires more than showing that Whitehead’s account is coherent, illuminating, experientially resonant, or internally systematic. It requires some comparative reason for preferring it over its strongest rivals.

The remaining burdens are therefore concrete.

We need to know what remains invariant when the Whiteheadian category is applied across scales; whether terms such as feeling, prehension, and inheritance retain enough experiential content to distinguish the view from more generic causal or relational accounts; which observations, explanations, interventions, or theoretical constraints favor the Whiteheadian whole over its strongest rivals; why Whitehead’s internal categoreal identity should govern theory selection rather than merely describe what follows once that theory is adopted; and what evidence would lower confidence in universal X.

None of those questions presupposes that actuality is nonexperiential.

They presuppose only that the universal scope of the disputed identity remains something a comparative inquiry is allowed to examine.

And here the reductios matter.

If leaving X unresolved already counts as removing X, suspension has become negation.

If asking what warrants X already presupposes not-X, requesting evidence has become evidence against the questioner.

If evidenced experience shared by X and ?X is allowed to distinguish X from ?X merely because X interprets that experience universally, shared evidence has been made to select between theories that both contain it.

If failure of factorization automatically selects Whitehead, changing the comparison procedure has somehow determined the result.

And if any framework that permits X to remain unresolved is already defective because X is required for intelligible causation, then the candidate hypothesis has become a prerequisite for participating in the comparison.

Generalize those rules and every sufficiently comprehensive ontology can protect its own defining commitments in the same way.

That is why the issue is methodological before it is adversarial. A comparison that cannot leave the disputed identity open long enough to test what earns it cannot tell us whether that identity won because of the evidence or because the rules already assumed it.

And “winning” here does not mean defeating Matt.

It means something much simpler and more important: which whole account deserves more confidence after its assumptions, evidence, alternatives, and possible failures are exposed to comparison.

If Matt supplies the missing bridge and Whitehead’s universal identity outperforms its strongest whole rivals, I should move toward his view.

If my D factorization proves unstable, I should stop using it and compare the whole theories directly.

If another whole ontology performs better, both of us should update.

If the available evidence still fails to discriminate among the strongest rivals, then the warranted result is unresolved comparison, not a covert commitment to nonexperience.

That is what ?X means.

It does not mean not-X.

It does not mean “causality first, experience later.”

It does not mean a neutral causal substance awaiting an experiential predicate.

It does not mean a complete physicalist ontology with feeling removed.

It means:

I accept experiential actuality wherever the evidence establishes it, and I leave unresolved whether the Whiteheadian identity of actuality with experiential feeling holds universally.

That is the entire disagreement.

So my position remains simple:

I subtract no evidenced experience, posit no nonexperiential remainder, and add no experiential predicate to a supposedly complete causal carrier. I leave only the universal scope of the constitutive identity unresolved.

Awe does not abolish that comparison. Wonder is where inquiry begins; it is not where comparative burden ends.

I agree with Matt nearly all the way to the universal identity claim. I am still asking what earns its scope.
