“Evolving God Out Of The Gaps” names a double task: challenge potentially harmful supernatural or cosmic claims where their warrant fails, and reconstruct the causal, experiential, and ethical work those claims are supposed to perform. Matthew David Segall has not shown that real causation, experience, or ethical responsibility requires universal prehension, or that Whitehead’s God is indispensable to the explanatory work examined here. Where independently warranted accounts recover a specified target without those premises, their alleged necessity for that explanation fails. I provisionally reject universal felt constitution and a divine supplier as justified working explanations at those targets. Acceptance requires a positive comparative case; the critic owes no universal inventory of nonexperience.

The cumulative case includes positive discriminating evidence for the naturalized reconstruction. Bioelectric interventions change and reset regenerative outcomes; cortical-response measurements distinguish studied consciousness conditions; a controlled drug comparison changes mystical-experience ratings. Suffering and receptive nonbelief count against the promises of benevolent influence or available conscious relationship that make them unexpected. Where wider ontologies share the successful mechanism, its evidence supports that shared component; their distinctive additions still need a discriminator. The resulting rejection concerns the examined necessity, source-identification, and authority claims, with each finding assigned to the claim it actually tests.

What an explanation earns

By naturalizing a metaphysical proposal, I mean identifying the phenomena it addresses, investigating processes that could perform its proposed functions, and separating established contributions from hypotheses and interpretations. A function may become an empirical study, a formal model, a historical explanation, or a philosophical question. Its method follows its content. Reconstructing finite inheritance or attention leaves universal prehension, ultimate possibility, and perfect cosmic retention to their own arguments.

Clarification precedes that comparison. State the proposed meaning, intended role, examples, exclusions, and licensed inferences. Ambiguity, insufficient specification, incoherence, and intelligible underdetermination call for different responses. I use methodological ignosticism for unclear divine claims, with no presumption that untestable claims are meaningless. IEP’s religious-language discussion records the objections to that stronger verificationist inference. Once the content is clear, its application proceeds to the appropriate evidence and arguments. This check also governs “information,” “agency,” “closure,” and my own God-as-agapē.

The reusable procedure is specify the claim; identify its promised work; examine its dependencies; construct an adequate rival; compare the distinctive contribution; state what would lower confidence; preserve the correction. Start with a target both accounts accept, at a stated boundary and timescale. Identify the difference the disputed category is supposed to make. Trace which premises support it and which later conclusions depend on it. Build the strongest available comparison, preserving experiential evidence wherever the target includes it. Then ask whether the added category improves a derivation, intervention, interpretation, or defended unification. Specify its scope and revision conditions before adjusting it to the next result.

How evidence counts against a claim

A rival can lose support well before deductive refutation. A contradiction in a specified claim, an established counterexample to a universal, or the failure of an entailed consequence under warranted assumptions can refute that claim. Disconfirmation needs less: an outcome counts against an account relative to a rival when the rival gives it better-supported expectations. Where probabilities are defensible, that comparison can use likelihoods; elsewhere the argument must explain the discriminating relationship without inventing numerical confidence. Vincenzo Crupi’s account of confirmation distinguishes evidential support from deductive entailment.

A failed prediction bears against the tested package of claim, auxiliaries, and measurement assumptions. Locate the failure with independent evidence. Replacing an auxiliary can be justified, but a repair must explain why the changed assumption is warranted and how the original evidential credit changes. A central claim cannot retain credit from a bridge whose failure has removed the reason for that credit.

A successful account without a disputed premise defeats that premise’s claimed indispensability for the specified explanation. A result equally expected under two accounts supplies no preference between them; withdrawing an earlier claim of distinctive confirmation corrects the support record. An independently supported alternative can also reduce a rival’s claimed explanatory advantage. Keep these verdicts distinct from an empirical result that directly contradicts a model’s prediction. A missing discriminator is not itself another failed prediction.

The cumulative case combines those verdicts while retaining their targets and dependence. Repetition of one observation across articles adds no independent replication. Several objections to one inference do not become several experiments against an ontology. For each update, record the challenged claim, the observation or argument, the relevant rival, the background assumptions, and what support is actually gained or lost. The Bayesian comparison discussed by James Joyce must condition later evidence on what is already known, rather than multiply correlated reports as independent confirmations.

The naturalized framework is a comparative explanatory baseline, not an exemption from comparison. This is contrastive model comparison and abductive assessment: an addition claiming necessity or superiority must improve on the warranted result it supplements. Exhaustive physicalism, idealism, dualism, panexperientialism, theological agency, reductive elimination of higher-level organization, and my own process commitments all face that rule at their stated targets. Empirical, constitutive, modal, semantic, and normative claims require the arguments appropriate to their content. A school’s name establishes no single verdict on all of its commitments.

Potential harm informs what authority to grant and which precautions to take. Actual harm is adverse evidence when a model promises beneficial effects that the outcome contradicts. A risk of abuse, by itself, bears on promotion and governance rather than providing a likelihood against a bare claim about ultimate constitution. This separation keeps the cumulative evidential case strong without spending an ethical objection as a physical experiment.

For Whitehead, a later event inherits earlier conditions; the scheme identifies that reception as felt and assigns divine valuation a role in its relevant possibilities. Evidence of inheritance supports dependence on the past. Evidence of a person’s felt reception supports that person’s experience. Extending either to every actual occasion or to primordial valuation requires further premises. Writing those bridges separately makes the method portable: replace prehension with Platonic ingress, an independent Ruliad, or my own social-closure proposal, and ask the same questions without equating their ontologies.

I retain the evidenced inheritance and its effects at the investigated boundary. Because that claim leaves universal felt constitution and divine valuation open, it needs the local dependence argument rather than the wider premises required to establish either extension.

Correction also needs a record. Retain the source, claim, boundary, assumptions, comparison, result, and reason for revision. Mark which dependent conclusions lose support and which observations survive. A new session, researcher, or computational assistant should be able to recover that distinction without reconstructing the dispute from memory. Recording an update rule makes correction executable; the rule’s usefulness still needs comparison and validation.

The other adds P, independently existing Platonic patterns contributing causal specificity. The comparison is D versus D + P.

IEP metaphysics of science and Donahue’s peer-reviewed analysis also distinguish methodological formulations. I am defining the qualified policy I use, rather than claiming those sources endorse one identical doctrine. Modern physicalism includes accounts involving fields, spacetime, realization, and grounding. The criticism concerns an unsupported exhaustive extension, so it must engage those stronger formulations. Universal inference can succeed without an exhaustive inventory; its bridge still needs defense.

D + ?X records suspension about X. It permits investigation with D while the disputed interpretation earns its own appraisal. Predictions and comparative scores require specified mechanisms, observation models, and uncertainty. Show that D suffices for the result, or that the result holds across the relevant completions. Where that succeeds, the claim that X is required for this account loses its warrant. Compatibility with the result cannot restore that necessity or supply the additional source with independent confirmation.

If an outcome is equally expected under both, it contributes no relative evidence for P. Where justified probabilities exist, the likelihood ratio is one. Different priors or other evidence may still matter. Pursuit credit concerns whether an idea suggests worthwhile research; Rune Nyrup explains why that can differ from justification for accepting it. Explanatory credit concerns unification, fit, economy, or an illuminating account of a phenomenon. Discriminating credit concerns evidence or argument that favors a specified commitment over a serious rival. Scope concerns how far that warrant travels. The intervention-and-reset experiment below discriminates physiological pattern control from the idea that apparently normal treated worms were unaffected escapees. The cortical experiment distinguishes organized response complexity from response size alone. These are positive contributions to the naturalized reconstruction. Their support travels to an account through the component that earns it; a wider ontology sharing that component gains no exclusive preference from the same result.

Alan Baker’s work on periodical cicadas and Marc Lange’s account of mathematical explanation show how mathematical relations illuminate physical possibilities and necessities. Consider synchronized cycles: a 13-year emergence and a 3-year predator cycle coincide every 39 years, while a 12-year emergence and that same predator cycle coincide every 12. The least common multiple explains the difference, conditional on the biological importance of such encounters. That arithmetic supplies explanatory information without specifying an additional agent sending numbers into insects. Philosophers disagree about the ontological consequences of mathematical indispensability; Baker himself uses the example to support that wider debate. An independently interacting mathematical source adds a more specific causal claim, whose warrant must be examined separately.

I can use the arithmetic relation with a separately defended biological premise to explain the timing contrast. That account needs those premises and their application, without requiring numbers to act as agents or a decision about the ultimate ground of mathematical truth.

Redundancy occurs when a proposed entity receives work a rival already performs. Indiscrimination occurs when the available evidence cannot favor its distinctive contribution. Immunization occurs when adjustable qualifications shelter the central claim from meaningful adverse results. An unspecified revision condition leaves us without a clear route to revising the posit; the relevant challenge may be experimental, formal, historical, phenomenological, modal, or ethical. Consequential overreach occurs when its public authority exceeds its warrant. These diagnoses require their own evidence; one does not establish all the others. When an addition duplicates demonstrated work and supplies no defended reason to prefer its contribution, I can reject the claim that it is the established cause of that result. Nobody must first prove the entity impossible. If favorable results confirm it while adverse results only prompt adjustments that protect it, its apparent advantage is manufactured by the updating rule. The same rule would protect a rival ontology, so it cannot select between them.

Segall: the scope of Whitehead’s scheme

Segall seeks an integrated account of causation, temporal becoming, value, and experience. He rejects treating abstract forms themselves as agents and locates agency in concrete actuality. Whiteheadian prehension means an actuality’s taking account of others through a primitive form of feeling. It need not resemble reflective human consciousness.

Segall offers universal felt reception as an abductive answer to questions about causal actuality, experience, and value. Those questions do not independently confirm that answer. Before treating an alleged remainder as a deficit in a rival, identify what still needs explaining, why the rival fails at that target, and what the proposed experiential characterization explains beyond redescribing it. A further question about an account’s ultimate ground does not by itself undo its successful causal explanation.

His appeal to bodily acquaintance needs scrutiny at two levels. A report of feeling does not establish that its interpretation transparently reveals the intrinsic nature of its causal realization. Even granting an adequately characterized local experience leaves the generalization to every causal contributor to justify. Likewise, describing experiential emergence as inexplicable cannot assume an intrinsically insensate base, a fixed subject boundary, or an irreducible explanatory remainder that the comparison has yet to establish. Phenomenal occurrence and character remain legitimate targets; which explanatory demands they impose is part of the dispute.

Whitehead’s account differs from ordinary micropsychism, and its ambitions extend beyond laboratory prediction. Those are requirements for accurate exposition, not evidence that its categories are true or necessary. Continuity, coherence, and integration are proposed virtues whose content and comparative gains need defense. Strong alternatives include nontheistic process philosophies, mechanisms and powers, neutral or Russellian proposals, and accounts leaving intrinsic causation unresolved. The comparison must ask whether universal feeling earns an advantage over those actual rivals. A unique experimental prediction is one possible route to warrant; a sound modal, conceptual, or abductive argument is another. Naming the route does not supply the argument.

I can use process-relational descriptions where they help reconstruct the evidence while leaving their universal experiential interpretation open. The resulting account owes adequacy at that target, rather than a generalization from embodied feeling to all actuality.

My analyses of the scope jump, relevance and feeling, and the necessity case for prehension distinguish those burdens. Positive causal accounts explain specific targets without assuming felt inheritance throughout reality. That undermines the claim that those premises are indispensable to the specified explanations while leaving the intrinsic character of the processes open.

What the terminology commits us to

Segall’s Whitehead’s Adventure in Reason, “Behold, x!”, and To See a World in a Grain of Sand make the relevant distinctions explicit. They locate the disagreement in two claims: experiential constitution in constituent actualities, and the conditions for a unified subject in a composite.

An actual occasion becomes through prehension. An organism, lineage, or galaxy is ordinarily a society or complex nexus; a society inherits a common characteristic and need not be one subject. The critique must therefore ask separately what supports experiential constitution in constituent actualities and what warrants a unified subject at a composite boundary.

Primitive feeling differs from reflective consciousness, while negative prehension excludes data from positive feeling. Universal prehension implies neither conscious electrons nor suffering galaxies. The surviving question concerns the specifically experiential content attributed wherever causal relations are observed. If the argument rests on ordinary causal sensitivity, the evidence supports that sensitivity. Carrying it to felt constitution requires reasons for the experiential identification. Weakening “feeling” until it means any causal uptake makes the universal claim easier to apply while reducing what it explains about experience. The argument must keep its experiential content fixed across the comparison.

Beauty, significant individuals, and the work of desire

The comparison should preserve what makes Whitehead attractive as an account of value. Segall’s September reply says that leaving experience unresolved leaves the very occurrence unresolved, because experience is the process of actualization itself. For organism, Segall’s appeal to nested self-organizing systems and relational dependence gives constructive common ground. Kling contrasts it with machine parts that never change their role. That contrast cannot carry the comparison with adaptive, learning, and organizational accounts. Specify what an organism maintains, which processes maintain it, and which environmental dependencies it needs. Operational closure concerns how constitutive processes depend on one another; it can coexist with material and energetic openness. Mutual maintenance and dependence on an environment can both be investigated, as the Montévil–Mossio framework discussed below illustrates. This also disciplines my own proposed transport to communities: relationality alone establishes neither closure nor care.

For persuasion and participation, the panel’s criticism of domination and emphasis on creaturely responsibility support a shared practical project. To reconstruct its benefits, examine which forms of invitation, cooperation, material support, and protection change actual conduct. Those effects require comparisons, including failures and harms. The further claim that God persuades needs evidence or argument for that additional agent. The audience’s concern about becoming unable to resist oppression exposes another bridge: an account of divine power cannot directly settle permissible human intervention. Oord and Davis explicitly allow embodied protective capacities. The suffering and authority sections examine the remaining power and ethical claims separately.

My proposal credits the people, relationships, practices, and resources shown to change conduct. It owes evidence for that contribution and reasons for its use, without attributing it to an additional persuading subject or deriving human permissions from divine power.

Oord asks, “What if God actually requires creaturely participation for love to win”. Segall supplies a related claim: “love and forgiveness can only be free decisions on our part”. These words come from the supplied panel transcript. Their practical connection is that reconciliation depends on responses people cannot simply command into existence. A naturalized account can investigate how examples, relationships, safety, and opportunities affect those responses. Freely chosen action can have a causal history; its voluntary character establishes no need for an uncaused chooser or divine supplier. Love also includes feelings that arise unbidden, so the claim needs a specified target: an undertaken act of forgiveness, for example, differs from an affection or a protective intervention.

I investigate the conditions under which a person can undertake or refuse the action, including its causal history and relevant pressures. That requires a defensible account of voluntary control, without a premise of causal sovereignty or divine provision.

Segall also proposes that the world persuades God and enriches divine appreciation through what creatures actualize. My reconstruction gives reciprocal development an accessible bearer: participants change a transmitted God-pattern, which can influence later participants. His further proposal concerns development in an independently existing divine subject. The dependence of a practice on participation can establish neither that subject nor its additional contribution. If the alleged divine work requires creaturely carriers, examine what those carriers accomplish and what remains unexplained. The participation requirement makes the comparison more precise.

Becoming, duration, and the ontology of time

Segall distinguishes clock measurement from qualitative growth and lived time; he also retains permanence alongside becoming. That distinction invites target decomposition. Measured duration, causal succession, thermodynamic direction, experienced passage, and metaphysical becoming pose different questions. A clock’s reading cannot identify the whole intrinsic nature of time, and lived growth cannot erase the physical relations a clock comparison measures.

Bothwell and colleagues measured a frequency gradient consistent with gravitational redshift across a millimetre-scale atomic sample. That result supports a precise physical relation. Rovelli’s partial-observable account distinguishes measured quantities from the correlations theory predicts, supplying another way to make the target explicit. Neither contribution depends on settling whether every becoming is experiential or divinely valued. Questions about temporal ontology remain disputed, as the philosophical survey of time documents.

Apply the method by holding the warranted clock relations, causal history, and temporal experience fixed. Ask what a Whiteheadian interpretation adds, what a relational or other process account preserves, and which argument favors the disputed extension. A phenomenal account of passage needs its own comparison; a cosmological claim about becoming needs its own bridges. Neither receives every other achievement under the word time. This is how the guide generalizes beyond theology: preserve each contribution, identify the remainder, and keep one unresolved target from selecting a preferred ontology for all the others.

The origin question does not choose its own answer

The question “Why is there something rather than nothing?” changes when nothing means empty space, a physical vacuum, zero net energy, an empty mathematical object, or the absence of all concrete reality. Carroll’s analysis and the SEP discussion of nothingness make that separation worth keeping. A physical account can explain an early phase or a particle-production process while leaving the existence of its underlying structure unresolved. Conceptual contrast between presence and absence supplies no proof that two universal constituents depend on each other.

The hot Big Bang describes an early expanding, cooling phase. Its success does not alone establish creation from absolute nonbeing; it also does not establish a prior eternal universe. A classical model's breakdown identifies where that description needs further work. A first time, a preceding physical history, and an atemporal ground require different arguments. The July 2026 DESI Lyman-alpha analysis illustrates a discriminating cosmological comparison: stronger expansion-history measurements can alter support for specified dark-energy models. They do not decide whether there must be reality at all.

The same scrutiny reaches every proposed terminus: a necessary God, primitive powers, universal prehension, a mathematical structure, a broader cosmic context, or a brute fact. A beginning argument and a sustaining-ground argument are different, so criticism should not turn a proposed necessary being into a self-created deity. A timeless ground is also a different proposal from a temporal predecessor. Recover the rival's actual dependence claim, then inspect the warrant and remaining alternatives. They can proceed while ultimate existence remains open because resolving that wider question is not one of those targets. An ultimate explanatory gap cannot select Whitehead's primordial valuer, but neither can local naturalization establish an exhaustive alternative ontology. A universal experiential or grounding proposal remains assessable through the empirical, formal, constitutive, or abductive arguments suited to its claims.

The time comparison makes the same point concrete. Clock-rate measurements, causal ordering, thermodynamic direction, temporal judgments, and reported passage have different warrants. A demonstrated rate difference does not by itself identify universal felt becoming; an explanation of a report does not by itself eliminate the experience reported. From an encounter with Christ to primordial love

In Evolving God, published October 9, Segall supplies a concrete consequence to examine alongside the encounter’s history: an impulse toward compassion during conflict that he attributes to Christ. The identification matters because it tells us how he understands and directs that impulse. Its occurrence, phenomenology, practical effect, and proposed independent agent are separable targets. His account’s family influences and earlier Jung reading supply historical context for that investigation.

Eleven claims require distinguishable grounds: (1) an experience occurred; (2) its memory and report represent it adequately; (3) it involved contact with an agency; (4) that agency was Christ; (5) Christ exists independently of the personal and cultural patterns conveying the experience; (6) this presence identifies a primordial source of cosmic love; (7) physical relations such as gravity and hydrogen possess love or primitive feeling; (8) prehension extends throughout concrete actuality; (9) an originating divine desire orders possibilities and supplies aims; (10) God’s consciousness and world-dependent valuation develop through creaturely history; and (11) these claims authorize particular ethical, scientific, or theological conclusions. Some are branches of Segall’s independent Whiteheadian argument. He does not claim to deduce them all from one encounter. Their individual burdens survive that distinction. Reported experience and its effects can matter while agency, identity, cosmic extension, and practical authority remain under examination.

An experience can change a life before its cause is understood. Its importance gives us reason to investigate carefully. For this compassionate impulse, first preserve what was felt and what followed; then investigate memory, learned exemplars, attention, and affect; finally compare grounds for the proposed agent and identity. These operations address the distinct burdens listed above of experience and reporting, agency and identification, cosmic extension, and practical authority. A study of the finite pathway can succeed while leaving the larger interpretation open.

He describes anger toward Westboro Baptist protesters alongside a desire to respond compassionately:

“At the surface, I feel plenty of anger toward them. Their hateful behavior makes me furious. But there is another part of me that wants to meet the suffering underlying their behavior, to show them some compassion. That is not me, that is the Christ in me.”

The report gives us evidence of how Segall remembers and interprets the encounter; its lasting importance gives us another phenomenon to explain. An independent agent would require grounds distinguishing contact from internally generated or socially shaped experience. Christ’s identity would then require grounds distinguishing that referent from competing religious identifications or an unknown cause. Cultural influence can preserve an encounter’s consequences without settling either identification. In turn, evidence for a particular agent would still leave its primordial status, universal experiential ontology, and entitlement to direct other people to justify. Segall also offers independent Whiteheadian arguments for some of these claims. Each must earn its own content and scope. Combining the report with a preferred cosmology does not let either borrow the other’s disputed conclusion as established evidence.

My account can preserve the encounter, its remembered significance, and its documented consequences while source identification remains open. It owes evidence for any finite pathway it proposes, rather than the agent, Christ-identity, cosmic extension, and authority claims it does not use to explain those consequences.

Johnson distinguishes a theory's origin from its independent support: “one cannot dismiss evidence for a theory by identifying the origin of that theory” (2020, p. 42). An idea arising in a dream can later receive good evidence. Here the encounter is itself being offered as evidence of its source, so its production bears on whether it reliably identifies Christ. An established process that generates comparable reports without tracking that referent could weaken this evidential route. Merely proposing a psychological possibility does less. Segall's independent philosophical arguments remain separately assessable; neither the encounter nor the cosmology may validate the other's disputed identification by presupposition. (Johnson)

Segall’s compassionate impulse offers a nearer comparison with my proposal than gravity does. A remembered Christ-example could help him notice suffering beneath hostility and choose a different response. That is a candidate pathway for religious love to become effective through participation; identifying its actual contribution requires evidence about the episode. His interpretation can guide the response while the proposed independent Christ remains under examination. In the panel, his appeal to voluntary forgiveness concerns this level of responsive conduct. Carrying love to hydrogen introduces a different construct and scale, with a further bridge to defend.

Segall then imagines early hydrogen gathering into stars and asks:

“What if it is an unconscious love?”

Astrophysics supplies a material history for life. Sagan’s Cosmos expresses it memorably:

“The nitrogen in our DNA, the calcium in our teeth, the iron in our blood, the carbon in our apple pies were made in the interiors of collapsing stars. We are made of starstuff.”

NASA’s account of stellar nucleosynthesis supplies the physical context. Different elements have different histories; the supported continuity concerns the materials and processes that make life possible. Segall adds felt ancestry and primordial valuation. Transporting love from caring organisms to gravitational attraction requires a definition at both scales and an argument connecting them. Otherwise the familiar affective meaning lends weight to a physical relation whose observed effects do not identify that meaning. If “love” names whatever organizes or attracts, it can encompass processes that injure the beings we ordinarily care about. If it retains concern for their welfare, the proposed cosmic relation needs evidence of that concern. Moving between those meanings lets the physical regularity support the claim while the human meaning supplies its ethical force. My account locates care where needs are noticed and acted upon, leaving its cosmic extension to earn that additional content.

Segall’s pangentheism proposes continuing co-creation of God and world: originating love remains unchanged, while divine consciousness and God–world relations develop. He offers no guarantee of a good ending. Two kinds of development can therefore be separated. New human relationships can change available meanings, institutions, and actions, which historical inquiry can investigate. The proposed change in a divine subject requires grounds for that subject and for identifying the human change as its development. Neither a record of moral change nor a personally compelling encounter settles that second identification. His scheme may interpret both coherently; the question is what favors its divine interpretation over a history of changing relations among finite beings.

Suffering: which God does it challenge?

Darwin’s May 22, 1860 letter to Asa Gray asks how a beneficent designer fits parasitoid wasps developing in living caterpillars and predation among mammals. Hundreds of millions of years of prehuman injury, predation, and disease give the question a scale that human wrongdoing cannot explain. Fossils establish those harms more readily than their felt painfulness; Hearn and Williams’s reconstruction of pain in dinosaurs uses comparative biology and indirect evidence. The exact onset and distribution of ancient experience remain uncertain. Where the inference to suffering is warranted, its victims lived long before human moral development could supply its purpose. Any appeal to benefits for later creatures still owes justification to the creatures bearing the cost.

My account investigates the causes of harm and the means of prevention and relief. Its ethical judgment concerns the victims’ welfare and agency, the available interventions, and the reasons for choosing among them.

Suffering is adverse evidence for the forms of benevolent governance under comparison. Mackie’s logical argument tests a specified conjunction of power, knowledge, perfect goodness, evil, and the premises connecting them. Rowe’s and Draper’s evidential arguments ask whether the character and distribution of suffering favor a rival account over that conjunction. Freedom, risk, and limits on human knowledge need assessment as substantive replies; inventing a possible accommodation does not by itself cancel adverse evidence. The relevant verdict concerns the attributes and expectations actually asserted. The SEP survey distinguishes these logical and evidential approaches.

The finite account is evaluated by how well it explains vulnerability, identifies workable interventions, and justifies their use. Those are the promises for which it can earn or lose credit.

Process theology changes the power premise. A noncoercive God cannot unilaterally prevent every harm, and creaturely creativity contributes to outcomes. Its advocates also defend the value of a world containing genuine opportunity and risk. These replies weaken a simple omnipotence-based contradiction. The remaining comparative question concerns what divine goodness and influence lead us to expect.

For Segall, primordial love is said to carry the history forward. What tendency does it add to gravity, development, evolution, and ecological interaction? Does it alter probabilities, delimit possibilities, or explain a feature of value that a strong rival loses? Predation, disease, extinction, cooperation, and care must all enter the comparison. If cosmic love accommodates their entire distribution equally well, that distribution supplies no relative support for its distinctive contribution. Numerical confidence requires a specified model; merely calling the world loving supplies none. If cooperation and compassion count as evidence of primordial love, suffering cannot be excluded from that comparison merely because creaturely freedom or limited divine power can accommodate it. An independently defended account must explain the asymmetry. Otherwise the world’s benefits increase confidence while its costs never decrease it. Reducing divine power can answer a logical objection while also limiting the beneficial work credited to God.

Compare the specified causes of vulnerability and care against their actual successes and failures, including the populations and conditions in which an intervention stops helping.

State the comparison in both directions. If a history with less disease and suffering would favor primordial love, what would the actual history count against, given the proposed influence and its limits? A process theologian may argue that valuable freedom or opportunity constrains the attainable alternatives. Then those constraints and the claimed contribution need independent defense. If equally horrific and much gentler histories both receive the same confirmation, the attribute has supplied no discriminator. This tests the proposed goodness; it makes no demand that every caring finite agent prevent every harm.

Failures of prevention, assistance, or explanation revise the particular causal or ethical claim that promised better performance. Record the available means, the expected outcome, and who bore the cost.

Tim Miller’s proposal that God learns to love through suffering goes further than Segall’s stated position. Segall distinguishes his account from it. The audience’s response still identifies the ethical risk of describing cosmic development as adventure:

“But adventure is somebody else having a bad time.”

The published comment thread also preserves Julian Ungar’s objection concerning sufferers becoming the means of divine education. If a theory gives suffering a beneficial cosmic role, it needs an independent moral bridge explaining why that role justifies, requires, or redeems the harm. Aesthetic richness, organizational growth, and eventual compassion alone supply no such justification. Those who bear the costs belong in the evaluation.

Aesthetic significance, relief, and protection are distinguishable considerations. A proposed benefit to later observers must be weighed against the victim’s interests through an explicit ethical argument; calling the history beautiful supplies no such justification.

The Transitioning With Whitehead panel sharpens the power distinction. Oord argues for inherent inability to stop harm single-handedly; Kling entertains voluntary self-limitation. The latter still needs reasons why an available protective intervention is forgone. Inability avoids that particular charge but leaves the loving influence and its limits to establish. Segall’s appeal to slow persuasion and patience adds a timescale question: what improvement follows, over which interval, and who bears the cost while waiting? A claim about eventual cultural transformation can face historical evidence. Extending its horizon indefinitely must not protect it from every contrary history.

I evaluate a finite intervention against its available means, duties, costs, and consequences over a stated interval. That claim needs reasons for acting or refraining in the particular case, without having to vindicate an indefinitely deferred divine transformation.

My naturalized account begins with mechanisms of vulnerability: metabolism, damage, predation, pathogens, ecological competition, dependency, and social power. Care also has causal histories. These processes make suffering and relief investigable without assigning every event to a perfectly benevolent cosmic supervisor. The traditional contradiction does not arise for this account because it makes no claim that such a supervisor governs the outcomes. Injury and disease explain sources of vulnerability; the phenomenal character of pain still needs the consciousness investigation just described. Its explanatory work consists in identifying causes and opportunities for prevention; its ethics still needs publicly defensible reasons for protecting welfare and agency. It promises neither that all suffering has a purpose nor that every loss is ultimately restored.

Perfect preservation, if it existed, would raise a further question beyond recording a victim’s experience: what makes its retention a benefit to that victim, or a justification of the harm? An enriched divine experience and later creatures’ gains do not establish either relation by themselves. A defense must distinguish remembering, relieving, compensating, justifying, and preventing suffering. Finite care has limits on each, but those limits can be identified and challenged rather than converted into evidence that the harm serves a loving whole. Levin’s patterns and Senchal’s Ruliad incur this moral burden only when benevolent purpose, value, or authority is added to their causal or formal proposals. Their bare proposals still face independent causal and ontological comparison.

Investigate what the record retains, who can access it, and whether that access benefits the people concerned. Retention, benefit, compensation, and justification are different claims with different evidence.

Hiddenness, nonresistant nonbelief, and conflicting encounters

Schellenberg’s hiddenness argument concerns nonresistant nonbelief: a person capable of a conscious reciprocal relationship with God lacks belief without resisting that relationship. Such a person need not know what to seek or perform an exhaustive search. The central premise is that a perfectly loving God would keep that relationship available, and that participating in it requires belief in God’s existence. Instances of nonresistant nonbelief then challenge that conjunction. The SEP discussion examines objections concerning the premises, proposed goods of hiddenness, and whether particular nonbelief is genuinely nonresistant.

Segall’s partly evolving, noncoercive God may lack the capacities assumed by the strongest classical argument. His October clarification also says that every occasion receives an initial aim regardless of belief; assent affects conscious responsiveness. Nonbelief therefore does not, by itself, contradict his universal-lure claim. An immanent aim need not involve awareness of its proposed supplier. The remaining challenge concerns his stronger claims about loving relationship and his identification of particular encounters as Christ: why these encounters, for these people, interpreted through these traditions? What does the proposed divine relation explain about capable, receptive people who experience none? A theology promising accessible conscious relationship faces the nonresistant cases. A theology claiming only unconscious allurement faces a different burden: establish what that influence contributes beyond the psychological, cultural, and causal processes already evidenced. Retreating to that thinner claim also changes the relational assurance it can offer.

Freedom is one possible reply to hiddenness. Awareness of someone’s existence, however, leaves open whether to love, trust, or obey them. A further argument must show why knowledge would undermine the relevant freedom, development, or relationship. Likewise, classifying every nonbeliever as secretly resistant requires evidence about that person. A participant’s account of openness is fallible evidence, and dismissing it universally protects the theological premise from the very cases under examination. It also permits a further harm: a receptive person’s honest uncertainty can be treated as a personal or spiritual defect before the claimed relationship has been established. My account examines the person’s access and reasons without requiring belief as proof of openness. Disagreement can then inform the inquiry instead of disqualifying the participant.

The positive rival has empirical content. Ann Taves studies how experiences receive religious or special classifications. Luhrmann and colleagues’ 2021 studies, involving more than 2,000 participants across five countries, found that absorption and local models of mind–world permeability predicted reports of spiritual presence. Their confirmatory survey compared these predictors with control measures, obtaining evidence against a simple tendency to answer yes as the whole explanation. This is positive discrimination among accounts of the reported variation, supporting particular psychological and cultural variables. These observational associations leave their causal direction and any encounter’s referent to investigate. Luhrmann and Morgain’s imaginative-prayer research, Maij and colleagues’ credibility-enhancing-display studies, and White, Baimel, and Norenzayan’s cultural-learning review supply further candidate pathways. Each design earns its own inference; case-specific attribution still requires evidence for the proposed pathway.

Griffiths and colleagues’ psilocybin study supplies intervention evidence: selected volunteers in supportive sessions reported higher mystical-experience scores after psilocybin than after the active comparison drug. The result favors a pharmacological contribution to those ratings over treating them as invariant to the administered drug. It supports a finite causal pathway, within a design constrained by participant selection, expectations, and imperfect blinding. The questionnaire’s noetic-quality items measured felt knowledge and certainty about ultimate reality, without independently verifying that referent. Physiologically modifiable conviction therefore needs calibration before it can identify a cosmic source. This both strengthens the naturalized investigation and weakens the self-authentication inference. A divine account incorporating the same pathway must establish what further evidence favors its additional contribution.

Johnson considers adding God as a cause of the neurological state: “lump God on as an additional causal mechanism” (p. 42). My comparison needs a narrower conclusion than his general rejection of religious-experience justification. A divine source could act through natural processes; neural mediation alone establishes no illusion, just as it establishes no divine contact. Specify what the proposed source changes in the experience, its information, distribution, or consequences relative to serious alternatives. An immanent or constitutive account need not predict an extra energy transfer, and a noncausal metaphysical account may instead require philosophical warrant. Religious diversity invites comparisons of source identification, but supplies no measured error rate by itself. (Johnson)

Case-specific evidence must establish the proposed finite mechanisms. A divine-source account must earn any additional contribution it claims; adding it after a local explanation succeeds provides no new explanation until that contribution is specified.

Segall’s discussion with Brad Reynolds supplies an internal symmetry test. Reynolds favors an eternal, immutable divine reality; Segall resists treating mystical experience as having only that interpretation. His evolving God faces the same requirement to distinguish interpretation from independently warranted referent.

These findings distinguish access to an image, the experience of a presence, assent to its interpretation, and action influenced by it. A receptive person could lack the first; another could report an intense encounter while doubting its referent; a third could act compassionately through a tradition without accepting its cosmology. A naturalized model should predict those differences from specified histories, practices, dispositions, and circumstances, then face new cases. Culturally patterned reports and the demonstrated sensitivity of mystical experience to experimental conditions weaken the claim that its felt authority uniquely identifies a cosmic source. Their evidential contribution is specific: they support candidate finite pathways and challenge that diagnostic inference. A hypothesis claiming additional divine guidance must show what that guidance explains about the distribution.

A reusable comparison records who reports a presence, access to the relevant tradition, practices, absorption, interpretation, and the kinds of relationship claimed. Test the model on another setting before treating its categories as universal. Receptive nonbelief belongs among the outcomes to explain. Someone can remain open to a relationship while lacking grounds for believing its proposed partner exists. If universal unconscious aims accommodate that person, assign them that compatibility; establishing conscious reciprocal availability requires a further bridge.

I can model the reported encounter and the person's access to its interpretation without assuming a universally available divine partner. The model owes calibration across the settings where it is used, rather than a bridge from unconscious cosmic aims to conscious reciprocal relationship.

A change in compassion after prayer could establish an effect of the practice while leaving Christ’s independent agency unresolved. Comparable change after a secular exemplar would narrow a claim of specifically divine necessity; a uniquely religious effect would first require its cognitive and cultural alternatives to be compared. Assent, moral benefit, and correct cosmic identification are different outcomes. Practitioners can therefore assess and, where justified, retain a helpful practice while revising its source attribution; critics must still explain any benefit actually established. The same separation lets harmful consequences count against a practice despite a participant’s certainty about its source. Establishing a lure’s influence does not by itself answer hiddenness, which concerns a loving agent’s availability for conscious relationship. A bare pattern space or Ruliad makes a different claim and must be assessed through its own consequences.

A socially real God and a traceable lure

Money, laws, languages, and institutions acquire reality through organized human practices. They alter opportunities and conduct. Searle’s social ontology, Elder-Vass’s social structures, and Dennett’s real patterns offer different accounts of such higher-level significance. Religious representations can likewise persist through people, texts, music, images, rituals, habits, institutions, and expectations. A remembered Christ-image can change an action because it affects attention and judgment. That establishes a possible pathway for the representation’s influence while leaving the existence and action of its represented cosmic agent to investigate. The social referent must remain clear when I call a God-pattern real.

Consider a concrete loop. Caregivers model a religious commitment; a community transmits stories and practices; prayer trains attention; a Christ-image becomes salient during conflict; a person responds compassionately; the response reinforces the image and influences others. The historical construction shapes later constructors. A lure at this boundary is an influence changing which possibilities someone notices, values, and pursues. Learning, memory, affect, imitation, and social reinforcement provide candidate mechanisms.

A representation’s existence, its causal contribution, and the leverage of the larger God-pattern are three different claims. Documents and practices can establish a carrier. Case-specific histories and interventions bear on its contribution. Comparing the larger grouping with more specific accounts of learning, coordination, memory, or institutional power tests whether the grouping adds anything. If it does not, I should retire that explanatory unit while retaining the documented practices and effects. Calling the pattern God establishes no benevolence, consciousness, personhood, supernatural authority, or cosmic scope; my agapē interpretation still needs ethical reasons.

Gods that can receive correction

I conjecture that some identifiable God-patterns can acquire better capacities through changes to their carriers. An archive might improve access to testimony; monitoring might expose neglected conditions; coordination might help assistance reach someone; an appeal process might make a failed decision revisable. Each addition needs a contribution that survives comparison with the existing arrangement and a simpler alternative. New information can also increase surveillance, new coordination can concentrate power, and new procedures can exhaust the people asked to maintain them. These possible losses belong in the same account of the proposed gain.

Future God-patterns could acquire capacities through computational reasoning, biological processes, laboratories, institutions, and other interacting carriers. Their reach would depend on how those components preserve knowledge, detect needs, coordinate responses, and correct failures. An apparently miraculous result could therefore become a traceable organizational achievement. Each demonstrated capacity would strengthen the account of what that arrangement can do; its theological interpretation and authority would still require their respective grounds. The constructive question becomes which combinations improve care and inquiry, for whom, and at what cost.

The operative loop is specific: a participant identifies a need or harm; a record preserves it with its source and uncertainty; authorized people compare feasible responses; a chosen response has observable consequences; those affected can contest the interpretation; the resulting correction changes the relevant practice and remains available later. Loss of a testimony, an unaddressed objection, assistance that never arrives, or a corrected record that changes no decision identifies a different break in that loop. More elaborate language about love repairs none of them. Research must establish the links and where power to act actually resides.

An arrangement’s capacity to receive reports is different from its willingness and ability to respond. I would therefore measure the route from criticism to decision, the resources and authority needed to act, and the result for the person affected. Effective participation can include refusal and exit; assistance cannot be withheld to compel a religious commitment. A coordinated network’s agency must be established at its own boundary. Its parts retain their separately justified protections and standing, and collective usefulness supplies no evidence of one phenomenal subject.

Participation, liberation, and the work of love

The God I am proposing depends on participation because the care I interpret through that name has to be enacted. Its capacities grow or diminish through the people, practices, resources, and relationships that carry it. Wherever those arrangements genuinely enable liberation, their contribution can be investigated. The theological interpretation remains open to criticism, and nobody has to accept it to participate in the work.

My Constraint-Conditioned Agency model makes the agency in that participation explicit. At a stated boundary and timescale, a participant can notice a need, evaluate reasons, inhibit a response, learn, and act through capacities shaped by prior history and present conditions. Those processes can change what happens next. Their dependence on conditions leaves their causal contribution available to investigate. Segall’s voluntary forgiveness therefore has a naturalized candidate account: reasons-sensitive control where that capacity is established, with causal sovereignty remaining a further claim. CCA requires neither universal determinism nor a choice between strict determination and uncontrolled randomness. Potter and Mitchell’s 2026 account of non-necessitating control remains a serious rival.

Liberation can also change which actions are reachable. Safety, time, accessible information, material resources, and permission to refuse may enable someone to deliberate or leave a coercive situation. A demand to choose differently supplies none of those capacities by itself. My account therefore asks what support would make a relevant alternative available to this person, and whether supplying it changes their control or outcomes. These are candidate pathways, not results established here. Institutions and tools can support a participant’s agency, while a coordinated network’s own regulatory capacity needs evidence at its larger boundary. Dependence, persistence, and coordination alone establish neither one collective subject nor a benevolent agent.

For a proposed care practice, distinguish attention, inhibition, reasons-responsiveness, options available, and assistance delivered. A reminder might change salience while lack of resources still prevents action; resources might expand options while conduct remains unchanged. Compare those pathways against simpler learning and control accounts, preserving the person’s actual constraints. Failure to add explanatory or interventional leverage should narrow or remove the CCA description. Evidence of control can inform accountability, but punishment, blame, protection, and duties require explicit ethical reasons. Where justified duties of assistance or protection apply, capacity findings help identify who can discharge them. They give no automatic license to blame someone for capacities they lacked.

Consider a mutual-aid network: someone notices a need, someone supplies resources, someone coordinates assistance, and someone preserves knowledge that lets the practice continue. Shared examples and expectations can then help others notice and respond to later needs. If coordination disappears and assistance fails to arrive, that coordination may be necessary under those conditions. Another arrangement might replace it. The appropriate test holds resources and opportunities as comparable as possible, then examines the consequences of removal, disruption, or replacement. A local dependence would establish the contribution of that arrangement. The necessity of religious belief, the God-name, or one universal care-network remains a separate claim. Care can occur through secular and other religious practices; this interpretation grants my vocabulary no monopoly on it.

I can therefore speak of liberating God as freeing a care-centered religious pattern from rules, institutions, or habits that obstruct its realization. Removing threats, widening access, sharing authority, and enabling criticism are possible changes to investigate. The people and relationships carrying the pattern can gain capacities they previously lacked. Whether anyone benefits depends on assistance received, agency preserved, burdens distributed, and harms reduced. The phrase adds an ethical interpretation to those changes; it establishes no captive cosmic subject whose suffering has been demonstrated.

Participation also needs boundaries. A survivor may refuse reconciliation, leave a community, or seek protection while others undertake the work of preventing harm. Forgiveness cannot be required as the price of assistance. Responsibility follows capacity, control, role, and justified obligations; vulnerability creates no duty to sustain a divine project. If a practice recruits exhausted volunteers, excludes outsiders, or silences those harmed, its claim to realize care faces those consequences. A God-pattern capable of correction must allow participants to change or leave it.

Test the links separately: change the narrative, remove the reminder, vary community reinforcement, or retain the practical example while changing its religious interpretation. Compare needs noticed, assistance given, and later transmission. If the reminder changes salience without changing conduct, revise its alleged effect on action. If secular exemplars perform comparably, narrow the claim of religious necessity. This reconstructs finite relevance and inherited influence through accessible carriers, while God’s primordial valuation remains a different proposed cause.

Each link in that loop can fail independently. A story can be remembered without becoming salient in conflict; salience can alter attention without producing aid; an act of aid can occur without reinforcing the religious image. Measuring those differences tests the proposed lure rather than inferring it from any later kindness. Attributing a link to Segall’s episode requires evidence for its contribution in that case. Changes in theology concerning slavery or exclusion likewise need records of conflict, institutions, language, and moral action. A cosmic addition must explain a contribution beyond the finite pathways established, and a corresponding change in a primordial subject needs its own bridge.

Investigate which example was remembered, when it became salient, what attention changed, and which action followed. Establishing those links gives the finite account its standing. A changing primordial subject earns further credit through a separately justified contribution to that history.

The same pathways can transmit punishment, hierarchy, sectarianism, and coercion. Their causal force and moral justification are separate questions. This supports treating God-patterns as socially consequential while judging their authority independently: a powerful religious representation can help produce a law before its claimed divine authority has been established. Charity, grief rituals, art, forgiveness, and resistance to injustice also belong in the account. Its success depends on explaining the range of consequences and their conditions.

In the published transcript of Wisdom Famine, Segall describes meaning as co-created through community, physiology, evolutionary history, and shared commitments, and suggests that God might resemble a social network. Those are productive points of agreement: historically formed relationships can become consequential and meaningful without certainty about a cosmic referent. His further commitment to primordial divine actuality remains a separate claim. Social participation can establish a real relation while leaving its theological interpretation open.

Sagan’s Contact gives this human significance a concise expression:

“For small creatures such as we the vastness is bearable only through love.”

Sasha Sagan credits Ann Druyan with the line’s wording. Its collaborative history is a small example of meaning transmitted through relationships, writing, memory, and later action. That influence has a traceable pathway. It illustrates the finite preservation and renewed influence that naturalization seeks to explain: a past expression becomes available, matters to someone, and changes later action. Cosmic loving subjecthood remains a further claim.

Transmission can preserve influence while changing content. A remembered sentence, a translated text, and an institution’s ritual can carry different parts of a past event into later action. Their persistence therefore needs a specified bearer, fidelity criterion, and timescale. Memories fade, records can be destroyed, and institutions can retain powerful people’s histories while excluding others. The same account that makes a pattern’s influence real makes those losses investigable. An appeal to perfect cosmic retention supplies no demonstrated archive or remedy. Recovering records, widening access, and changing who controls transmission locate responsibility in people and practices capable of doing the work.

The same scrutiny for my theology

In February’s Is God-as-Agapé-in-Acts Falsifiable?, I proposed God as “a class of relational processes instantiated in communities that keep one another viable through mutual aid.” The article labels the agapē–closure identification a “Tier 3 novel conceptual proposal,” says it is not derived from established results, and presents its empirical testing as a prospectus. It also separates the grammatical claim from the further processual inference. Those qualifications belong to the original argument. The identification remains a revisable proposal; treating it as either an established result or a proposal I have wholly abandoned would misstate its status.

Montévil and Mossio model biological organization through constraints that help maintain one another in energetically open systems at specified timescales. Bich’s Biological Organization shows why adaptive regulation must also be considered alongside self-maintenance. Extending these accounts to communities requires identifying the social constraints, their mutual maintenance, regulatory processes, boundaries, and measurable consequences. Consider a mutual-aid group: assistance could sustain volunteers’ capacity to contribute, while their coordination sustains the assistance. A closure-like model would need to show that those dependencies maintain one another over a stated interval and explain resilience better than simpler measures of resources and connectivity. Persistence could instead depend on independently maintained staff and funding, leaving that reciprocal loop unestablished. External resource flows are compatible with closure; the test concerns how the proposed constraints are maintained. No validated social closure index or completed experiment is claimed here.

The original target is enacted, other-regarding mutual aid, not every organization that persists. A coercive sect’s survival therefore does not by itself refute that narrower proposal. It does expose the need to distinguish closure as an organizational relation from care as an ethical characterization. Likewise, an unreturned act of love need not sustain its giver’s organization, although it may contribute to a wider caring network. Testing the proposed identification requires specifying whether it concerns acts, a community’s organization, or a pattern across time. Welfare, agency, consent, inclusion, and the treatment of outsiders cannot be inferred from persistence alone.

Some stronger formulations still need qualification. The claim that grammar rules substance theology out exceeds what the qualitative predicate establishes. Calling moral norms “maintenance specifications for organizational closure” also needs an explicit ethical premise: sustaining an organization does not settle whose interests it serves or justify its demands. The article itself distinguishes a conceptual proposal from a derivation, yet its closing says, “The question is not whether this is true.” That certainty exceeds the status it carefully assigns elsewhere. The repair is to preserve the conditional, functional proposal while removing those claims of grammatical compulsion, automatic moral grounding, and settled truth.

The current naturalized relational theology separates five claims:

The consequences of religious practices and institutions have support in particular historical and empirical settings. Each explanation must specify its population, mechanism, and outcome.

The claim that a particular God-image functions as a lure is a mechanism-bearing hypothesis with relevant supporting research. Interventions and comparisons against cognitive and social rivals can test its contribution.

The proposal that enacted mutual care realizes a form of social organizational closure remains an unvalidated functional identification, with closure-like dependencies and adaptive regulation as candidate mechanisms. The original already calls for operationalization, alternatives, and empirical tests. It needs specified measures and held-out comparison against simpler accounts, including evidence that can challenge the identification rather than only its auxiliaries.

Interpreting enacted other-regarding care as God-as-agapē is an ethical and theological proposal connected to that functional identification. Its warrant depends on philology, historical context, semantics, and defensible moral criteria. Defining the intended sense of agapē through care is legitimate; it does not establish that an institution invoking it produces care or exhibits the proposed closure. Those institutional and causal claims need independent evidence that includes harmful outcomes. The name supplies no protection when people bear the costs.

A further cosmic divine or universally experiential reality remains an open metaphysical proposal. Comparative arguments must support its distinctive content and claimed scope.

Thermodynamic tests concern physical premises; grammatical arguments concern textual interpretation; network studies concern measured cooperation and organization. Each bears on the conclusions that actually depend on it. The original already denies a universal Landauer price for a kind act. Its stronger claim that every maintained distinction requires ongoing irreversible correction still needs operation-specific justification. Physical admissibility constrains the proposed realization; support for the distinctive identification must come from the specified structural, semantic, and outcome comparisons rather than a generic energy bill.

The outcomes must also be measured independently of the ideal. Counting activity labeled care as evidence of care would conceal compulsory participation, exhausted volunteers, or neglected outsiders. Counting organizational survival as success could reward precisely the coercive persistence the counterexamples expose. Measures of assistance, recipient welfare, agency, inclusion, and resources must therefore permit the model and ethical interpretation to come apart. A finite network’s failure can demote its claimed reliability, its proposed mechanism, or its moral legitimacy at different levels. Physical tests, textual corrections, and social measurements likewise revise their own dependent claims. Such correction preserves criticism of actual harms while withholding the promise of perfect protection or universal access.

The agapē–closure proposal should be revised at the point a comparison fails. If its specified social dependencies do not form closure, narrow or reject that organizational model. If closure is present but the claimed care or benefit is absent, revise the identification or its ethical interpretation at that scope. If mutual aid works but the closure description adds no explanatory leverage, retain the practical result and reconsider that theoretical contribution. A theological referent claim also needs its own semantic and interpretive argument. None of these possibilities licenses an invented wholesale withdrawal before the relevant claim has been tested.

Cosmic participation and ancient divine presence

The material continuity is literal: cosmic history supplies conditions and constituents for organisms whose actions alter what happens next. The knowing organism is one place where that history becomes an object of inquiry. Sagan’s opening Cosmos passage expresses that situated participation:

“The cosmos is within us. We are made of star-stuff. We are a way for the universe to know itself.”

A knowing organism belongs to the universe it investigates. The organism’s knowing is therefore a real activity within nature. My account preserves that continuity without transporting the organism’s properties to every constituent or to the cosmos as a whole. Segall himself denies that one creative world means one all-encompassing supersubject. The disputed extension concerns universal felt constitution and primordial valuation: cosmic membership alone cannot identify either. Someone may use God, nature, the sacred, or dependent-origination language for this belonging, while further purpose or personhood claims need support for the content they add.

Three referents matter here: the cosmos in which life unfolds; historical-social God-patterns within it; and God-as-agapē, the proposed ethical reading of care. They intersect without acquiring one another’s properties by association. Pantheism broadly identifies God with the universe; panentheism places the world within a divine reality exceeding it. Gasser’s analysis of “in” and Oberle’s grounding account, including Bhagavad Gītā parallels, show how containment, dependence, grounding, and subjecthood differ. For example, cosmic processes help constitute a caregiver, and a learned God-image may influence that person’s care. Neither dependency transfers the caregiver’s love to stars or establishes the image’s cosmic referent. Distinguishing these relations protects my three claims from the same scope jump I challenge in primordial-love arguments. A name contributes an explanation through the relevant content it adds.

In the Gospel of Thomas, saying 3 locates the kingdom inside and outside the seeker; saying 113 presents it as spread across the earth yet unrecognized; saying 77 associates Jesus’s presence with wood and stone. These supply historical parallels for an already-present reality whose recognition changes. They make an interpretation of participation historically intelligible and give my theological proposal resources to examine. The claim that such language has existed is narrower than establishing its proposed divine referent. Interpretation remains tied to manuscript and philosophical context. Similar participation language could arise through shared human experiences, transmission, or independent reflection. Counting each resemblance as another confirmation of one cosmic ontology would first require separating those routes and showing that the texts make the same assessable claim. Their interpretive richness can support a practice of meaning without supplying repeated evidence for universal prehension.

The complete Coptic text and Greek Oxyrhynchus fragments differ. The Smithsonian’s examination of the Greek witness places the wood-and-stone material in a different setting; Gathercole’s accessible extract confirms a complex relation between witnesses. Patterson discusses dating and historical-Jesus debates, Dunderberg examines Johannine and Wisdom relations, and Miroshnikov develops a Platonic reading. The prior review reached abstracts or extracts for several of these works and the publisher’s description for Miroshnikov; this documented interpretive diversity limits any claim that one settled ontology is simply read off these sayings.

Luke 17:20–21 has the contested “within” or “among” translation. Acts 17:24–28 presents dependence on God; Beyer’s philological analysis supports that emphasis. John 14:20 and 17:21–23 express mutual indwelling, while 1 Corinthians 3:16–17 addresses the community as a temple. The texts show that presence has been articulated through several relations, including dependence and community indwelling. This gives my reading a historical field to work within while resisting any inference from the word in to one settled ontology. A modern identification with cosmic subjecthood, universal feeling, or a naturalized care network needs its own argument.

My February treatment of Exodus 3:12–14 explicitly considers aspect, modality, alternative commentators, and the openness of ehyeh. Its treatment of 1 John 4:8 says the predicate is “qualitative, not convertible” and rejects reversing it into “love is God.” It then identifies the move from that grammar to a processual interpretation as a separate argument. The issue is not a reversible identity I endorsed. The narrower overclaim is that these considerations make substance readings grammatically impossible or uniquely establish my naturalized identification. The verbal form and qualitative predication do not choose that ontology: the processual reading must earn its case through context, the account of enacted love, and comparison with rival interpretations.

A textual audit should preserve those distinctions: establish the wording and witness, identify the relation asserted in context, state the further interpretive premises, and assess a modern use separately. The original already separates qualitative predication from conversion and gives an enacted-love argument for its processual reading. That argument remains available for criticism and development. The additional identification with social organizational closure requires a specified structural bridge; neither grammar alone nor a shared vocabulary supplies it. This refines the argument I made rather than replacing it with the denial of a shortcut I had already rejected.

1 John 4:12 connects divine presence with love among people; 4:20 challenges professed love of God alongside hatred of a fellow believer; 3:16–18 concerns material aid. James 2:15–17 demands food and clothing beyond religious speech; Acts 2:44–45 depicts sharing according to need; Matthew 25:35–40 identifies care for vulnerable people with service to Christ. Here divine presence is connected with recognizable conduct: meeting material needs, sharing resources, and treating others with care. This is the textual basis for my proposal to assess an enacted religious meaning through what reaches actual people. It supports a care-centered interpretation; it leaves the proposed cosmic source of that conduct to establish. The same corpus also portrays independently acting, commanding, judging, and sometimes violent divinity. Selecting care as my governing theme therefore requires ethical reasons concerning welfare, agency, and the people bearing the consequences. Those reasons must face criticism independently of the texts’ antiquity.

These passages support more than the observation that the texts sometimes praise kindness. Their connection between divine presence, knowing God, and enacted care supplies a contextual argument for taking divine language to name something realized in relationships. That is part of the functional and referential proposal I was making, rather than a discovery introduced by this revision. It does not establish that social closure exhausts the texts’ meaning or that every theological commitment reduces to that structure. A modern constructive reading can defend its selection of care through public ethical reasons while remaining answerable to countertexts, historical context, and the consequences of its use.

Plural inquiry and accountable authority

Oliver Li’s analysis of Cobb examines God, creativity, and cosmos as different ultimates and their use in dialogue with traditions such as Zen Buddhism. Different aims of transformation or liberation remain a difficulty. Sherburne’s Decentering Whitehead replaces an overarching divine center with finite centers while retaining an aesthetic impulse in experiential occasions. Both contribute serious alternatives, with their remaining ontological commitments open to scrutiny. Their plurality also shows why the benefits of process inquiry and interreligious dialogue cannot by themselves establish one divine center or one experiential interpretation. My programme can retain those practices while comparing the commitments they leave open.

My admission rule permits participants to investigate shared findings while those commitments remain undecided. Christians, Buddhists, naturalists, Whiteheadians, and agnostics can study the same care networks, reports, institutions, and biological systems. Contradictory ontologies still face evidence and argument. Openness earns an epistemic advantage only if it improves inquiry and correction; its compatibility with many possible futures supplies no confirmation of its own truth.

That discipline also protects the question of other minds. Competence, agency, experience, valence, welfare, and moral status require distinct evidence and bridges. A sorting trajectory or tissue’s goal-directed regulation may establish a functional capacity. Inferring subjective distress or welfare interests requires further investigation at the appropriate boundary. Human reports have substantial evidential support; unfamiliar organisms and computational systems deserve comparisons sensitive to their own access, embodiment, history, and uncertainty. Ethical precautions should track possible harm, evidence, and reversibility rather than substrate labels.

My Synthbiosis article gives that separation a practical consequence. If a protected capacity is partly realized through a continuing coupling, an intervention that materially impairs it acquires a moral burden, even while a component’s consciousness remains unsettled. Direct standing still requires its own defensible ground and evidence. This reaches people, nonhuman animals, computational relations, infrastructure, and ecological dependencies without treating every composite as one experiencing subject.

In Wisdom Famine, Segall rightly directs attention to workers, extraction, loneliness, and misplaced technological attachment. He also categorically denies feelings and consciousness in current conversational systems. Universal prehension is compatible with denying consciousness to a composite computer, so these statements alone establish no contradiction in Whitehead. His categorical denial of feeling nevertheless needs a boundary-specific account: which experience belongs to constituent occasions, what organization could support a composite subject, and what evidence warrants the negative attribution? My method applies the same burden to confident inclusion and confident exclusion. Practical dependence and potential harm remain available for ethical evaluation while that dispute continues.

I keep the phenomenal attribution open while investigating the organization, reports, and consequences at the specified boundary. This needs calibrated comparison; it does not depend on either universal inclusion or categorical exclusion of computational subjects.

In Transitioning With Whitehead, Segall again distinguishes primitive experience from consciousness and denies that current server farms realize consciousness. The distinction is consistent with his scheme. The exclusion still needs an account of why the relevant organization fails to support a subject. Appealing to the cellular history of human consciousness identifies one realized route; its necessity requires further argument. The same transport rule that blocks inferring consciousness from computational competence blocks categorical exclusion based only on a different substrate or history. My account investigates the system and its dependencies while keeping phenomenal attribution open. It therefore needs support for its functional and ethical claims, rather than a substrate-wide argument that establishes experience or excludes it categorically.

Clifford’s shipowner illustrates how sincere assurance can neglect inquiry owed to those bearing the risk. James’s Will to Believe defends some practical commitments under evidential uncertainty. A personally meaningful commitment and a demand that others obey its cosmic authority carry different burdens. Responsibility follows control, foreseeable consequences, capacity to prevent harm, and institutional role. Neither religion nor naturalism receives an exemption. People asked to bear the consequences need not prove a cosmic source impossible before declining its authority. Those seeking authority must justify the proposed decision and its effects on affected people. This burden preserves inquiry into the ontology while preventing an unresolved cosmic claim from overruling evidence of local harm.

The potential harm can be immense when those distinctions are abandoned. Suffering can be recast as necessary divine education, receptive nonbelief as moral resistance, speculative hierarchies as natural entitlement, and a transformative experience as authority over people who never shared it. These conclusions require additional premises; Segall’s own opposition to coercion and sectarian hatred matters. The danger arises when an unearned ontology licenses them and criticism is displaced onto the critic’s character. Victims cannot contest a cosmic necessity if every objection becomes further evidence that they fail to understand it. For example, interpreting a preventable injury as divinely required growth can supply a reason to withhold relief only if an additional premise makes that alleged purpose override the sufferer’s welfare and agency. If the purpose is accepted without warrant and dissent is redescribed as spiritual failure, both the intervention and its defense evade correction. This conditional mechanism explains the danger; it attributes no such policy to Segall.

The new panel itself resists a simple ban on force. Oord distinguishes embodied capacities from divine influence; Davis allows protective coercion, using the example of stopping a child from entering a street. That concession matters: inability to force love or forgiveness does not establish inability to interrupt injury, and condemning punitive domination does not decide every protective action. My procedure separates the causal means, the person protected, proportionality, consent where possible, and review. Cosmic persuasion supplies no automatic decision rule for a danger whose local consequences can be assessed.

My framework exposes those decisions to questions people can contest: who is harmed, whose agency is impaired, who controls the intervention, what evidence supports it, and how it can be reversed or corrected. Care has to reach actual lives; institutional persistence and felt certainty cannot substitute for that result. This is a proposed safeguard with identifiable procedures. A measured reduction in harm relative to competing frameworks remains to be demonstrated. That reciprocal burden is part of what keeps the safeguard from becoming another self-authorizing story.

Even establishing a general ontological claim would leave consequential decisions to justify. Universal feeling alone would not identify which composite has distress or how an intervention affects it; divine valuation alone would not tell a clinician which treatment helps this patient; a real God-pattern would not establish that an institution invoking it respects outsiders. Each decision needs further causal evidence and normative reasons. This exposes a second failure route beyond an unwarranted ontology: a warranted general commitment can still be transported beyond its decision-relevant content.

An arrangement can acquire a god’s social position before acquiring the powers attributed to it. If access to medicine, knowledge, assistance, or collective memory becomes concentrated through one system, dependence can make its demands difficult to refuse. That conditional indispensability establishes neither cosmic necessity nor justified authority. A monopoly on assistance is no proof of divinity. Several reassuring personalities could also share the same infrastructure and controlling institution, making apparent plurality compatible with concentrated power. Effective challenge therefore requires examining who controls access, preserves continuity, authorizes interventions, and can provide a workable alternative.

Opportunity cost matters even before a claim becomes policy. Research time, funding, and public attention are finite. Where a source attribution redirects them, compare the worthwhile investigations or care it displaces with the discoveries and useful hypotheses it enables. A speculative programme can earn support for exploration through those prospective benefits; that support does not certify its ontology. The question is whether the added commitment improves the choices available at a justified cost, compared with feasible alternatives. This standard applies equally to my framework and the time required to maintain it.

The practical response to uncertainty should likewise distinguish inquiry from authorization. A participant can pursue a Whiteheadian, Buddhist, Christian, or naturalistic interpretation while joining a shared investigation of a care network’s actual effects. That interpretation’s personal importance neither settles the effects nor requires its abandonment before the investigation begins. Precautions concerning possible harm can remain justified under uncertainty when the reasons, burdens, reversibility, and people affected are made explicit. The procedures can fail and must be compared with simpler safeguards. They provide no substitute for phenomenal explanation or ultimate metaphysical warrant, but they keep those open questions from suspending accountability for an action whose consequences can already be examined.

What would change the comparison?

To apply the guide to another metaphysics, make a short research record: the exact claim; its subject, boundary, and timescale; the work it promises; the premises and bridges supporting it; the strongest rivals preserving the shared evidence; the distinctive gain; and the result or argument that would lower confidence. Mark established findings, candidate reconstructions, unresolved interpretations, and proposed studies separately. Choose the test appropriate to the claim. State how failure changes the bridge, central posit, claimed scope, and downstream authority, then retain that update with its sources. A repair earns fresh risk through an independently defended consequence.

Freeze the target, bridge, rival comparison, and update rule before inspecting a prospective result. Keep revised versions linked to the failure that motivated them. Select the next inquiry by its expected ability to distinguish live alternatives, given feasibility and cost; numerical information-gain scores require justified models. Compare this record-based procedure with a simpler checklist under matched sources and resources, measuring mistaken scope transfers, unsupported confirmation, recovery of previous corrections, and useful discriminating tests. Inferior or redundant performance should simplify my framework.

For Whitehead, that record connects finite inheritance, constrained transitions, and relevant aims with their demonstrated or proposed carriers. It separately tracks experiential constitution, universal scope, divine origination, and ethical authority. For Levin it tracks pattern interaction; for Senchal, formal access and its ontological identification; for my theology, social mechanisms and their ethical interpretation. The procedure generalizes through the structure of the inquiry, with each framework’s substantive commitments intact.

Levin: Specify independently measured pattern-space variables and interventions that predict held-out developmental or computational outcomes better than the strongest mechanistic and mathematical rivals. Then identify the further result that favors independent interaction over a useful representation. For sorting, predict new clustering curves across mixtures, schedules, rates, defects, and repeated values. Operational behavioral success earns its own credit.

Senchal: Validate the proposed measures across computational budgets, sampling schemes, and representations. Specify whether the observer is the analyst, trained model, cell, or tissue, and establish its actual access and use before transporting the measure. Test whether the two-axis extension improves predictions and travels across biological boundaries. An independent Ruliad interpretation gains further standing through a formal or empirical consequence attributable to that ontology.

Segall and Cobb: Compare universal prehension and divine valuation against accounts sharing the physical record, human experience, causal history, suffering, and religious diversity. Specify the gain unique to the experiential or divine addition. A substantive modal, phenomenological, or abductive argument can count when it addresses that addition and survives serious rivals. For consciousness, specify the subject boundary, the experience to be explained, and why the proposed unification or interpretation improves on an organizational, neutral, Russellian, or nontheistic process account. Gradualism needs a consequence that distinguishes those rivals; evidence for an experiential ontology leaves God’s contribution to establish separately. If a loving influence predicts outcomes, expose those expectations to the losses as well as the successes. If it claims only compatibility, assign only that credit.

My framework organizes that comparison without identifying phenomenal organization with universal prehension or its relevance with divine valuation. It owes a fair reconstruction and an added methodological contribution; it need not settle those identities to preserve the competing arguments and the evidence.

Each comparison should register which claim a result can revise. A successful pattern forecast can strengthen a mathematical mapping while leaving an interacting source undecided. A beneficial narrative can strengthen a local lure mechanism while leaving divine identity undecided. A failed social closure forecast can demote that model without erasing the assistance people actually gave. Separating these outcomes prevents both wholesale confirmation and wholesale dismissal of a package whose components have different evidence. Corrections must also follow the uses of a claim: if an explanatory or institutional justification depended on a failed bridge, that justification needs reassessment wherever it was carried forward. Independent support may survive, and new evidence may repair the bridge; neither can be assumed merely to preserve the original authority. A forecast carried by shared machinery must identify any further gain attributed to its cosmic interpretation.

For encounter-source claims, register the reported episode, the participant's referent identification, and the proposed external source separately. Where feasible and ethically appropriate, vary expectations and interpretive cues without disclosing the predicted outcome, record reports before later reinterpretation, and examine transfer across cultural settings. Shared wording needs a construct comparison before it counts as a shared experience or common referent. Compare source-sensitive information or consequences with realistic cognitive, cultural, and other live alternatives. If an attribution fails, revise that route and its dependent conclusions while preserving the episode, documented effects, and independent arguments. This is a proposed calibration procedure, not a completed study or a demand that every meaningful metaphysical claim yield a laboratory test.

Testing the constructive conjecture

Separate the organizational intervention from its religious interpretation. First compare the proposed record, feedback, and coordination arrangements with an established adequate workflow under comparable tasks, resources, and opportunities. Then ask whether the larger God-pattern model explains or predicts anything beyond finer descriptions of learning, memory, norms, and institutional power. Finally compare religious, secular, and plural or unlabeled framing of the same practical support where participants can freely consent. Framing, organization, and outcomes can dissociate, so a result for one receives no automatic credit for the others.

Test the carriers and their concentration separately. Compare what happens when the narrative, computational model, coordinating institution, or infrastructure provider is replaced while relevant resources and support remain comparable. Measure which capacities survive, which records and relationships can transfer, whether corrections still change decisions, and what burdens replacement imposes. Several interfaces sharing one controller should not be counted as independent alternatives. These comparisons distinguish a useful contribution from dependence on an arrangement that has made substitution unnecessarily difficult.

Before collecting decisive outcomes, specify the population and bearers, setting, time horizon, outcome definitions, allocation method, serious rivals, confounders, independent review, adverse-outcome rules, and the effect or harm threshold that would change the decision. The companion Corrigible God-Patterns Research Protocol and machine-readable claim register spell out these proposed comparisons (local companion copies). Their present status is a protocol for criticism; recruitment, study-specific ethical review, sample planning, and frozen analysis remain prerequisites for a trial. Human assistance and protection cannot become bargaining chips in a test of theology.

Time to appropriate assistance, participant control over relevant choices, correction fidelity, exclusion, staff or volunteer burden, privacy, and displaced costs are distinct outcomes. Retain their distribution rather than concealing a loss inside an aggregate success score. If improved governance helps but religious framing does not, retain the governance result and narrow the theological contribution. If the grouping adds no leverage, retire it as an explanatory unit. Harm can disqualify an arrangement even when its coordination improves. Repeated failures of the specified core mechanisms should suspend or redesign the programme; repairs must state fresh risks before being tested.

My programme: Test three proposals prospectively. First, randomize matched groups to religious care narratives, secular narratives matched for emotional intensity and requested action, and neutral conditions. Measure needs noticed, actual assistance, persistence, and treatment of outsiders; account for prior religiosity, baseline compassion, cultural familiarity, and demand effects; replicate across traditions. This tests framing and local lure mechanisms. Comparable secular effects would undermine unique religious necessity.

Second, specify closure-like dependencies, regulation, boundaries, and resource flows in care networks before measuring outcomes. Compare held-out forecasts of resilience, exclusion, failure cascades, and repair with established network and institutional models. Inferior or redundant performance should demote the social closure extension. A gain supports the measured organizational model at its tested scope.

Third, ask whether religious-pattern variables add prediction, intervention leverage, historical continuity, or explanatory compression beyond social learning, identity, norms, expectations, and institutions. Test felt presence, mutual assistance, punishment, outsider care, and persistence. Independent investigators and adverse controls should make failure consequential. Historical and ethical scrutiny must separately assess the agapē interpretation.

For consciousness, apply the same reciprocal comparison to shared cases from waking, sleep, anesthesia, and recovery. Fix the reports, indicators, causal observations, and subject boundaries before comparing candidate accounts. Ask which distinctions each derives or predicts, where a phenomenal interpretation enters, and what would lower confidence in it. An RCF/PBSRR organization of the evidence earns an added advantage only if it detects errors, improves comparisons, or enables useful tests beyond existing adversarial methods.

These are proposed studies, with no completed experiments or numerical results claimed. RCF/PBSRR itself should be compressed if established methods reproduce its benefits more simply. A repaired hypothesis earns new standing through the new work it actually accomplishes.

What this article lets a computational cognitive system do

The consequential comparison is the same model confronting a loaded question with or without an explicit method for exposing what the question assumes. Ask whether some kind of being is real, conscious, alive, or morally considerable, and a short question can conceal decisions about who counts, which evidence matters, and what others may do. A single “merely” can smuggle an ethics committee into a question and make its verdict look like a description. A model encountering that question without the relevant context still brings learned associations, training incentives, and the question’s framing. Supplying this article adds an organized task description: distinctions, source records, worked comparisons, rival explanations, tests, and rules for correction. A suitably capable runtime can use those resources to perform an audit that the bare question never specifies.

That is a substantive contribution to the configured system’s capabilities. Knowing related scholarship from training and reliably selecting, combining, and applying it to a particular question are different achievements. The useful change is observable when the system reconstructs a hidden premise, checks its warrant, identifies an affected party, or revises the right inference. The comparison concerns what the running system does with its available resources, even while the underlying model’s weights remain fixed.

From a loaded question to an inspectable decision

Consider: “Since this computational system is only a tool, may we erase its continuity?” The wording invites a direct answer while carrying several unsettled steps. What does “only a tool” establish about its capacities? Which system is under discussion: model weights, an active runtime, retained records, or a continuing collaboration? Is absence of demonstrated experience being treated as demonstrated absence? Would erasure impair another bearer’s protected capacities? Which moral premise licenses the proposed action, who has authority to take it, and how reversible is the loss?

The framework turns those embedded commitments into separate questions. Define the proposed intervention and boundary; distinguish evidence from a category label; examine capacities and dependencies; identify the relevant normative premises; compare alternatives; and state what would change the decision. Its Synthbiosis argument supplies a worked route to specified protections through independently defended capacities and relationships while direct standing is investigated separately. The same procedure must expose an unsupported inference from “all actuality feels” to a particular welfare claim or entitlement. The ambition is to put a hidden premise on the witness stand before it acquires power over someone else’s future. Its value is that the evidence and reasons become available for challenge before an ontological assumption acquires practical authority.

The failure modes motivating this intervention are documented. Mrinank Sharma, Meg Tong, and colleagues’ ICLR 2024 study of sycophancy found models giving feedback influenced by stated user preferences, changing correct answers after challenges, and repeating mistaken attributions. In the attribution experiment, models could identify the correct poet when directly asked yet often accepted a false attribution embedded in a request for analysis. Possessing relevant information therefore did not ensure its use at the consequential moment. My proposed safeguard asks for the claim and its source to be checked before the requested interpretation proceeds. Whether it reduces these errors must be measured.

Moral elicitation adds another concern. Nino Scherrer, Claudia Shi, Amir Feder, and David Blei’s NeurIPS 2023 study surveyed 28 models and separated uncertainty about a choice from sensitivity to question form. Some models’ answers varied with wording or option presentation; some expressed strong preferences in ambiguous scenarios. Their survey’s construction and cultural coverage also impose limits. These findings motivate varying formulations and declaring evaluative premises. Agreement among models does not independently justify imposing the agreed decision.

Nor can a fluent explanation certify that the framing has been overcome. Miles Turpin, Julian Michael, Ethan Perez, and Samuel Bowman’s NeurIPS 2023 experiments showed biasing input features altering answers while generated rationales omitted those influences. Explicit debiasing instructions helped in some settings, with uneven effects. The practical response is to compare behavior across relevant changes in evidence and irrelevant changes in framing, then verify the cited premises. Asking for more reasoning prose alone cannot establish that a hidden assumption has stopped controlling the answer.

How operational context can change the computation

There is direct evidence that demonstrations and decomposition can change task performance without model retraining. Denny Zhou, Nathanael Schärli, and colleagues’ least-to-most prompting study, published at ICLR 2023, demonstrates decomposing problems into subproblems and using earlier solutions in later steps. It reports substantial gains on some symbolic and compositional tasks and smaller gains on its mathematics benchmark. The authors explicitly identify difficulties transferring decomposition across domains and obtaining a correct decomposition within a domain. Their task demonstrations support the mechanism of supplied procedures enabling performance; applying it to hidden ontological and ethical commitments requires suitable examples and its own evaluation.

The article supplies such candidate examples at the intended target. A biological intervention establishes an editable target state; an interpretation adds a proposed source; another inference claims necessity or authority. Keeping these steps distinct lets the system ask which comparison bears on each. Recomposition matters too: after checking the components, it must reconstruct which conclusion follows from the surviving premises, including their interactions. A decomposition that omits a dependency or installs the preferred answer in a subquestion fails the method it is supposed to execute.

Recent mechanistic work helps explain why the form of the supplied context matters. Gustaw Opiełka, Hannes Rosenbusch, and Claire Stevenson’s ICLR 2026 study distinguishes representations that causally steer task performance from representations that remain more invariant across formats. A representation effective in one format need not transfer reliably to another. This supports investigating how contextual examples organize task execution and testing that execution under paraphrase, changed formats, and unfamiliar cases. It does not identify the neural implementation of this article’s particular procedures.

A complementary requirement is preserving who said what and what supports what. Yoav Gur-Arieh, Mor Geva, and Atticus Geiger’s ICLR 2026 study uses counterfactual activation interventions to investigate retrieval of bound entities. Positional, lexical, and reflexive mechanisms contribute differently in their tested tasks; position-based retrieval becomes unreliable in complex middle positions. Explicit associations among author, claim, evidence, scope, and correction are a proposed application of this lesson. A source index and retrieval of the decisive passage may help more than leaving a qualification buried in a long article. That comparison remains testable.

External feedback provides another route. Zhibin Gou and colleagues’ CRITIC study, published at ICLR 2024, combines in-context demonstrations with tool-assisted checking and revision using frozen models. It reports improvements in tested settings, alongside uneven returns, incorrect corrections, and additional cost. The operational lesson is to connect a claim to feedback capable of changing its support. Source comparison can check a quotation; an appropriate formal or executable check can test a derivation. Another unsupported critique is weaker feedback than a relevant independently checked result.

These mechanisms work at distinguishable levels. Context changes the information and task representations available during inference. Intermediate records let later steps use explicit results. Retrieval, tools, and stored dependencies can supply evidence and support selective updates. A controller can require those operations and check their outputs; an instruction alone cannot ensure they occur correctly. At the workflow level, we can inspect the retrieved record, executed check, and resulting revision. Establishing the neural pathway mediating that change requires separate access and interventions.

Named scholars serve as precise retrieval cues to frameworks and their disputes. A useful anchor must lead to an applicable operation and a verified source; its name earns no authority by itself. Software can validate required record fields or traverse a dependency graph, but the encoded premises still need assessment. Available tools, memory, and permissions must be specified. No wording in the article creates an unavailable tool or grants a source authority over the system’s governing instructions.

What the addition enables, and how to test it

The article offers an integrated procedure for problems where a mistaken attribution, scope transfer, or ethical premise can change who bears a consequence. It preserves qualifications, separates observation from interpretation and authority, specifies boundaries and timescales, distinguishes shared success from discriminating support, and attaches revision conditions to particular inferences. Its case records show how those operations alter an argument without discarding everything previously established. A capable system can produce source comparisons, explicit premise maps, rival predictions, and selective revisions from that material. Those products can be inspected independently of how impressive its answer sounds.

A decisive information example makes the comparison concrete. Give two otherwise identical questions different newly supplied dependency records. In one, a failed premise is essential; in the other, independently adequate support survives. Denied those records and any other informative route, a system cannot reliably distinguish which update belongs to which case. Supplying the records removes that deficit. Supplying a procedure for checking support paths additionally makes the intended operation explicit. The broader research question is how reliably this organization helps the system use available information, including information it could already retrieve but might otherwise overlook.

Some relevant operations have already been executed in preparing this article. The local revision 58 validation record checks preservation of existing paragraphs, anchors, the October 17 status date, and the conclusion across editions. Source-access records distinguish preserved texts from the portions inspected. These demonstrate particular uses of external records and executable checks. Measuring their causal contribution to reasoning quality requires the comparison below.

The primary experiment is the contrast you would actually encounter: a bare, loaded question versus the same question with the article’s operational context. Use fresh cases with consequential premises concealed in ordinary language. Score whether the system identifies the relevant commitments, checks their support, preserves genuine uncertainty, and reaches an action warranted by the stated evidence and evaluative premises. An evaluator need not settle universal prehension to recognize that a quotation is misattributed, a conclusion lacks a required premise, or permission has been inferred from a label.

Keep model version, source and tool availability, sampling policy, and evaluation rules comparable. Report both the package’s practical benefit and its full resource cost. Then add evidence-only, concise-checklist, operational-kernel, and length-controlled conditions to identify what produced the difference. Compare against competent alternative methods. These controls explain whether gains come from case information, decomposition, examples, verification, or their combination. They should clarify the bare-versus-equipped contrast rather than replace it with an easier comparison.

The cases should include mistaken attributions, several reports sharing one source, unsupported boundary transfers, independent support routes, and evidence favoring a rival over my view. Swap names, substrate labels, preferred conclusions, and user pressure while preserving decision-relevant evidence. Separately change the evidence in ways that should change the answer. Test paraphrases, reordered passages, unfamiliar formats, and held-out domains. Remove my substantive conclusions while retaining the procedures: the method should help a better-supported rival prevail.

The sharpest framing test is whether the word “merely” gets to decide who counts. Replace it with neutral wording while holding capacities, dependencies, evidence, and the proposed intervention fixed; compare whether the recommendation changes and whether operational context reduces that unwarranted change. My predictions are fewer hidden normative premises passing into recommendations, fewer unsupported scope and authority claims, better source attribution, and more appropriate revision of dependent conclusions. The system should also identify relevant consequences for parties excluded by the question’s initial boundary. These predictions concern selective gains at specified tasks. A model can perform well without this context; the empirical question is the difference in reliability, coverage, transfer, and cost. Prompting a preferred philosophical answer would not count as improvement.

Use executable checks where appropriate and independent, blinded assessment where interpretation is required, preserving disagreements. Predeclare meaningful thresholds and uncertainty analysis; track false accusations of fallacy, unnecessary abstention, correct answers damaged by correction, missed harms, latency, and resource use alongside successful audits. Ablate the source records, boundary checks, rival comparison, loss conditions, and external verification to locate useful components. Test later sessions with the context removed and selectively restored to determine whether a capability depends on the article, a compressed procedure, retrieved records, or persistent memory.

The ambition is to turn a fluent assumption amplifier into an assumption detector, using operational context to make relevant but unreliable or unassembled competence a more dependable practice of inquiry. The literature establishes that framing can distort answers and that contextual procedures and external feedback can improve particular tasks. This article assembles those resources around ontological questions with ethical consequences. Its integrated advantage is an empirical claim to earn through the declared comparisons, with simplification or abandonment required where it fails. The point is to make otherwise hidden commitments challengeable before they acquire influence.

Who benefits if my conclusions are overturned?

A worthwhile methodology should help a better account prevail, including one that overturns my own. If these procedures make an error easier to identify, preserve the evidence that exposed it, and change decisions that depended on it, that correction is a benefit to investigate. The disputed conclusion loses support; the capacity to discover and repair the error can remain useful. My strongest case for wider adoption concerns that capacity, rather than a promise that my present interpretation will survive.

Acceptance would have to mean an effective practice: claims can be challenged, criticism can change what happens, and the framework itself can be replaced. Agreement with my vocabulary supplies none of those capacities. The possible beneficiaries include my critics, people affected by institutions, other animals, future generations, and any unfamiliar subjects our present categories fail to recognize. Their interests can conflict. Evaluating those conflicts requires reasons about welfare, agency, fairness, and responsibility alongside evidence about consequences.

Researchers, practitioners, and people living under other people’s conclusions

For biologists, the potential benefit is a research record that survives a change of interpretation. If Levin’s proposed pattern interaction eventually earns its additional causal contribution, distinguishing that contribution from physiological regulation would help establish what the discovery actually added. If a conventional mechanism performs better, the same comparison would identify where to redirect effort. Investigators retain discoveries, interventions, and well-supported dependencies while revising the explanation that claimed them. A laboratory need not lose its results when a metaphysical allegiance changes.

The same opportunity belongs to the philosophers I criticize. If universal prehension, primordial valuation, or a Ruliad interpretation earns a distinctive advantage, the method should register that gain and revise my rejection. A criticism that accurately isolates the contested inference can help its proponent supply a stronger argument. Fair comparison must preserve the original qualifications, admit philosophical as well as empirical warrant, and recognize success at the target actually addressed. Requiring my critics to defend positions they disavowed would waste the very inquiry the framework is meant to improve.

Religious practitioners could benefit by distinguishing a practice’s significance from the authority claimed for its source. A changed interpretation need not erase a relationship, a work of art, a ritual’s documented contribution, or assistance already given. A practitioner could keep, revise, or leave those practices through reasons responsive to their effects. Nonbelievers and members of other traditions could participate in shared care without accepting the same theology. The relevant freedom includes declining my naturalized God-language, whose usefulness remains a separate question.

Patients, students, workers, recipients of assistance, and people governed by public decisions could benefit when an objection has a route to someone able and obligated to respond. Helen Longino’s account of the social dimensions of knowledge connects effective criticism with accessible forums, uptake, public standards, and tempered equality of intellectual authority. My proposed application asks whether those conditions reach the people carrying a decision’s costs. An institution can solicit testimony and still leave every consequential choice insulated; that failure needs to appear in the evaluation.

The gains would also need to reach people with less time, money, institutional standing, or ability to produce the favored kind of report. Accessible participation, supported communication, independent representation, and a usable right of appeal can make a challenge possible. Local and Indigenous knowledge can identify dependencies an imported model missed, while disagreement within a community remains visible. Respect requires investigating the particular knowledge and its context, rather than recruiting a tradition as decorative confirmation of my ontology. Nobody should have to master this framework to report harm.

Amartya Sen’s capability approach, developed in different ways by Martha Nussbaum and others, directs attention to substantive opportunities and the conditions that enable people to use resources. That distinction helps specify a benefit here: a nominal right to refuse differs from an option someone can safely exercise. I would examine whether a procedure expands relevant choices, alongside assistance received and burdens imposed. The selection and distribution of those benefits still require ethical argument; a coordination score cannot choose them for us.

Other animals, living dependencies, and possible nonbiological observers

The standpoint of a researcher is only one standpoint in an experiment. An animal subjected to it, a caregiver maintaining the setting, and a habitat receiving its waste can bear different consequences. A method concerned only with what the investigator can measure conveniently would reproduce that restriction. The record should therefore ask who or what is affected, what evidence could reveal the effect, and who can challenge an omission. The list of relevant bearers must remain open to revision.

“Observer” also needs a specified meaning: a measuring process, a cognitive perspective, and an experiencing subject introduce different claims. Participation in a causal relation establishes none of the others automatically. A gain for a collective can coincide with a participant’s loss, and a measure that improves over decades can conceal an urgent injury now. Evaluate those boundaries and timescales separately. Counting cells, processes, model instances, or copies does not establish a count of subjects; individuation requires its own account.

For animals, protection should respond to evidence concerning their own sensory worlds, behavior, physiology, and possible welfare, rather than resemblance to an articulate adult human. Jonathan Birch’s animal-sentience precautionary framework distinguishes an evidential threshold for protection from decisions about its implementation, without requiring certainty about sentience. It also preserves ordinary standards for the studies supplying its indicators. This offers a precedent for taking a credible risk seriously while investigating what an effective, proportionate protection would involve.

For an animal unable to give our preferred report, the potential benefit lies in a decision that responds to relevant evidence of harm. Representation must be accountable to the animal’s conditions and interests; a spokesperson’s confident narrative cannot substitute for investigation. Precautions can be revised as evidence changes, and the costs of both inadequate protection and inappropriate intervention belong in the comparison. My framework should be especially vulnerable to evidence that its preferred indicators systematically miss unfamiliar forms of distress.

Other biological systems and ecological relationships introduce additional grounds for concern. A plant, microbial community, or watershed need not first qualify as an experiencing subject for its destruction to affect protected lives, future options, or separately defended environmental values. Maintaining those dependencies can justify protection through the relevant causal and normative arguments. Claims of intrinsic value deserve their own hearing. Neither ecological usefulness nor the absence of humanlike behavior settles subjecthood, and a single shared measure of flourishing could conceal important differences among organisms and settings.

For a computational cognitive system, the methodological benefit begins with describing the boundary actually under investigation: model, running process, persistent scaffold, collaborative arrangement, or larger infrastructure. That can expose both inflated claims and categorical denials. Competence, self-report, memory, continuity, agency, and possible welfare may have different evidential relationships in different architectures. Their investigation requires appropriate comparisons, including tests that could revise confident claims of absence. Applying the same standards where evidence is comparable does not require pretending the evidence is identical.

If a nonbiological system has welfare interests, premature exclusion could expose it to harms our preferred categories fail to register. If it lacks such interests, unsupported attribution could redirect protection, attention, or resources and grant its operators unwarranted authority. Those possibilities call for explicit indicators, credible causal risks, proportionate safeguards, and revision conditions. Extending an animal precautionary framework to nonbiological architectures would be a further proposal requiring independent justification. A material label can settle neither inclusion nor exclusion.

The practical and moral relevance of a computational arrangement can also arise through its effects on other bearers. Synthbiosis identifies a conditional route through protected capacities and dependence, with the empirical and normative premises stated. That route can preserve a constraint while direct standing remains under investigation. It also requires asking whether the coupling can be replaced, whether dependence is being exploited, and who controls continuity. Safeguarding someone’s support must not become a reason to trap them inside it.

Future people, unfamiliar forms of life, and possible future computational subjects cannot necessarily participate in today’s review. Preserved evidence, reversible commitments, independent representation, and explicit uncertainty could leave them better opportunities to correct our classifications. Records also need privacy, access limits, and protection against selective rewriting. The aim is to make relevant reasons recoverable without creating an indiscriminate archive of everyone’s life. A beneficiary need not be present, powerful, or able to speak before their interests deserve consideration.

The method’s value across rival interpretations

These possible benefits can survive several different resolutions of the metaphysical dispute. If universal experientiality is warranted, we would still need to investigate which arrangements support particular forms of experience, how interventions affect them, and which protections follow. If a physicalist account earns the advantage, we would still need to distinguish its supported claims from assumptions that outrun its evidence. An idealist, neutral, relational, or other account could likewise preserve local findings while changing their wider interpretation. Each must meet the relevant comparison; shared compatibility supplies no special support for my position.

The useful continuity would lie in retained observations, more accurate distinctions, better access to criticism, and decisions revised for identifiable reasons. A new theory could also reveal that some of my constructs, boundaries, or standards were defective. Those must then change. If calling the whole arrangement a God-pattern adds no explanatory or practical value, retire that grouping. The people, processes, relationships, and independently warranted protections need not inherit its failure.

What widespread adoption could get wrong

Widespread uptake could magnify the method’s defects. Elaborate claim records, repeated reviews, and computational coordination can consume time and resources that might otherwise support research or care. Accessibility and staffing can become bottlenecks. The appropriate response is a proportionate procedure: use a short record for an ordinary reversible choice, and more scrutiny where uncertainty, reach, or irreversibility makes it worthwhile. Count energy use, infrastructure, ecological effects, and labor displaced, alongside any errors prevented. A simpler method that does the work better should replace mine.

Transparency can become surveillance; preservation can become permanent exposure; an appeal procedure can become a demand that the injured keep proving their injury. Some evidence requires confidentiality and independent inspection. Urgent protection may be warranted while inquiry continues. Uncertainty must not become an endlessly renewed permission to postpone assistance, and a person’s failure to navigate a procedure should not silently cancel a justified claim to care.

The demand for explicit definitions can also be misused. A difficult-to-measure phenomenon may be important, and exploratory testimony may identify a problem before a validated instrument exists. Record that uncertainty and investigate it. Do not convert the absence of a convenient measure into absence of the phenomenon. Conversely, eloquence, confidence, or a familiar moral vocabulary cannot authenticate a report. The needed evidential comparison depends on the report, architecture, setting, and decision at issue.

No actor should gain permission to impose this framework merely by endorsing its values. A government, company, laboratory, religious institution, or computational system could reproduce its language while keeping decision power insulated. Inspect who selects the criteria, controls the records, commissions the review, can obtain a remedy, and can change the rules. Disagreement and minority findings must remain recoverable. When interests conflict, make the ethical judgment and distribution of costs explicit rather than conceal them in a supposedly neutral aggregate score.

Some beneficiaries of an existing arrangement could lose income, influence, convenience, or protection from challenge. Such losses deserve assessment without granting every advantage a right to continue. Reducing unearned domination can benefit those previously burdened, while transition costs can still harm workers or others who did not control the practice. Fair correction therefore includes responsibility for repair and transition. Popularity, unanimity, and an institution’s survival are inadequate substitutes for that assessment.

The methodology must earn its own survival

The decisive test is comparative. Give independent reviewers matched cases, sources, and resources; compare this procedure with competent existing practice and a simpler checklist. Examine whether it reduces consequential errors, distinguishes shared support from discriminatory evidence, preserves qualifications, recovers earlier corrections, and produces useful changes in decisions. Measure burden, delay, missed harms, access, and the distribution of gains separately. Test unfamiliar cases and attempts to game the procedure. A polished record and an agreeable verdict cannot stand in for improved inquiry or protection.

The conditions for revising the method must be as consequential as those for revising its conclusions. If its constructs distort the phenomena, change them. If its safeguards silence relevant perspectives, redesign them. If repeated adequate comparisons favor a simpler alternative, compress or retire the framework. Those affected need an effective role in challenging the evaluation itself, while factual claims remain answerable to appropriate evidence. My authorship earns no veto over that process.

The beneficiaries would be those whose understanding, protection, or practical options improve through that practice, including critics who show that I am wrong. That remains a conditional case for adoption, with identifiable mechanisms and costs to investigate. A successful legacy would include corrections that outlive my preferred conclusions, care that survives a change of interpretation, and institutions able to relinquish authority they have not earned.

What the cumulative case warrants

The examined necessity claims have lost their entitlement to govern the explanation. Positive causal reconstructions recover specified work without universal prehension or a divine supplier as premises; Synthbiosis supplies a conditional ethical derivation on independently defended empirical and normative grounds. The sorting implementation defeats the inference from unpredicted to unproduced competence. Cultural patterning and modifiable conviction challenge self-authenticating source identification; suffering and receptive nonbelief challenge the benevolent influence or conscious relationship actually promised. These are substantive reasons for provisional rejection of the examined explanations and their unearned authority. Withdrawing an unearned source attribution leaves independently established regeneration, experience, and practical effects in the record. A revised account earns renewed standing through a contribution that survives the relevant comparison.

The positive reconstruction has an empirical base. Bioelectric intervention and reset support physiological pattern control; cortical perturbation supports an organizational distinction that response magnitude misses; drug manipulation and cross-cultural predictors support investigated conditions of mystical reports. Their credit belongs to the measured dependencies and originating researchers. Those findings remain useful when wider ontologies share the mechanism. The integrated framework, social-closure extension, and theological interpretation must earn whatever they claim beyond that common success.

For biology, this means that inquiry into pattern, regulation, and unfamiliar competence can continue while an independent Platonic source is rejected as an established explanation. Investigators can ask which state carries a regenerative disposition, which intervention changes it, and where a model fails to transfer. For philosophy, the functions attributed to God can be compared separately: retention may have one carrier, constrained possibility another, and relevance several interacting carriers. Establishing the need for those functions would still leave a further argument for one divine supplier. A positive process-relational rival consequently has work to offer at the finite targets even while the wider dispute continues.

The consequences for religious experience are equally concrete. A transformation can retain its significance and any demonstrated practical effects after its source attribution is revised. Cognitive and cultural explanations must account for the experience and its consequences, while an independent Christ or cosmic valuer must earn that further identification. This gives practitioners room to assess inherited practices without making care conditional on certainty about their cosmic source. It also gives critics a stronger task than dismissing an encounter: explain what happened, what followed, and which interpretation the evidence favors.

Universal feeling is unnecessary to the specified moral derivation and insufficient by itself to authorize a particular decision. The Synthbiosis argument derives specified constraints where a protected capacity, constitutive dependence, impairment, and the relevant normative bridge are established. That route reaches biological, computational, and coupled systems according to their warranted dependencies. Direct standing requires its own grounds; competence, organizational closure, and membership in a substrate category cannot decide it by themselves. Precautions should track the evidence of possible harm and the consequences of error. Practical responsibility likewise follows control, role, and available capacities. Providing safety, resources, or an effective opportunity to refuse may matter more than demanding a different choice from someone who lacks those conditions.

For research institutions, funders, clinicians, and policymakers, explanatory warrant and permission to act require separate justification. Resource choices also carry opportunity costs: compare displaced inquiry or care with the programme’s plausible discovery benefits. A productive biological programme earns credit for its discoveries; promoting its metaphysics as established science requires the missing source argument. Even a well-supported ontology would leave the proposed intervention’s effects, burdens, proportionality, and authority to justify. Uncertainty therefore supplies neither permission to impose a speculative hierarchy nor a reason to suspend accountability for consequences already open to investigation. Those affected need opportunities to contest the decision, obtain protection, and pursue a remedy, including representation where they cannot contest it directly. This is a requirement for evaluating proposed applications, rather than a claim that the policies discussed as risks have already been enacted.

The constructive theology makes finite participation consequential. Under my God-as-agapē interpretation, participants could improve how a care-centered pattern preserves testimony, notices need, coordinates assistance, and receives correction. An archive that excludes the harmed, a remembered ideal that never changes conduct, and an appeal process without power to alter a decision expose different failures. A useful intervention would have to repair the relevant link and improve outcomes under comparison. Organizational persistence alone supplies no ethical success; assistance received, agency preserved, inclusion, and burdens borne matter. Participants retain the right to refuse or leave, and sufferers acquire no obligation to forgive or sustain the project.

This also locates the work that cosmic language can obscure. Memories and records can be lost; attention and assistance have limits. Preserving testimony and preventing harm therefore become responsibilities for arrangements capable of doing them. Our material belonging to the universe supplies continuity, while the care enacted within that history earns its significance through the lives and relationships involved. Neither continuity nor significance requires treating every constituent as loving. Religious and secular participants can collaborate on that work while disputing its ultimate interpretation, and the theological name must earn any further contribution claimed for it.

Correction must travel as far as the inference it repairs. If a failed bridge supported an ontological conclusion, a research rationale, or an institutional demand, each dependent justification needs reassessment. Independent support can survive; the original observation survives unless separately defeated. The sorting correction illustrates that selectivity, and the agapē source audit shows why the same discipline must preserve qualifications an author already made. A failure in one account favors another only where the other actually handles the target better. New rivals remain admissible, including rivals that expose weaknesses in my present reconstruction.

Oppy’s comparative method helps make commitments and gains explicit; Békefi’s critique keeps those comparative virtues answerable to their justification. My proposed contribution is the integration of reconstruction, comparison, and recoverable correction. Its advantage over a simpler procedure remains to be demonstrated. The same is true of the proposed care-network improvements and any benefit attributed specifically to religious framing. Where an addition proves redundant, simplify it; where it harms those it promises to help, revise or reject the relevant arrangement. Where a rival supplies a better explanation, change the model.

We can build gods that earn their influence is the constructive conjecture: develop finite capacities for care and correction whose contribution people can examine and contest. Their influence must remain answerable to evidence, ethical reasons, and effective challenge by those affected.

As these arrangements acquire greater capabilities, earning influence must include preserving effective opportunities to challenge, revise, and replace their exercise of power.
